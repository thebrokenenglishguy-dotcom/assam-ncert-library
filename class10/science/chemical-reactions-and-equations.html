Chapter 1 — Chemical Reactions and Equations

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. What do you observe when a magnesium ribbon is burnt in air?
Answer: It burns with a dazzling white flame and forms a white powder of magnesium oxide.
2. What observations can show that a chemical reaction has taken place?
Answer: A change in state, change in colour, evolution of a gas, or change in temperature can indicate a chemical reaction.
3. Why is a chemical equation balanced?
Answer: It is balanced to obey the law of conservation of mass. The number of atoms of each element must be equal on both sides.
4. Why does an iron nail become brownish when placed in copper sulphate solution?
Answer: Iron displaces copper from copper sulphate. Copper is deposited on the nail, while iron sulphate forms in solution.
5. What happens when copper powder is heated in air?
Answer: Copper reacts with oxygen and forms black copper(II) oxide: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. In 2PbO + C → 2Pb + CO₂, which statements about oxidation and reduction are incorrect?
Answer: Carbon dioxide is not being oxidised. Lead oxide is reduced and carbon is oxidised. Therefore, statements (b) is incorrect.
2. Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe is what type of reaction?
Answer: It is a displacement reaction. Aluminium displaces iron from iron(III) oxide.
3. What happens when dilute HCl is added to iron filings?
Answer: Hydrogen gas and iron chloride are produced: Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂.
4. What is a balanced chemical equation and why is balancing necessary?
Answer: It has equal numbers of atoms of every element on both sides. Balancing is necessary because mass is conserved.
5. Balance the four word reactions involving ammonia, hydrogen sulphide, barium chloride/aluminium sulphate, and potassium with water.
Answer: (a) N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. (b) 2H₂S + 3O₂ → 2H₂O + 2SO₂. (c) 3BaCl₂ + Al₂(SO₄)₃ → 3BaSO₄ + 2AlCl₃. (d) 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂.
6. Balance: HNO₃ + Ca(OH)₂; NaOH + H₂SO₄; NaCl + AgNO₃; BaCl₂ + H₂SO₄.
Answer: (a) 2HNO₃ + Ca(OH)₂ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O. (b) 2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. (c) NaCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl + NaNO₃. (d) BaCl₂ + H₂SO₄ → BaSO₄ + 2HCl.
7. Write balanced equations for calcium hydroxide with carbon dioxide; zinc with silver nitrate; aluminium with copper chloride; barium chloride with potassium sulphate.
Answer: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O; Zn + 2AgNO₃ → Zn(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag; 2Al + 3CuCl₂ → 2AlCl₃ + 3Cu; BaCl₂ + K₂SO₄ → BaSO₄ + 2KCl.
8. Write balanced equations and identify the reaction type for the four reactions given in the exercise.
Answer: (a) KBr + BaI₂ → KI + BaBr₂: double displacement. (b) ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂: decomposition. (c) H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl: combination. (d) Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂: displacement.
9. What are exothermic and endothermic reactions? Give examples.
Answer: Exothermic reactions release heat, e.g. combustion. Endothermic reactions absorb energy, e.g. thermal decomposition of CaCO₃.
10. Why is respiration exothermic?
Answer: Glucose reacts with oxygen and releases energy: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy.
11. Why are decomposition reactions called the opposite of combination reactions?
Answer: Combination reactions form one product from two or more reactants, while decomposition reactions split one reactant into two or more products.
12. Give one decomposition equation each where energy is supplied as heat, light and electricity.
Answer: Heat: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. Light: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂. Electricity: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂.
13. Differentiate displacement and double displacement reactions with equations.
Answer: Displacement: one element replaces another, e.g. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Double displacement: ions exchange, e.g. Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl.
14. Write the reaction used to recover silver from silver nitrate using copper.
Answer: Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag.
15. What is a precipitation reaction? Give an example.
Answer: A reaction that forms an insoluble solid precipitate is a precipitation reaction. Example: BaCl₂ + Na₂SO₄ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl.
16. Explain oxidation and reduction using gain/loss of oxygen, with examples.
Answer: Oxidation is gain of oxygen, e.g. 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO. Reduction is loss of oxygen, e.g. CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O.
17. A shiny brown element becomes black on heating in air. Identify it and the black compound.
Answer: The element is copper and the black compound is copper(II) oxide, CuO.
18. Why is paint applied to iron articles?
Answer: Paint keeps oxygen and moisture away from the iron surface, reducing corrosion or rusting.
19. Why are foods containing oil and fat flushed with nitrogen?
Answer: Nitrogen prevents contact with oxygen and slows oxidation, thereby preventing rancidity.
20. Explain corrosion and rancidity with one example each.
Answer: Corrosion is gradual damage of metals by environmental reactions, e.g. rusting of iron. Rancidity is oxidation of fats and oils causing unpleasant smell and taste.
