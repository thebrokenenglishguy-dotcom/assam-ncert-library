Control and Coordination

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. What is the function of receptors?
Answer: They detect changes in the environment and convert them into signals that the nervous system can process.
2. How does a nerve impulse cross a synapse?
Answer: Chemicals released at the axon end cross the synaptic gap and trigger an impulse in the next neuron.
3. What is a reflex arc?
Answer: It is the pathway through which a sensory input produces a rapid response, usually through the spinal cord.
4. Why are hormones regulated by feedback mechanisms?
Answer: Feedback keeps hormone levels within suitable limits and prevents excessive or insufficient secretion.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. Which listed substance is a plant hormone: insulin, thyroxin, oestrogen or cytokinin?
Answer: Cytokinin.
2. What is the gap between two neurons called?
Answer: A synapse.
3. What does the brain control?
Answer: Thinking, heartbeat regulation, balance and many other functions; therefore the broad answer is all of these.
4. What is the function of receptors and what if they fail?
Answer: They detect stimuli. If they fail, the organism may not detect important changes and may not respond properly.
5. Describe a neuron and its function.
Answer: A neuron has dendrites, a cell body and an axon. It receives and transmits nerve impulses.
6. How does phototropism occur?
Answer: Auxin promotes more cell elongation on the shaded side of a shoot, causing the shoot to bend towards light.
7. Which signals can be disrupted by spinal-cord injury?
Answer: Signals travelling between the brain and parts of the body below the injury can be disrupted.
8. How does chemical coordination occur in plants?
Answer: Plant hormones such as auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin and ABA move to target tissues and regulate growth and responses.
9. Why is control and coordination needed?
Answer: It allows an organism to detect changes and produce suitable, coordinated responses.
10. Differentiate involuntary and reflex actions.
Answer: Involuntary actions are automatic body functions such as heartbeat. Reflex actions are rapid, automatic responses to specific stimuli.
11. Compare nervous and hormonal control.
Answer: Nervous control uses electrical impulses and is rapid and short-lived. Hormonal control uses chemicals and is generally slower but longer-lasting.
12. How does movement in a sensitive plant differ from movement of our legs?
Answer: Sensitive-plant movement is usually due to changes in cell water/turgor, while leg movement is produced by coordinated nervous signals and muscle contraction.
