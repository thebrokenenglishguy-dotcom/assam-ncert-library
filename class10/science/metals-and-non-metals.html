Chapter — Metals and Non-metals

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene?
Answer: Sodium is highly reactive and can react violently with oxygen and moisture. Kerosene keeps it away from them.
2. What gas is produced when a reactive metal reacts with dilute HCl?
Answer: Hydrogen gas.
3. What happens when zinc is added to iron(II) sulphate solution?
Answer: Zinc displaces iron: Zn + FeSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Fe.
4. Why do metals form positive ions?
Answer: They generally lose valence electrons to attain a more stable electronic configuration.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. Which pair gives a displacement reaction: NaCl/Cu, MgCl₂/Al, FeSO₄/Ag, or AgNO₃/Cu?
Answer: AgNO₃ solution and copper metal.
2. Which methods prevent rusting of an iron pan: grease, paint, zinc coating, or all?
Answer: All of the above.
3. An element forms a high-melting, water-soluble oxide. Which is likely: Ca, C, Si or Fe?
Answer: Calcium.
4. Why are food cans coated with tin rather than zinc?
Answer: Zinc is more reactive and could react more readily with food or acidic contents.
5. How can a hammer, battery, bulb, wires and switch distinguish metals from non-metals?
Answer: The hammer tests malleability/shape change; the circuit tests electrical conductivity. Metals generally conduct and are malleable, while non-metals generally do not.
6. What are amphoteric oxides? Give two examples.
Answer: Oxides that react with both acids and bases. Examples: Al₂O₃ and ZnO.
7. Name two metals that displace hydrogen from dilute acids and two that do not.
Answer: Zn and Mg can displace hydrogen; Cu and Ag cannot.
8. In electrolytic refining, what are the anode, cathode and electrolyte?
Answer: Impure metal is the anode, pure metal sheet is the cathode, and a suitable salt solution of the metal is the electrolyte.
9. What happens when sulphur is heated and its gas is tested with dry and moist litmus?
Answer: Sulphur burns to form SO₂. Dry litmus shows little/no change; moist blue litmus turns red because SO₂ forms sulphurous acid with water.
10. Give two ways to prevent rusting.
Answer: Painting and galvanising are two common methods.
11. What type of oxides do non-metals generally form with oxygen?
Answer: Generally acidic or neutral oxides.
12. Why are platinum, gold and silver used for jewellery?
Answer: They are lustrous, less reactive and resist corrosion.
13. Why are sodium, potassium and lithium stored under oil?
Answer: They react rapidly with air and moisture.
14. Why can aluminium be used for cooking utensils although it is reactive?
Answer: It forms a protective aluminium oxide layer that prevents further corrosion.
15. Why are carbonate and sulphide ores converted to oxides during extraction?
Answer: Metal oxides are generally easier to reduce to the free metal.
16. Why does lemon or tamarind clean tarnished copper?
Answer: Their acids react with the basic/oxide coating and dissolve it.
17. Give two chemical differences between metals and non-metals.
Answer: Metals tend to lose electrons and form cations; non-metals tend to gain or share electrons. Metals generally form basic/amphoteric oxides, while non-metals generally form acidic/neutral oxides.
18. What kind of solution could remove the outer layer of gold jewellery and reduce its weight?
Answer: A strong chemical solution capable of dissolving gold, such as aqua regia, could attack the gold.
19. Why is copper used for hot-water tanks rather than ordinary steel?
Answer: Copper is much less reactive with water and corrosion is slower; it is also a good heat conductor.
