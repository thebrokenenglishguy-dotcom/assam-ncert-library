Heredity and Evolution

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. Why do variations improve survival chances?
Answer: A useful variation may help an organism survive and reproduce when environmental conditions change.
2. How are traits inherited?
Answer: Genes carried in DNA are passed from parents to offspring through reproductive cells.
3. What is the difference between homologous and analogous organs?
Answer: Homologous organs have similar basic structure but may perform different functions; analogous organs perform similar functions but have different basic structures.
4. Why are fossils important?
Answer: They provide evidence about organisms that lived in the past and help trace evolutionary relationships.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. In the Mendelian cross where all offspring have violet flowers but about half are short, what is the tall parent’s genotype?
Answer: TtWW.
2. Which is an example of homologous organs?
Answer: The arm of a human and the fore-leg of a dog.
3. Can bacteria, spiders, fish and chimpanzees be called ‘better’ or ‘worse’ in evolutionary terms?
Answer: No. Evolution does not have a universal ladder of better and worse body designs; suitability depends on environment.
4. Why can light-eyed children have light-eyed parents without proving dominance?
Answer: A trait’s appearance alone does not establish dominance; inheritance patterns across suitable crosses are needed.
5. How are classification and evolution linked?
Answer: Similarities and differences used in classification can reveal common ancestry and evolutionary relationships.
6. Explain analogous and homologous organs with examples.
Answer: Human arm and bird wing are homologous as forelimbs with a common basic plan. Wings of birds and insects are analogous for flight but have different structural origins.
7. How could you investigate dominant coat colour in dogs?
Answer: Breed individuals with different coat colours, record offspring traits over generations and use controlled crosses to infer dominance.
8. Why are fossils useful in evolution?
Answer: They show earlier forms, their sequence through geological time and transitional changes.
9. What evidence supports the origin of life from non-living matter?
Answer: Chemical evolution experiments and evidence of simple organic molecules forming under early-Earth-like conditions support the idea.
10. Why does sexual reproduction create more variation than asexual reproduction?
Answer: It combines genetic material from two parents and creates new gene combinations.
11. How is equal genetic contribution from father and mother ensured?
Answer: Gametes carry one set of chromosomes, so fertilisation combines one set from each parent.
12. Do only advantageous variations survive?
Answer: No. Natural selection favours useful variations in particular environments, but neutral or even harmful variations can also persist under some conditions.
