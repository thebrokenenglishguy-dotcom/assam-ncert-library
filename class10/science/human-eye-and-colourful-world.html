The Human Eye and the Colourful World

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. What is accommodation of the eye?
Answer: It is the ability of the eye lens to change its focal length so that objects at different distances are focused on the retina.
2. What are the near and far points of a normal young eye?
Answer: Near point is about 25 cm; far point is infinity.
3. How is myopia corrected?
Answer: With a suitable concave lens.
4. Why does the Sun look reddish at sunrise and sunset?
Answer: Sunlight travels through a longer atmospheric path, so shorter blue wavelengths are scattered more and longer red wavelengths reach the eye.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. The eye focuses at different distances because of what?
Answer: Accommodation.
2. Where is the image formed in a normal human eye?
Answer: On the retina.
3. What is the least distance of distinct vision for a normal young adult?
Answer: About 25 cm.
4. Which structure changes the focal length of the eye lens?
Answer: Ciliary muscles.
5. A person needs −5.5 D for distant vision and +1.5 D for near vision. Find focal lengths.
Answer: f=1/P. For −5.5 D, f≈−0.182 m=−18.2 cm. For +1.5 D, f≈0.667 m=66.7 cm.
6. Why does the sky appear blue?
Answer: Shorter blue wavelengths are scattered more strongly by atmospheric particles than longer wavelengths.
7. Why is the Sun reddish at sunrise and sunset?
Answer: The longer atmospheric path scatters much of the blue light away, leaving more red/orange light to reach the observer.
8. What is dispersion?
Answer: Splitting of white light into its component colours.
9. What is presbyopia?
Answer: An age-related defect in which reduced accommodation makes near vision difficult; it is commonly corrected with suitable convex/bifocal lenses.
10. Differentiate myopia and hypermetropia.
Answer: Myopia: distant objects are unclear and is corrected by a concave lens. Hypermetropia: nearby objects are unclear and is corrected by a convex lens.
