Chapter — Acids, Bases and Salts

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. Why do curd and sour substances not belong in brass or copper vessels?
Answer: Their acids can react with the metals and form harmful or unwanted salts.
2. Which gas is usually released when an acid reacts with a metal? How is it tested?
Answer: Hydrogen is released. It gives a ‘pop’ sound when a burning splint is brought near it.
3. Why do acids show acidic behaviour only in water?
Answer: Water helps acids ionise and produce H⁺/H₃O⁺ ions.
4. Why does dry HCl not change dry blue litmus?
Answer: Without water, HCl does not produce H⁺/H₃O⁺ ions, so it does not show acidic behaviour.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. A solution turns red litmus blue. What is its likely pH?
Answer: 10; it is basic.
2. Egg-shell solution gives a gas that turns lime-water milky. Which acid is likely present?
Answer: Hydrochloric acid (HCl).
3. If 10 mL NaOH needs 8 mL HCl, how much HCl is needed for 20 mL NaOH of the same concentration?
Answer: 16 mL.
4. Which medicine type treats indigestion?
Answer: An antacid.
5. Write the reactions of dilute sulphuric acid with zinc, dilute HCl with magnesium, dilute sulphuric acid with aluminium, and dilute HCl with iron.
Answer: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂; Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂; 2Al + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂; Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂.
6. How can an activity show that alcohol and glucose are not acids although they contain hydrogen?
Answer: Connect solutions separately in a simple conductivity circuit. Acid solutions conduct and light the bulb; glucose and alcohol solutions do not.
7. Why does distilled water conduct poorly while rain water conducts?
Answer: Distilled water has very few ions. Rain water contains dissolved salts and gases that provide ions.
8. Why do acids not show acidic behaviour without water?
Answer: They need water to form H₃O⁺ ions.
9. Five solutions have pH 4, 1, 11, 7 and 9. Identify neutral, strongly alkaline, strongly acidic, weakly acidic and weakly alkaline.
Answer: Neutral: 7; strongly alkaline: 11; strongly acidic: 1; weakly acidic: 4; weakly alkaline: 9. Increasing H⁺ concentration: 11, 9, 7, 4, 1.
10. Why does HCl react more vigorously with magnesium than acetic acid under the same conditions?
Answer: HCl is a strong acid and produces a higher concentration of H⁺ ions than weak acetic acid.
11. What happens to milk’s pH as it changes into curd?
Answer: It decreases because lactic acid is produced, making the milk more acidic.
12. Why may baking soda be added in a small amount to fresh milk?
Answer: It makes the milk slightly alkaline, delaying souring and allowing more time before curd formation.
13. Why must Plaster of Paris be stored in a moisture-proof container?
Answer: Moisture converts it into gypsum, reducing its useful setting property.
14. What is a neutralisation reaction? Give two examples.
Answer: An acid reacts with a base to form salt and water. Examples: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O; H₂SO₄ + 2KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O.
15. Give two important uses each of washing soda and baking soda.
Answer: Washing soda: softens hard water and is used in glass/soap industries. Baking soda: used in baking and as an antacid.
