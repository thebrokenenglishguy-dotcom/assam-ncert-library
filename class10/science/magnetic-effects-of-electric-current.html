Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. What did Oersted observe?
Answer: A current-carrying conductor deflects a nearby compass needle, showing that electric current produces a magnetic field.
2. What is a solenoid?
Answer: A long coil of insulated wire with many turns; when current flows, it produces a magnetic field similar to a bar magnet.
3. State Fleming’s left-hand rule.
Answer: With thumb, forefinger and middle finger mutually perpendicular, forefinger shows field, middle finger current, and thumb force/motion.
4. What is electromagnetic induction?
Answer: The production of induced current in a coil when the magnetic field linked with it changes.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. What does the magnetic field near a long straight current-carrying wire look like?
Answer: Concentric circles centred on the wire.
2. What is electromagnetic induction?
Answer: Induced current produced due to relative motion between a magnet and a coil or a changing magnetic field.
3. Which device produces electric current?
Answer: A generator.
4. What is the essential difference between AC and DC generators?
Answer: An AC generator uses slip rings; a DC generator uses a split-ring commutator.
5. What happens to current during a short circuit?
Answer: It rises sharply because the circuit resistance becomes very small.
6. State true/false: motor converts mechanical to electrical; generator uses electromagnetic induction; field at centre of long circular coil is approximately parallel; green wire is live.
Answer: False, True, True, False.
7. Give two ways of producing magnetic fields.
Answer: Using a permanent magnet and using electric current in a conductor/coil.
8. How does a solenoid behave like a magnet?
Answer: Its current produces a strong field with north and south poles; its field resembles that of a bar magnet.
9. When is force on a current-carrying conductor greatest?
Answer: When the conductor is perpendicular to the magnetic field.
10. An electron beam moves from back to front and is deflected to your right. What is the field direction?
Answer: Using the force rule for a negative charge, the magnetic field is directed downward in the stated viewing geometry.
11. Explain an electric motor and the role of split ring.
Answer: A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences torque. The split ring reverses current every half-turn so rotation continues in the same direction.
12. Name devices using electric motors.
Answer: Fans, mixers, pumps, washing machines, toys and many other appliances.
13. What happens when a bar magnet is pushed into, withdrawn from, or held stationary inside a coil connected to a galvanometer?
Answer: Current is induced while it is moving; the direction reverses on withdrawal. No induced current occurs when it is stationary.
14. Two nearby coils A and B: if current in A changes, is current induced in B?
Answer: Yes. The changing magnetic field of A changes the flux through B.
15. State the rules for magnetic-field direction, force direction and induced-current direction.
Answer: Right-hand thumb rule; Fleming’s left-hand rule; Fleming’s right-hand rule, respectively.
16. Explain an electric generator and the function of brushes.
Answer: Mechanical rotation changes magnetic flux through a coil and induces current. Brushes provide sliding electrical contact between rotating parts and the external circuit.
17. When does short circuit occur?
Answer: When live and neutral wires come into direct contact or a very low-resistance path forms, causing a large current.
18. What is the earth wire and why is it needed?
Answer: It connects the metal body of an appliance to earth and provides a safe path for leakage current, reducing shock risk.
