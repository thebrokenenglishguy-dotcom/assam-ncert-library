Electricity

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. What is electric current?
Answer: The rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor, I=Q/t.
2. What is potential difference?
Answer: Work done per unit charge in moving a charge between two points, V=W/Q.
3. State Ohm’s law.
Answer: At constant temperature, current through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference: V=IR.
4. What factors affect resistance?
Answer: Resistance depends on material, length, cross-sectional area and temperature; R=ρl/A.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. A wire of resistance R is cut into five equal parts and connected in parallel. Find R/R′.
Answer: Each part has R/5. Five equal parts in parallel give R′=R/25, so R/R′=25.
2. Which expression does not represent electrical power?
Answer: Power can be VI, I²R or V²/R. An expression that does not reduce to these is not power.
3. An electric heater uses 5 A at 220 V. Find power.
Answer: P=VI=220×5=1100 W.
4. Why does a bulb glow almost immediately after switching on?
Answer: The electric field and energy transfer establish through the circuit very quickly; electron drift itself is much slower.
5. What is the SI unit of resistivity?
Answer: Ohm metre (Ω m).
6. How are resistors in series different from parallel?
Answer: Series: same current and resistances add. Parallel: same voltage and reciprocal resistances add.
7. Why are household appliances connected in parallel?
Answer: Each appliance gets the supply voltage independently and can be switched on/off separately.
8. What is electric power?
Answer: The rate at which electrical energy is consumed or converted; P=VI=I²R=V²/R.
9. What is electrical energy?
Answer: Energy consumed by an electrical device. E=Pt; the commercial unit is kWh.
10. Why is an ammeter connected in series and a voltmeter in parallel?
Answer: An ammeter measures circuit current and has very low resistance, so it is placed in series. A voltmeter measures potential difference and has high resistance, so it is placed in parallel.
11. Two 24 Ω coils are connected separately, in series and in parallel to 220 V. Find currents.
Answer: Separately: I=220/24≈9.17 A each. Series: R=48 Ω, I≈4.58 A. Parallel: R=12 Ω, I≈18.33 A.
12. Compare power in a 2 Ω resistor in two given circuits: 6 V with 1 Ω and 2 Ω in series; 4 V across 12 Ω and 2 Ω in parallel.
Answer: Case (i): current=2 A, P=I²R=8 W. Case (ii): voltage across 2 Ω=4 V, P=V²/R=8 W.
13. Two 100 W and 60 W lamps at 220 V are parallel. Find line current.
Answer: I=P/V=(100+60)/220≈0.727 A.
14. Which uses more energy: 250 W TV for 1 h or 1200 W toaster for 10 min?
Answer: TV: 250 Wh. Toaster: 200 Wh. The TV uses more.
15. An 8 Ω heater draws 15 A. Find heat developed per second.
Answer: P=I²R=225×8=1800 W, so 1800 J/s.
16. Why is tungsten used in bulb filaments?
Answer: It has a very high melting point and can withstand high temperature while emitting light.
17. Why are alloys used in heating devices?
Answer: They generally have high resistivity, high melting points and resist oxidation at high temperature.
18. Why is series connection unsuitable for domestic circuits?
Answer: One failure would break the whole circuit, and appliances would not receive the required independent voltage.
19. How does resistance vary with wire area?
Answer: For fixed material and length, R is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area.
20. Why are copper and aluminium used for transmission wires?
Answer: They have low resistivity and therefore carry current with relatively small power loss.
