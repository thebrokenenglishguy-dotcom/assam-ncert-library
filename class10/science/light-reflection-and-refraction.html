Light – Reflection and Refraction

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. What are the two laws of reflection?
Answer: The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, normal and reflected ray lie in the same plane.
2. What is the relation between focal length and radius of curvature of a spherical mirror?
Answer: For a small-aperture spherical mirror, R = 2f.
3. What happens to light when it enters a denser medium obliquely from a rarer medium?
Answer: It bends towards the normal.
4. What is refractive index?
Answer: It is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. Which material cannot be used to make a lens: water, glass, plastic or clay?
Answer: Clay.
2. A concave mirror forms a virtual, erect and enlarged image. Where is the object?
Answer: Between the pole and the principal focus.
3. Where should an object be placed before a convex lens to obtain a real image of the same size?
Answer: At twice the focal length (2F).
4. A mirror and lens both have focal length −15 cm. What are they likely to be?
Answer: Both are concave.
5. A mirror always gives an erect image regardless of distance. Which mirror is likely?
Answer: A plane or convex mirror.
6. Which lens is best for reading small letters: +50 cm, −50 cm, +5 cm or −5 cm focal length?
Answer: A convex lens of focal length 5 cm, because it has greater converging power.
7. How can a concave mirror produce an erect image?
Answer: Place the object between the pole and focus. The image is virtual, erect and enlarged.
8. Name mirrors used in car headlights, vehicle rear-view mirrors and solar furnaces.
Answer: Concave mirror; convex mirror; concave mirror.
9. If half a convex lens is covered, does it still form a complete image?
Answer: Yes. The whole object image is still formed, but it becomes less bright because less light passes through.
10. An object 5 cm high is 25 cm from a convex lens of f=10 cm. Find image position and nature.
Answer: Using 1/f=1/v−1/u with u=−25 cm, v≈16.7 cm. The image is real, inverted and diminished; m≈−0.67, so height≈−3.3 cm.
11. A concave lens of focal length 15 cm forms an image 10 cm from it. Find object distance.
Answer: Using 1/f=1/v−1/u with f=−15 cm and v=−10 cm gives u≈−30 cm.
12. An object is 10 cm from a convex mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find image position and nature.
Answer: With u=−10 cm and f=+15 cm, v=+6 cm approximately. The image is virtual, erect and diminished behind the mirror.
13. What does magnification +1 for a plane mirror mean?
Answer: The image is erect, virtual and equal in size to the object.
14. A 5 cm object is 20 cm before a convex mirror of radius 30 cm. Find image position, nature and size.
Answer: f=+15 cm. Using the mirror formula gives v≈+8.6 cm and m≈+0.43, so image height≈2.14 cm; virtual, erect and diminished.
15. A 7 cm object is 27 cm before a concave mirror of f=18 cm. Find screen position, size and nature.
Answer: Using u=−27 cm, f=−18 cm gives v≈−54 cm. Magnification≈−2, so image height≈−14 cm; real, inverted and enlarged.
16. Find focal length of a lens of power −2 D and identify it.
Answer: f=1/P=−0.5 m=−50 cm. It is a concave/diverging lens.
17. A corrective lens has power +1.5 D. Find focal length and type.
Answer: f=1/1.5≈0.667 m or 66.7 cm. It is a convex/converging lens.
