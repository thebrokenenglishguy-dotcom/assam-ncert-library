Chapter — Carbon and its Compounds

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. Why does carbon form covalent bonds instead of simply forming C⁴⁺ or C⁴⁻ ions?
Answer: Losing or gaining four electrons requires too much energy or gives an unstable ion. Carbon therefore shares electrons.
2. What is a covalent bond?
Answer: A bond formed by sharing one or more pairs of electrons between atoms.
3. Why does carbon form a large number of compounds?
Answer: Because of tetravalency and catenation: carbon can form four bonds and link with other carbon atoms into chains and rings.
4. What is a homologous series?
Answer: A family of organic compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties, where successive members differ by –CH₂–.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. How many covalent bonds are present in ethane, C₂H₆?
Answer: Seven: one C–C bond and six C–H bonds.
2. What functional group is present in butanone?
Answer: Ketone group (>C=O).
3. Why does a cooking vessel become black outside when fuel burns poorly?
Answer: Incomplete combustion produces soot (carbon), which deposits on the vessel.
4. Explain covalent bonding using CH₃Cl.
Answer: Carbon shares electrons with three hydrogen atoms and one chlorine atom, forming four single covalent bonds and completing its octet.
5. What is a homologous series? Give an example.
Answer: A series with the same functional group and a regular –CH₂– difference, e.g. methane, ethane, propane and butane are alkane members.
6. How can ethanol and ethanoic acid be distinguished?
Answer: Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydrogencarbonate to release CO₂; ethanol does not. They also differ in smell and acidic behaviour.
7. Why do soaps form micelles in water?
Answer: Soap molecules have a water-loving end and an oil-loving end. Their tails trap grease inside while the ionic ends remain in water, forming micelles.
8. Why are carbon compounds widely used as fuels?
Answer: Many carbon compounds release a large amount of heat on combustion and are readily available.
9. Why does soap form scum in hard water?
Answer: Soap reacts with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions to form insoluble calcium/magnesium salts.
10. What happens when soap is tested with red and blue litmus?
Answer: Soap solution is basic, so red litmus turns blue; blue litmus generally remains blue.
11. What is hydrogenation and one industrial use?
Answer: Hydrogenation adds hydrogen to unsaturated compounds, usually with a catalyst such as Ni. It is used to make vanaspati from vegetable oils.
12. Which of C₂H₆, C₃H₈, C₃H₆, C₂H₂ and CH₄ undergo addition reactions?
Answer: C₃H₆ and C₂H₂, because they contain multiple bonds.
13. How can butter and cooking oil be chemically distinguished?
Answer: Cooking oil is generally more unsaturated and decolourises bromine water or alkaline KMnO₄ more readily than saturated fats.
14. Explain the cleaning action of soap.
Answer: The hydrophobic tail attaches to oily dirt while the hydrophilic ionic end remains in water. Agitation forms micelles that carry dirt into the water.
