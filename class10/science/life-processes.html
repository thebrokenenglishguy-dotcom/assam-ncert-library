Life Processes

Class X Science · Assam textbook

Questions inside the chapter

1. Why is diffusion insufficient for oxygen needs in multicellular organisms?
Answer: Most cells are far from the body surface, so diffusion alone cannot transport enough oxygen over large distances.
2. What raw materials do organisms obtain from outside?
Answer: They obtain food, water, gases such as oxygen or carbon dioxide, and minerals or other nutrients needed for life.
3. What processes are essential for maintaining life?
Answer: Nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion are major life processes.
4. What is the role of saliva?
Answer: Saliva moistens food and contains salivary amylase, which begins the digestion of starch.

End-of-chapter Exercises

1. The kidneys belong to which system?
Answer: Excretory system.
2. What does xylem transport?
Answer: Water and minerals from roots to the rest of the plant.
3. What does autotrophic nutrition require?
Answer: Carbon dioxide, water, chlorophyll and light energy.
4. Where is pyruvate broken down aerobically?
Answer: In mitochondria.
5. How are fats digested and where?
Answer: Bile emulsifies fats in the small intestine, and lipase breaks them into fatty acids and glycerol.
6. What is the role of saliva?
Answer: It moistens food and salivary amylase starts starch digestion.
7. What conditions are needed for autotrophic nutrition and what are the products?
Answer: Light, chlorophyll, carbon dioxide and water are needed; carbohydrates and oxygen are produced.
8. Differentiate aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
Answer: Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases more energy, producing CO₂ and water. Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen and releases less energy; yeast produces ethanol and CO₂.
9. How are alveoli suited for gas exchange?
Answer: They are numerous, thin-walled, moist and richly supplied with blood capillaries, giving a large surface area.
10. What happens if haemoglobin is deficient?
Answer: The blood carries less oxygen, causing tiredness, weakness and breathlessness.
11. Why is double circulation necessary?
Answer: It keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate and supplies oxygen-rich blood efficiently to the body.
12. Differentiate xylem and phloem.
Answer: Xylem mainly transports water/minerals, usually upward. Phloem transports food from sources to sinks in different directions.
13. Compare alveoli and nephrons.
Answer: Alveoli provide a large thin surface for gas exchange; nephrons filter blood and form urine through filtration, reabsorption and secretion.
