Very short/ short answer questions

1. When was Mahatma Gandhi born?

Answer:
  • Mahatma Gandhi was born on 2 October 1869.
  • He was born at Porbandar, Gujarat.

2. In which country did Gandhi first apply the technique of Satyagraha?

Answer:
  • Gandhi first used the technique of Satyagraha in South Africa.

3. Why did Rabindranath Tagore renounce his Knighthood?

Answer:
  • Rabindranath Tagore gave up his Knighthood to protest against the Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 1919.
  • He wanted to show his anger against the British government's cruel action.

4. Under whose leadership was the Khilafat movement started in India?

Answer:
  • The Khilafat Movement was mainly led by the Ali brothers — Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali.
  • Other Muslim leaders also supported the movement.

5. When did the Chauri-Chaura incident take place?

Answer:
  • The Chauri-Chaura incident took place on 5 February 1922.
  • It happened in Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh.

6. Under what circumstances did Lala Lajpat Rai lose his life?

Answer:
  • Lala Lajpat Rai was protesting against the Simon Commission.
  • The police used lathis to stop the protesters.
  • He was badly injured in the lathi charge.
  • He died because of these injuries on 17 November 1928.

7. When and in which session of the Indian National Congress was Purna Swaraj declared to be the chief goal of the Indians?

Answer:
  • Purna Swaraj was declared the main goal in 1929.
  • It was declared at the Lahore Session of the Indian National Congress.
  • The session was presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru.

8. Gandhi started on his Salt march from Sabarmati Ashram to the sea coast at Dandi. What was the distance between Sabarmati Ashram and the sea-coast at Dandi?

Answer:
  • The distance was about 390 km.
  • Gandhi started the march from Sabarmati Ashram and reached Dandi.

9. Who was known as Frontier Gandhi?

  • Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan was known as Frontier Gandhi.

10. Who was the sole Congress representative at the Second Round Table Conference in London?

11. Name the British Prime Minister who announced the Communal Award in 1932.

12. During the course of which movement did Gandhi give the slogan “Do or Die”?

Answer:

13. Who was the founder of the Azad Hind Fauj?

Answer:
  • Subhas Chandra Bose was the main leader who organised and led the Azad Hind Fauj (Indian National Army).

14. “Give me blood and I shall give you freedom”. This famous quote is attributed to which Indian leader?

  • This famous quote is attributed to Subhas Chandra Bose.

15. What is the name of the International Boundary Line between India and Pakistan?

Long Answers

1. What do you understand by the term Satyagraha? Briefly write about the local disputes where Gandhi first applied the technique of Satyagraha in India.

Answer:

Satyagraha means fighting against injustice through truth and non-violence.

  1. Champaran (1917): Farmers were forced by the British to grow indigo. Gandhi supported them and the British had to accept their demands.
  2. Ahmedabad (1918): Gandhi supported mill workers in their fight for better wages.
  3. Kheda (1918): Farmers suffered because of crop failure. Gandhi asked the government to stop collecting land revenue from them.

These movements made Gandhi very popular among the people.

2. Why was the Khilafat movement started in India?

Answer:
  1. The Ottoman Turkish Sultan was considered the Khalifa by many Muslims.
  2. After the First World War, Britain and its allies wanted to reduce the Sultan's power.
  3. Indian Muslims were unhappy with this decision.
  4. They started the Khilafat Movement to protect the position of the Khalifa.
  5. Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali led the movement.
  6. Gandhi supported it and joined it with the Non-Cooperation Movement.

3. Why did Mahatma Gandhi launch the Non-cooperation movement of 1920-22? What were its programmes? What made Gandhi suspend the movement?

Answer:

Gandhi started the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920 because he wanted to protest against British rule, was angry about the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, supported the Khilafat Movement, and wanted Indians to stop cooperating with the British government.

Main programmes:

  1. Boycott of government schools and colleges.
  2. Boycott of British goods.
  3. Boycott of government courts and offices.
  4. Giving up British titles and honours.
  5. Promotion of Khadi and Swadeshi goods.

Suspension: In February 1922, the Chauri-Chaura incident took place. Protesters attacked a police station and 22 policemen were killed. Gandhi believed the movement had become violent, so he suspended it.

4. What was the main aim of the Civil Disobedience Movement? What were the main features of the movement?

Answer:

The main aim was to oppose British laws peacefully and demand freedom for India.

  1. Gandhi started it with the Dandi Salt March in 1930.
  2. People broke the salt law.
  3. People refused to pay some government taxes.
  4. People boycotted British goods.
  5. People protested against British laws peacefully.
  6. Women took part in large numbers.
  7. Many people were arrested by the British government.

5. Under what circumstances was the Quit India movement launched? Do you consider the movement to be a milestone in India's struggle for independence? Give reasons in support of your answer.

Answer:

The Quit India Movement was launched in 1942.

Why was it launched?

  1. The British involved India in the Second World War without asking Indian leaders.
  2. Indians wanted the British to leave India immediately.
  3. The British government did not accept the demands of Indian leaders.
  4. The Cripps Mission failed to solve the problem.
  5. Gandhi and the Congress decided to start the Quit India Movement.

Why was it important?

  1. Gandhi gave the famous slogan “Do or Die.”
  2. People across India joined the movement.
  3. Students, workers, women and ordinary people took part.
  4. The British faced strong opposition throughout the country.
  5. It showed that Indians would no longer accept British rule.
  6. It increased pressure on the British to leave India.

6. Write a brief note on the Indian National Army.

Answer:
  • The Indian National Army (INA) was also known as the Azad Hind Fauj.
  • It was formed to fight against British rule in India.
  • Mohan Singh played an important role in its early formation.
  • Later, Subhas Chandra Bose took leadership of the INA.
  • Bose gave the army the slogan “Jai Hind.”
  • He also gave the famous call “Give me blood and I shall give you freedom.”
  • The INA fought against the British during the Second World War.
  • Its activities created strong patriotic feelings among Indians.
  • The INA became an important part of India's struggle for independence.
