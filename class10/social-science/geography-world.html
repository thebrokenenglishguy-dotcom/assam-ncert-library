Exercise

1. Write in brief about the earth’s lithosphere and hydrosphere.

Lithosphere

  • The lithosphere is the solid outer part of the Earth.
  • It includes continents, islands, rocks and soil.
  • Human beings live mainly on the lithosphere.

Hydrosphere

  • The hydrosphere includes all the water on Earth.
  • It includes oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, glaciers and groundwater.
  • About 71% of the Earth's surface is covered by water.

2. How was the earth in the early days according to the Continental Drift Theory?

  • According to the Continental Drift Theory, all the continents were once joined together.
  • They formed one huge landmass called Pangaea.
  • Pangaea was surrounded by a large ocean called Panthalassa.
  • Later, Pangaea slowly broke into smaller pieces.
  • These pieces moved and formed the continents we see today.

3. Write briefly about the topography of the ocean floor.

The ocean floor is not flat. It has different landforms:

  1. Continental shelf – shallow part near the coast.
  2. Continental slope – steep slope after the continental shelf.
  3. Deep-sea plain – large and almost flat area.
  4. Oceanic trenches – very deep and narrow depressions.
  5. Mid-ocean ridges – long underwater mountain ranges.

4. Write briefly about the distribution of oceans and continents.

  • Oceans cover about 71% of the Earth's surface.
  • Continents cover about 29%.

The five major oceans are:

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Southern Ocean
  5. Arctic Ocean

The seven continents are:

  1. Asia
  2. Africa
  3. Europe
  4. North America
  5. South America
  6. Australia/Oceania
  7. Antarctica

5. Write in short about the landforms present on the earth’s land surface.

The main landforms are:

  1. Mountains – very high areas of land.
  2. Plateaus – high and mostly flat areas.
  3. Plains – large areas of flat or gently sloping land.
  4. Valleys – low areas between mountains or hills.
  5. Deserts – very dry areas with little rainfall.

6. Briefly discuss the physical characteristics of Asia.

Asia is the largest continent in the world.

  1. The Himalayas are in the south-central part of Asia.
  2. Mount Everest, the world's highest peak, is in the Himalayas.
  3. The Tibetan Plateau is the highest large plateau.
  4. Asia has large plains such as the Indo-Gangetic Plain.
  5. It has large deserts such as the Gobi and Arabian Deserts.
  6. Many important rivers flow through Asia.
  7. Asia has many different types of climate and vegetation.

7. Give your answer with reasons

(a) Why do 90 per cent of the world's total population concentrate in the northern hemisphere?

About 90% of the world's population lives in the Northern Hemisphere because:

  1. The Northern Hemisphere has a larger area of land.
  2. It has large and fertile plains.
  3. It has many rivers and water resources.
  4. The climate is suitable for human settlement in many areas.
  5. Important agricultural and industrial regions are located there.
  6. Most of the world's large cities are also found in the Northern Hemisphere.

(b) What is the reason behind lack of permanent human settlement in Antarctica?

There is no permanent human settlement in Antarctica because:

  1. It has an extremely cold climate.
  2. Most of the land is covered by thick ice.
  3. Temperatures can fall very far below 0°C.
  4. There is very little vegetation.
  5. Agriculture is almost impossible.
  6. The conditions are very difficult for normal human life.

9. Write short notes

(a) Pangaea

  • Pangaea was a huge supercontinent.
  • According to the Continental Drift Theory, all the present continents were once joined together.
  • This large landmass was called Pangaea.
  • Later, it broke into smaller parts.
  • These parts slowly moved and became the present continents.

(b) Arctic Ocean

  • The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest major ocean.
  • It is located around the North Pole.
  • It is surrounded mainly by North America, Europe and Asia.
  • It remains covered by sea ice for much of the year.
  • It has a very cold climate.

(c) The Continent of Oceania

  • Oceania is the region of the Pacific Ocean containing Australia and many islands.
  • Australia is the largest landmass in this region.
  • It also includes many islands of the Pacific.
  • Important island groups include Melanesia, Micronesia and Polynesia.
  • The region has many different cultures and natural environments.

(d) The lakes of Africa

Africa has many large lakes.

Important lakes include:

  1. Lake Victoria
  2. Lake Tanganyika
  3. Lake Malawi
  4. Lake Chad
  • Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa by area.
  • These lakes are important for water, fishing, transport and agriculture.

(e) The rivers of Asia

Asia has many large and important rivers.

Some major rivers are:

  1. Yangtze
  2. Huang He
  3. Ganges
  4. Brahmaputra
  5. Indus
  6. Mekong
  7. Ob
  8. Yenisei

These rivers are important for agriculture, transport, water supply and electricity generation.

(f) The rivers of Europe

Important rivers of Europe include:

  1. Volga
  2. Danube
  3. Rhine
  4. Rhône
  5. Elbe
  • The Volga is the longest river in Europe.
  • These rivers are important for transport, agriculture and industries.

(g) The Appalachian Highland Region

  • The Appalachian Highland is an important mountain region of eastern North America.
  • It extends mainly through the United States and Canada.
  • The Appalachian Mountains are old mountains.
  • They are lower and more worn down than the young fold mountains.
  • The region has forests and many mineral resources.

(h) The lakes of North America

North America has many important lakes.

The five Great Lakes are:

  1. Superior
  2. Michigan
  3. Huron
  4. Erie
  5. Ontario
  • They are located between the United States and Canada.
  • They are important for transport, fishing and water supply.
