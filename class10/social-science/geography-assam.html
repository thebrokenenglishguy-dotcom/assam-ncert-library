1. Write briefly about Assam's land area, population and population density with necessary data.

  • The total area of Assam is about 78,438 sq. km.
  • According to the 2011 Census, Assam's population was about 3.12 crore.
  • The population density was about 398 persons per sq. km.
  • Assam covers about 2.4% of India's total geographical area.
  • The population is not equally distributed throughout the state. Ospin Academy

2. Briefly mention how the literacy rate of Assam is increasing.

  • The literacy rate of Assam has increased over the years.
  • According to the 2011 Census, the literacy rate was 72.19%.
  • Male literacy was about 77.85%.
  • Female literacy was about 66.27%.
  • The increase is mainly due to the growth of schools, education facilities and government programmes. Ospin Academy

According to the 2011 Census:

Remember:

  • First-class town → population of 1 lakh or more.

4. Describe the trend of population growth in Assam during 1901-2011.

  • Assam's population increased continuously from 1901 to 2011.
  • In 1901, the population was about 32.9 lakh.
  • In 1951, it increased to about 80.3 lakh.
  • After independence, population growth became faster.
  • Migration from outside Assam was an important reason for this rapid growth.
  • Population density increased from about 42 persons/sq. km in 1901 to 398 persons/sq. km in 2011.
  • Assam's share of India's population also increased during this period. Ospin Academy

5. Analyse the geographical region-wise distribution of population in Assam.

The population of Assam is not equally distributed.

  1. The Brahmaputra Valley has the largest share of population.
  2. About 85% of Assam's population lives in the Brahmaputra Valley.
  3. The valley has fertile soil and plain land.
  4. These conditions are suitable for agriculture and transport.
  5. The Barak Valley is also fairly densely populated.
  6. The hill region has much less population because of its difficult terrain.
  7. Therefore, population density is highest in areas with better land, agriculture, transport and economic opportunities. Ospin Academy

6. Briefly discuss the causes responsible for variation in the distribution of Assam's population with suitable examples.

The main causes are:

  1. Physical conditions – Plain areas have more population than hilly areas.
  2. Fertile soil – Fertile areas support agriculture and attract people.
  3. Economic development – Developed areas have more employment opportunities.
  4. Transport and communication – Areas with better transport have more settlements.
  5. Availability of resources – Water, forests and minerals influence settlement.
  6. Social and cultural factors also affect population distribution.

Example:

The Brahmaputra Valley is densely populated because it has plain land, fertile soil and better opportunities for agriculture and transport. Ospin Academy

9. Mention the different human migration streams sequentially coming to Assam since ancient times to the present.

Different groups came to Assam at different times:

  1. Austrics – one of the earliest groups; they came from South-East Asia.
  2. Tibeto-Burman/Mongoloid groups – came through the Himalayan region.
  3. Indo-Aryans – came from the western/northern Indian plains.
  4. Muslim groups – began coming from the early medieval period.
  5. Ahoms – came from Myanmar through the Patkai Hills in 1228 under Sukapha.
  6. East Bengal peasants – migrated to Assam mainly during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
  7. Later, people from different parts of India also came for jobs, trade and other activities. Ospin Academy

10. Give the ethnic identity of the Ahoms.

  • The Ahoms belong mainly to the Tibeto-Burman/Mongoloid group.
  • They originally came from China and later settled in Myanmar.
  • In 1228, they entered Assam through the Patkai Hills.
  • They were led by Sukapha.
  • They first settled in Upper Assam.
  • They established the Ahom Kingdom.
  • The Ahoms ruled Assam for about 600 years.
  • They made important contributions to the culture, society and history of Assam. Ospin Academy

11. Briefly state how the transport system of Assam has been helping the state's economy.

Transport is very important for Assam's economy.

  1. It connects different parts of Assam.
  2. It connects Assam with the rest of India.
  3. It helps transport raw materials and finished goods.
  4. It helps farmers take their products to markets.
  5. It supports trade and industries.
  6. It helps Assam supply goods to other North-Eastern states.
  7. Important products such as tea, oil and natural gas can be transported to other places.
  8. Thus, transport helps in the economic development of Assam. Ospin Academy

12. Write a short note on water transport in Assam.

  • Assam has many rivers, so inland water transport is important.
  • The main waterways are the Brahmaputra and Barak rivers.
  • About 891 km of the Brahmaputra is navigable within Assam.
  • The Brahmaputra waterway was declared National Waterway-2.
  • The Barak waterway was later declared National Waterway-16.
  • Water transport is useful for carrying people and heavy goods.
  • It is generally cheaper than some other forms of transport.
  • It is also useful during floods and natural disasters. Ospin Academy

13. Mention five major causes which are regarded as hindrance to the development of transport system of Assam.

Five major causes are:

  1. Difficult physical conditions such as hills, wetlands and floodplains.
  2. Frequent floods and heavy rainfall damage roads.
  3. Assam is connected with the rest of India through a narrow corridor.
  4. Poor development of industries and trade reduces transport demand.
  5. Lack of sufficient investment and infrastructure.
  6. In some areas, transport projects are not completed properly or on time.
  7. Lack of proper coordination between road, railway, water and air transport also creates problems. Ospin Academy

18. How has the growing population of Assam put impact on the natural resources?

  3. Wetlands are filled for settlements.
  4. Agricultural land is converted into residential areas.

  8. Forest and mineral resources are used faster.

22. What are the agro-climatic regions of Assam? Mention the characteristics of each of them in brief.

Assam has six agro-climatic regions.

1. North Bank Plain Zone

  • Located north of the Brahmaputra.
  • Has fertile plains.
  • Receives heavy rainfall.
  • Rice and other crops are important.

2. Upper Brahmaputra Valley Zone

  • Includes areas such as Dibrugarh, Tinsukia, Jorhat and Golaghat.
  • Famous for tea cultivation.
  • Has fertile soil and good rainfall.

3. Central Brahmaputra Valley Zone

  • Mainly includes Nagaon and Morigaon.
  • Suitable for agriculture.
  • Rice is an important crop.

4. Lower Brahmaputra Valley Zone

  • Includes areas such as Kamrup, Barpeta, Nalbari, Goalpara and Dhubri.
  • Has fertile alluvial plains.
  • Rice and other crops are widely grown.

5. Barak Valley Zone

  • Includes Cachar, Karimganj and Hailakandi.
  • Has fertile plains.
  • Rice, tea and other crops are important.

6. Hill Zone

  • Includes Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao.
  • Has hilly and forested land.
  • Jhum cultivation is practised in some areas. Ospin Academy

23. What are the types of rice cultivated in Assam? Briefly write about these.

There are three main types of rice cultivated in Assam:

1. Winter Rice / Sali Rice

  • It is the most widely grown rice.
  • It is grown during the monsoon season.
  • It is harvested around November–December.
  • It grows well in fertile alluvial soil.

2. Summer Rice / Boro Rice

  • It is mainly grown in low-lying areas.
  • It is harvested during June–July.
  • It is important in flood-prone areas because it can be harvested before major floods.

3. Autumn Rice

  • It is grown mainly in the plains.
  • It is harvested around June–July.
  • Both sowing and planting methods are used. Ospin Academy

26. Write briefly about jhum cultivation (shifting cultivation) carried out in Assam.

  • Jhum cultivation is also called shifting cultivation.
  • It is mainly practised in the hill areas of Assam.
  • It is common among some tribal communities.
  • First, trees and vegetation are cleared and burnt.
  • The ash acts as a natural fertiliser.
  • Crops are grown for a few years.
  • When soil fertility decreases, farmers move to another piece of land.
  • It is mainly practised in Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao.
  • Excessive jhum cultivation can cause deforestation, soil erosion and loss of soil fertility. Ospin Academy

30. Write about the tea industry of Assam.

  • A government tea garden was started in 1833.

32. Mention the problems of agricultural development in Assam.

The main problems are:

  1. Traditional farming methods are still common.
  2. Jhum cultivation is practised in hill areas.
  3. Agricultural land is often divided into small plots.
  4. Irrigation facilities are not sufficient.
  5. Floods and river-bank erosion damage crops and farmland.
  6. Modern farming technology is not used enough.
  7. Storage and preservation facilities are limited.
  8. Farmers may not get a fair price for their products.
  9. Good-quality seeds and fertilisers are not always easily available.
  10. Transport and communication facilities are inadequate in some farming areas.
  11. Agricultural infrastructure is still underdeveloped. Ospin Academy
