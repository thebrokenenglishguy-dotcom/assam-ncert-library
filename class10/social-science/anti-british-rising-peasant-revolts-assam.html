Long answer type questions:

1. Discuss in brief the causes of the revolt of 1857-58.

There were several reasons for the revolt of 1857–58 in Assam:

  1. The British introduced a cash system of collecting taxes.
  2. People had to pay taxes in money, which was difficult for poor farmers.
  3. Marwari and Bengali moneylenders started lending money at high interest.
  4. Many Assamese nobles and important people lost their old positions and privileges.
  5. The British took control of many revenue-free lands.
  6. The abolition of slavery also created problems for some sections of society.
  7. Many people were unhappy with British rule and their policies.
  8. These problems created anger against the British and helped the revolt to grow.

2. Discuss the role of Maniram Dewan in the revolt of 1857-58 in Assam.

  1. He wanted to remove British rule from Assam.
  2. He wanted to restore the Ahom kingdom.
  3. He wanted Kandarpeswar Singha to become the Ahom king.
  4. In 1857, he went to Calcutta to request the British to restore the Ahom monarchy.
  5. There he met Madhu Malik, a Bengali Muktiyar.
  6. They secretly planned to fight against the British.
  7. Maniram contacted people in Assam and planned to get support from soldiers.
  8. He planned to bring arms and ammunition from Calcutta.
  9. The British discovered the plan through his letters.
  10. Maniram was arrested and later hanged on 26 February 1858 along with Piyoli Barua.

Thus, Maniram Dewan became one of the earliest important freedom fighters of Assam.

3. Discuss the reasons why the revolt of 1857-58 failed in Assam.

  1. The number of rebels was very small.
  2. Most of the support was limited to Jorhat and Sibsagar.
  3. There was little support from places like Nagaon, Kamrup and Guwahati.
  4. The rebels delayed starting the revolt.
  5. This gave the British enough time to prepare.
  6. Maniram Dewan was in Calcutta, far away from Assam.
  7. The rebels failed to keep their plans secret.
  8. The British came to know about the plan through the letters.
  9. The rebels did not have enough arms and ammunition.
  10. The local soldiers did not give enough support to the rebels.

Therefore, the British were able to stop the revolt before it could become a large movement.

4. Discuss the importance of the revolt of 1857 in Assam.

  1. It was one of the first major attempts in Assam to fight against British rule.
  2. The sacrifice of Maniram Dewan and Piyoli Barua inspired the people.
  3. Many peasant revolts took place in Assam after 1857.
  4. These revolts continued in different places from 1860 to 1894.
  5. The revolt created a feeling of nationalism among the people.
  6. Both Hindus and Muslims took part in the movement.
  7. Later freedom fighters were inspired by the sacrifice of the early rebels.
  8. Therefore, the revolt became an important part of Assam's freedom struggle.

5. Write a note on the Phulaguri Dhawa.

  • The Phulaguri Dhawa was an important peasant revolt in Assam.
  • It took place in 1861 at Phulaguri in Nagaon.
  • The peasants were unhappy with British taxes and policies.
  • The British had also banned poppy cultivation.
  • The peasants wanted the ban to be removed.
  • They also opposed the tax on Tamul-Paan.
  • The peasants held Raij Mels to discuss their problems.
  • On 18 October 1861, Lieutenant Singer came to control the meeting.
  • A fight started between the peasants and the police.
  • Lieutenant Singer was killed during the clash.
  • The British later arrested and punished many peasants.

The Phulaguri Dhawa is remembered as an important early peasant protest against British rule in Assam.

6. Discuss the peasant revolt of North Kamrup in 1894.

  1. The British increased the land revenue in the Brahmaputra Valley.
  2. The farmers found it very difficult to pay the increased tax.
  3. They first sent petitions to the government.
  4. When the government did not listen, the farmers organised Raij Mels.
  5. Large numbers of farmers gathered at places such as Rangia, Barama and Lachima.
  6. At Rangia, thousands of farmers gathered in January 1894.
  7. In some places, people attacked revenue officials.
  8. The British used the police and military to suppress the revolt.
  9. Many farmers were arrested and punished.
  10. The revolt showed the strong anger of the farmers against British taxation.

7. Give an account of the peasant revolt of Patharughat in 1894.

  1. It took place in 1894 in the Darrang district.
  2. The main reason was the increase in land revenue.
  3. The peasants first protested peacefully through Raij Mels.
  4. They refused to pay the increased revenue.
  5. A large meeting was planned at Patharughat.
  6. On 28 January 1894, about 2,000 peasants gathered there.
  7. The British officials ordered the peasants to leave the place.
  8. The peasants refused to leave.
  9. The police then opened fire on the crowd.
  10. Many peasants were killed and injured.
  11. Several peasants were also arrested and punished.

The Patharughat revolt became an important symbol of the peasants' fight against British exploitation.

8. Write a note on the Jaintia revolt against the British in 1850.

Important: The question says 1850, but the detailed section associates the Jaintia revolt with 1861–63.

  • The British imposed house tax on the people of the Jaintia Hills.
  • Later, they also introduced income tax.
  • The people were unhappy because they had not been used to paying such taxes.
  • They also opposed British interference in their traditional way of life.
  • The Jaintias started a revolt against the British.
  • Ukiang Nongbah became the main leader.
  • The rebels attacked the British camp at Jowai.
  • The British sent soldiers to control the revolt.
  • Ukiang Nongbah was captured and hanged by the British.
  • The revolt finally ended by 1863.

9. Write about the anti-British revolt of 1891 in Manipur under the leadership of Tikendrajit.

  • In 1890, there was a dispute in the royal family of Manipur.
  • The British interfered in the matter.
  • Tikendrajit was the Commander-in-Chief of the Manipuri army.
  • He opposed British interference in Manipur.
  • The British wanted to arrest and remove him.
  • Chief Commissioner J.W. Quinton came to Manipur with British officers to capture him.
  • The Manipuris fought against the British.
  • Quinton and some other British officers were killed.
  • The British then sent a large military force to Manipur.
  • Tikendrajit was captured by the British.
  • He was given the death sentence and hanged on 13 August 1891.
  • He is remembered as a brave leader who fought against British rule.

Write Short Notes :

1. The Revenue system implemented by the British in Assam.

  • The British changed the old system of revenue collection in Assam.
  • Earlier, people often paid their dues in kind.
  • The British introduced the system of paying taxes in cash.
  • This was difficult for many farmers because they did not have enough money.
  • Farmers had to borrow money from moneylenders.
  • The moneylenders often charged high interest.
  • The British also introduced new taxes such as Stamp Tax, Income Tax, License Tax and Grazing Tax.
  • The heavy taxes made the condition of the farmers very poor.
  • These policies became one of the main reasons for the peasant revolts in Assam.

2. Maniram Dewan.

  • Maniram Dewan was an important Assamese leader.
  • He was one of the early people who started tea gardens in Assam.
  • He first worked with the British but later became their opponent.
  • He wanted to restore the Ahom kingdom.
  • He supported Kandarpeswar Singha as the Ahom king.
  • He played an important role in planning the revolt of 1857 in Assam.
  • He worked with Madhu Malik and other leaders.
  • The British discovered his plan and arrested him.
  • He was hanged on 26 February 1858.
  • He is remembered as an important freedom fighter and martyr of Assam.

The peasant revolts were important because:

  1. They showed that the people were not ready to accept British exploitation.

  3. They created anger against British rule.
  4. They brought people together through Raij Mels.
  5. They inspired later anti-British movements.
  6. They helped create a feeling of unity and nationalism.
  7. They became an important part of the freedom struggle of Assam.
