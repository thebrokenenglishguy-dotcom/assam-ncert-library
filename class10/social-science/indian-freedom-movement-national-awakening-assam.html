Very Short Answer type Questions

1. When was the Treaty of Yandaboo signed?

The Treaty of Yandaboo was signed on 26 February 1826.

2. Which period is known as Company Raj in Assam?

The period from 1826 to 1858 is known as Company Raj in Assam.

3. When was Bengali Language introduced in Assam?

Bengali was introduced as the official language of Assam in 1837.

4. When was Bengali Language replaced by Assamese language?

Assamese replaced Bengali as the official language in 1873.

5. What was the mouthpiece of Assam Chatra Sanmilan?

Milan was the mouthpiece of Assam Chatra Sanmilan.

6. What was the main objective of the Ryot Sabha?

The main objective was to protect the interests of farmers. It demanded lower land tax and removal of grazing tax.

7. When was All Assam Ryot Sabha established?

The All Assam Ryot Sabha was established on 8 April 1933.

8. Who was the Secretary of Ahom Sabha?

Padmanath Gohain Baruah was the Secretary of the Ahom Sabha.

9. When and under whose leadership was Jorhat Sarbajanik Sabha constituted?

It was constituted in 1884 at Jorhat under the leadership of Jagannath Barua.

10. Who were the first President and Secretary of Assam Provincial Congress Committee?

President: Kuladhar Chaliha
Secretary: Nabin Chandra Bordoloi

11. Who was the first formally elected president of APCC?

Bishnuram Medhi was the first formally elected President of APCC.

12. Who was the first Prime Minister of Assam and when was he elected?

Gopinath Bordoloi was the first Prime Minister of Assam. He was elected in 1946.

13. On what crime Kushal Konwar was hanged?

Kushal Konwar was hanged in connection with the derailment of a train at Barpathar (Sarupathar) during the Quit India Movement.

14. When was Gauhati University established?

Gauhati University was established on 26 January 1948.

15. When the name Assam Medical College was formally introduced?

The name Assam Medical College was formally introduced on 3 November 1947.

16. When was Gauhati Medical College formally started?

Gauhati Medical College was formally started on 20 September 1960.

Short Answer type Questions

1. Write a brief note on Assamese Literary Society.

  1. The Assamese Literary Society was formed in 1872.
  2. It promoted Assamese language and literature.
  3. It encouraged educated Assamese people to develop their language and culture.
  4. It helped create social and national awareness.
  5. It played an important role in the growth of Assamese literature.

2. Give a brief trace of important nationalist rise in Jonaki Age.

  1. The Jonaki Age began around 1889 with the publication of Jonaki.
  2. Jonaki was an important literary magazine of Assam.
  3. Chandrakumar Agarwala was its first editor.
  4. Lakshminath Bezbarua, Chandrakumar Agarwala and Hemchandra Goswami created national feelings through their writings.
  5. Their writings helped create national awareness among Assamese people.

3. Write briefly on the political demands of Ahom Sabha.

  1. The Ahoms should be recognised as a minority community.
  2. They should get reserved seats in the Assembly.
  3. They demanded a separate electorate for the Ahom community.
  4. They worked to protect the social, economic and cultural interests of the Ahoms.

4. What were the issues raised by Assam Association before the British Government?

  1. Opposition to high taxes.
  2. Demand for prohibition of opium.
  3. More Assamese representation in government bodies.
  4. Opposition to the influence of tea planters in local bodies.
  5. Development of technical education.
  6. Assamese teachers in Cotton College.
  7. Opposition to the Partition of Bengal in 1905.
  8. Establishment of a university in Assam.

5. Mention about the Assamese leaders who had led the Non-cooperation Movement in Assam.

Important leaders included Chandranath Sarma, Hemchandra Barua, Omeo Kumar Das, Muhibuddin Ahmed, Lakhidhar Sarma, Nabin Chandra Bordoloi, Kuladhar Chaliha, Tarun Ram Phookan and Bishnuram Medhi. They encouraged students, lawyers and common people to take part.

6. Write about Cunningham Circular.

  1. The Cunningham Circular was issued on 30 May 1930 by R. J. Cunningham, Director of Public Instruction.
  2. Students and their parents were asked to promise that students would not take part in political activities.
  3. Disobedient students could face fines, loss of scholarships or expulsion.
  4. Students protested against the circular.
  5. It increased student participation in the Civil Disobedience Movement.

7. Write briefly about the problems faced by the Ministry of Gopinath Bordoloi after independence.

  1. Partition badly affected Assam.
  2. Communication with the rest of India became difficult.
  3. Railway and river routes between Assam and West Bengal passed through East Pakistan.
  4. Assam faced problems because of the new international border.
  5. Immigration from East Pakistan increased.
  6. Trade in tea, jute, silk and other goods was affected.
  7. The state had a weak economy and lacked proper educational facilities.

8. Write brief history of establishment of Assam Agriculture University.

  1. Agricultural research started with a research station at Karimganj in 1913.
  2. Another research station was started at Titabar in 1923.
  3. Assam Agricultural College was established at Jorhat in 1948.
  4. Assam Veterinary College was also established.
  5. In 1969, these institutions were brought together to form Assam Agricultural University.
  6. The university works for agricultural education, research and development.

9. Write brief history of establishment of Assam Medical College.

  1. Its history goes back to the Berry White School of Medicine, established at Dibrugarh in 1900.
  2. It was started with a donation of ₹50,000 from Dr. John Berry White.
  3. In 1910, two X-ray machines were brought from England.
  4. On 3 November 1947, it was formally renamed Assam Medical College.
  5. It became the first medical college in North-East India.

Long Answer type Questions

1. Discuss the role and activities of Asomiya Bhasa Unnati Sadhini Sabha.

  1. It worked for the development of Assamese language.
  2. It wanted Assamese to become equal to other developed languages.
  3. It popularised Assamese and encouraged people to protect their mother tongue.
  4. It held discussions on Assam's history, culture, literature and language.
  5. Its members wrote books, poems and articles about Assamese history and culture.
  6. Lakshminath Bezbarua and Padmanath Gohain Baruah helped create pride in Assam's history.
  7. Its activities created national and social awareness.
  8. It helped in the growth of Assamese literature.
  9. It later became an important step towards the development of Asom Sahitya Sabha.

2. Write about the contribution of Assam Chatra Sanmilan.

  1. It was the first important student organisation of Assam.
  2. It brought Assamese students together.
  3. Lakshminath Bezbarua was its first president.
  4. It worked mainly for the development of Assamese language and literature.
  5. It encouraged students to love their mother tongue and culture.
  6. At first, it stayed away from direct political activities.
  7. Later, its students took part in the national movement.
  8. During the Civil Disobedience Movement, students took part in protests and picketing.
  9. It created national awareness among the youth of Assam.

3. Give an account on the objectives and role of the Ryot Sabhas.

  1. They protected the interests of peasants.
  2. They opposed the British land revenue system.
  3. They demanded reduction of land tax and abolition of grazing tax.
  4. They opposed unfair treatment of farmers and raised their problems before the government.
  5. Tezpur Ryot Sabha and Nowgong Ryot Sabha were important early Ryot Sabhas.
  6. They worked through meetings and petitions.
  7. The All Assam Ryot Sabha was formed on 8 April 1933 and brought different Ryot Sabhas together.
  8. They increased political awareness among farmers.

4. Write about the objectives and activities of Jorhat Sarbajanik Sabha.

  1. It presented people's problems before the government.
  2. It explained government policies to the people.
  3. It worked to improve the condition of the people and opposed unfair policies.
  4. It raised its voice against the Assam Land and Revenue Regulation Act, 1886.
  5. It supported farmers protesting against increased land revenue.
  6. It demanded gradual abolition of opium.
  7. It opposed the Partition of Bengal in 1905.
  8. It worked through peaceful and constitutional methods and created political awareness.

5. Write on the contribution of Assam Association towards social reform in British ruled Assam.

  1. The Assam Association was formed in 1903.
  2. It acted as a voice of the people of Assam.
  3. It presented people's problems before the British government.
  4. It opposed high taxes and unfair government policies.
  5. It demanded prohibition of opium.
  6. It wanted more Assamese people in government and local bodies.
  7. It demanded technical education and supported Assamese language and education.
  8. It opposed the Partition of Bengal in 1905.
  9. It demanded a university in Assam.
  10. It supported the Non-Cooperation Movement.
  11. In 1921, it merged with the Assam Provincial Congress Committee.

6. Give brief description on the creation and the role of Assam Provincial Congress Committee.

  1. The Assam Provincial Congress Committee (APCC) was formed in 1921.
  2. The Assam Association decided to join the Congress movement.
  3. The decision to merge with the APCC was taken at Jorhat on 18 April 1921.
  4. An ad hoc APCC was formed in June 1921.
  5. Kuladhar Chaliha became its president; Bishnuram Medhi later became the first formally elected President.
  6. The APCC invited Mahatma Gandhi to Assam in 1921.
  7. It promoted Swadeshi and Khadi and organised Assam's participation in national movements.
  8. The 1926 Congress session was held at Pandu, Guwahati.
  9. The APCC played an important role in Assam's freedom struggle.

7. Give brief description on the role of Assam in Swadeshi Movement.

  1. The Swadeshi Movement began in 1905 against the Partition of Bengal.
  2. The movement spread to Assam.
  3. People used Indian-made goods and boycotted many British goods.
  4. The Assam Association supported the movement.
  5. It encouraged Assamese language and education and national schools were opened in different places.
  6. The movement became especially strong in the Surma Valley.
  7. It encouraged self-dependence and increased national feeling.
  8. It connected Assam with the wider Indian freedom movement.

8. Give an account on the role of Assam in Non-cooperation movement.

  1. The movement began under Mahatma Gandhi's leadership.
  2. Gandhi visited Assam in 1921.
  3. Students boycotted government schools and colleges.
  4. Leaders such as Omeo Kumar Das, Lakshmidhar Sarma and Chandranath Sarma encouraged students.
  5. Many lawyers gave up legal practice.
  6. People boycotted British goods and the use and production of Khadi increased.
  7. The movement also helped reduce opium consumption.
  8. Tea garden workers supported the movement.
  9. It became a mass movement in Assam.

9. Give an account on the role of Assam in Civil Disobedience Movement.

  1. Assam took an active part in the Civil Disobedience Movement.
  2. Bishnuram Medhi, Muhammad Tayebullah and Ambikagiri Raychoudhury played important roles.
  3. Students boycotted classes and joined protests.
  4. The Cunningham Circular made students more angry.
  5. Assam Chatra Sanmilan decided to take part.
  6. Students organised picketing at government offices, opium shops and liquor shops.
  7. Farmers joined and demanded reduction of land revenue.
  8. Women also took an active part.
  9. The movement became a mass movement in Assam.

10. Write about the Quit India Movement in Assam.

  1. The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1942.
  2. It demanded that the British should leave India.
  3. Students, workers, political leaders and ordinary people took part in Assam.
  4. There were strikes, demonstrations and protests.
  5. Students distributed anti-British leaflets.
  6. Some people damaged railway tracks and communication lines, and government buildings were attacked in some places.
  7. Important incidents took place at Barpathar, Rangia, Panbari and Nagaon.
  8. The British used police and military force; many people were arrested and punished.
  9. Kushal Konwar was hanged in connection with the Barpathar train derailment.
  10. The movement showed the strong desire of the people of Assam for freedom.

11. Give a brief description about the institutions established in Assam under the leadership of Bordoloi Ministry.

  1. Assam Agricultural University: Agricultural research had started at Karimganj in 1913 and Titabar in 1923. Assam Agricultural College was established at Jorhat in 1948. Assam Veterinary College was established in 1948. In 1969, Assam Agricultural University was established by bringing these institutions together.
  2. Gauhati University: It was established on 26 January 1948 and helped develop higher education in Assam.
  3. Assam Medical College: Its history began with the Berry White School of Medicine in 1900. It was renamed Assam Medical College on 3 November 1947 and became the first medical college in North-East India.
  4. These institutions improved higher education, agriculture and medical education in Assam.
