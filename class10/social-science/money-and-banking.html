Exercise — Very Short Answer Type Questions

1. What is barter?

Answer:

  • Barter is the direct exchange of one good or service for another good or service without using money.

Example: Rice is exchanged for clothes.

2. What is money?

Answer:

  • Money is anything that is generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services.

3. Mention one important function of money.

  • Money acts as a medium of exchange.

4. Give an example of non-legal tender money.

  • Cheque is an example of non-legal tender money.

5. What is representative paper money?

  • Representative paper money is paper money that represents a certain amount of metallic money, such as gold or silver, kept with the issuing authority.

6. What is a bank?

  • A bank is a financial institution that accepts deposits from people and gives loans to those who need money.

7. In which year was the Reserve Bank of India set up?

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was set up in 1935.

8. What is current deposit?

  • A current deposit is a bank deposit from which money can be withdrawn whenever required, usually through cheques.

Short Answer Type Questions

1. How does the lack of double coincidence of wants create problems in the barter system?

  • In barter, two people must want each other's goods at the same time.
  • This is called double coincidence of wants.
  • For example, a person having rice must find someone who has clothes and also wants rice.
  • If the second person does not want rice, the exchange cannot take place.
  • Therefore, barter makes exchange difficult and time-consuming.

2. What is meant by store of value?

  • Store of value means that money can be kept and used in the future.
  • We can save money today and use it later to buy goods and services.
  • Thus, money helps us save our purchasing power.

Example: If you save ₹1,000 today, you can use it later to buy something.

3. Which characteristic of money is the most important one and why?

  • The most important characteristic of money is general acceptability.
  • Money must be accepted by people in exchange for goods and services.
  • If people do not accept something as payment, it cannot function properly as money.

4. What is liquidity of money?

  • Liquidity means how easily an asset can be converted into money and used for payment.
  • Money has the highest liquidity because it can be directly used to buy goods and services.
  • For example, cash can be used immediately to purchase goods.

5. 'Money is the common unit of measurement of the value of goods and services.' Explain.

  • Money provides a common measure of value.
  • The value of different goods and services can be expressed in terms of money.
  • For example: A book = ₹200; A pen = ₹20; A bag = ₹800.
  • Therefore, money makes it easy to compare the values of different goods and services.

7. What are the functions of the Regional Rural Banks?

Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) mainly:

  1. Provide loans to farmers.
  2. Provide credit to small and marginal farmers.
  3. Help agricultural development.
  4. Provide loans to small businesses and rural workers.
  5. Encourage saving in rural areas.

Long Answer Type Questions

1. Explain four demerits of the barter system.

The barter system has several disadvantages:

1. Lack of double coincidence of wants

  • Both persons must want each other's goods.
  • This is difficult to find.

2. Lack of common measure of value

  • There is no common unit to measure the value of different goods.
  • It becomes difficult to decide how much of one good should be exchanged for another.

3. Difficulty in storing wealth

  • Some goods cannot be stored for a long time.
  • They may spoil or lose their value.

4. Difficulty in making future payments

  • It is difficult to make loans or payments in the future using goods.
  • The value of goods may change with time.

Therefore, the barter system was inconvenient for large-scale economic activities.

2. Explain any four characteristics of money.

1. General acceptability

  • Money must be accepted by people as payment for goods and services.

2. Durability

  • Money should be strong enough to last for a long time.
  • It should not easily get damaged.

3. Portability

  • Money should be easy to carry from one place to another.

4. Divisibility

  • Money should be available in different denominations.
  • This makes small and large payments easy.

Other important characteristics: stability of value, recognisability and uniformity.

3. Explain four major functions of money.

1. Medium of exchange

  • Money is used to buy and sell goods and services.
  • It removes the difficulties of the barter system.

2. Measure of value

  • Money measures the value of goods and services.
  • Prices are expressed in terms of money.

3. Store of value

  • Money can be saved and used in the future.
  • It allows people to preserve their purchasing power.

4. Standard of deferred payment

  • Money makes future payments easy.
  • Loans and debts can be expressed in terms of money.

4. Is cheque money? Give reasons for your answer.

  • A cheque itself is not money.
  • It is an instrument used to make payments.
  • A cheque gives an instruction to a bank to transfer money from one person's bank account to another.
  • A cheque is not generally accepted as legal tender.
  • However, bank deposits against which cheques can be drawn are considered part of the money supply.

Therefore: A cheque is a means of payment, but it is not money itself.

5. Mention four problems associated with money.

1. Inflation

  • Excessive increase in money and demand can contribute to a rise in prices.
  • This reduces the purchasing power of money.

2. Black money

  • Some people hide income from the government to avoid taxes.
  • This creates black money.

3. Counterfeit money

  • Fake currency can be produced illegally.
  • It can create problems in the monetary system.

4. Unequal distribution of wealth

  • Money and wealth may become concentrated in the hands of a small number of people.
  • This can increase economic inequality.

6. Explain any four functions of the Central Bank.

In India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is the Central Bank.

1. Issue of currency

  • RBI has the main authority to issue and manage currency notes in India.

2. Banker to the government

  • RBI keeps government accounts.
  • It manages many banking transactions of the government.

3. Banker's bank

  • Commercial banks keep reserves with RBI.
  • RBI provides financial assistance to banks when necessary.

4. Controller of credit

  • RBI controls the supply of credit and money in the economy.
  • It uses monetary policy tools to control credit.

Other functions: management of foreign exchange, regulation of banks and maintenance of financial stability.

7. Explain any two major functions of the commercial banks.

1. Accepting deposits

Commercial banks accept different types of deposits from people, such as:

  • Savings deposits
  • Current deposits
  • Fixed deposits

2. Providing loans

  • Banks lend money to individuals, businesses and other organisations.
  • They charge interest on loans.
  • Loans help people and businesses meet their financial needs.

8. Briefly explain any two functions of each of the following:

(i) IDBI

IDBI – Industrial Development Bank of India

  1. It provided financial assistance to industries.
  2. It supported industrial development and investment.

(ii) RRBs

RRBs – Regional Rural Banks

  1. They provide loans to farmers and rural people.
  2. They support agriculture and rural economic activities.

(iii) NABARD

NABARD – National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development

  1. It provides and supports credit for agriculture and rural development.
  2. It helps in the development of rural areas and rural financial institutions.

(iv) SIDBI

SIDBI – Small Industries Development Bank of India

  1. It provides financial assistance to small and medium enterprises.
  2. It promotes the development and growth of small-scale industries.
