Environment and Environmental Problems

Exercise

1. Write in your own words the meaning of environment.

  • Environment means everything that surrounds us.
  • It includes living things and non-living things.
  • Air, water, land, plants, animals and human beings are parts of the environment.
  • All these things are connected with each other.

2. Mention some of the biotic and abiotic elements of environment.

Biotic elements: These are living things.

  • Human beings
  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Birds
  • Bacteria

Abiotic elements: These are non-living things.

  • Air
  • Water
  • Soil
  • Sunlight
  • Rocks
  • Temperature

3. Write the meaning of lithosphere.

  • The lithosphere is the solid outer part of the Earth.
  • It includes the land, rocks and soil.
  • It forms the surface on which humans, plants and animals live.

4. What are the features included in the hydrosphere?

The hydrosphere means all the water found on Earth.

  1. Oceans and seas
  2. Rivers
  3. Lakes
  4. Ponds
  5. Groundwater
  6. Glaciers and ice
  7. Water vapour in the atmosphere

5. Write about the composition of the atmosphere.

  • The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding the Earth.
  • Nitrogen – about 78%
  • Oxygen – about 21%
  • Other gases – about 1%
  • Other gases include carbon dioxide, argon and other gases.
  • The atmosphere also contains water vapour and dust particles.

6. Give an outline of the extent of the biosphere.

  • The biosphere is the part of Earth where life exists.
  • It includes parts of the land, water and atmosphere.
  • Plants, animals, humans and microorganisms live in the biosphere.
  • Life exists from the lower part of the atmosphere to the upper part of the oceans and underground areas.
  • Thus, the biosphere is the life-supporting zone of Earth.

7. Discuss briefly the relation among the four major components of environment.

The four major components are:

  1. Lithosphere – land and rocks
  2. Hydrosphere – water
  3. Atmosphere – air
  4. Biosphere – living things

They are closely connected.

  • Plants grow on the lithosphere.
  • Plants need water from the hydrosphere.
  • Plants need air from the atmosphere.
  • Plants and animals are part of the biosphere.
  • A change in one component can affect the others.

8. Mention the major causes of environmental change.

  1. Natural changes such as earthquakes, floods and volcanic eruptions.
  2. Population growth.
  3. Deforestation.
  4. Growth of industries.
  5. Urbanisation.
  6. Mining and overuse of resources.
  7. Increase in vehicles and transport.
  8. Pollution caused by human activities.

9. Explain the meaning of environmental problem.

  • An environmental problem is a harmful change in the environment.
  • It can harm humans, animals, plants and natural resources.
  • Examples include air pollution, water pollution, deforestation, global warming and desertification.

10. Write geographical divisions of the environmental problems.

Environmental problems can be divided into three geographical levels:

  1. Local environmental problems
  2. Regional environmental problems
  3. Global environmental problems

11. What is a local environmental problem? Give examples.

  • A local environmental problem affects a small area or a particular place.

Examples:

  • Pollution of a local river.
  • Garbage problem in a city.
  • Noise pollution near a busy road.
  • Water shortage in a particular village.

12. Give two examples of regional environmental problem.

Two examples are:

  1. Floods affecting a large region.
  2. Deforestation over a large area.

Other examples include regional air pollution and soil erosion.

13. Which problems are considered as global environmental problem?

Problems that affect many countries or the whole world are called global environmental problems.

Examples:

  1. Global warming
  2. Climate change
  3. Ozone layer depletion
  4. Loss of biodiversity
  5. Desertification

14. Mention the major environmental problems of the world.

  1. Air pollution
  2. Water pollution
  3. Land pollution
  4. Deforestation
  5. Desertification
  6. Global warming
  7. Climate change
  8. Loss of biodiversity
  9. Ozone layer depletion
  10. Depletion of natural resources

15. Give the meaning of the term pollution.

  • Pollution means the addition of harmful substances to the environment.
  • These harmful substances are called pollutants.
  • Pollution makes air, water or land unsafe and unhealthy.

16. What is water pollution? How does it occur?

  • Water pollution means making water dirty or harmful by adding unwanted substances.

It occurs due to:

  1. Industrial waste entering rivers.
  2. Sewage being discharged into water.
  3. Agricultural chemicals entering rivers and lakes.
  4. Throwing garbage into water bodies.
  5. Oil spills.
  6. Harmful chemicals and plastics entering water.

17. Write a short note on land pollution.

  • Land pollution means the pollution or degradation of land.
  • It is mainly caused by plastic waste, industrial waste, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, garbage and mining.
  • Land pollution reduces soil quality.
  • It can also harm plants, animals and humans.

18. Write how air is polluted.

Air is polluted when harmful gases and particles enter the atmosphere.

  1. Smoke from factories.
  2. Smoke from vehicles.
  3. Burning of coal, petrol and other fuels.
  4. Burning of garbage and forests.
  5. Dust from construction and mining.
  6. Harmful gases released by industries.

19. What is a desert? Give the definition of desertification.

Desert: A desert is a very dry area where rainfall is very low. It has very little vegetation.

Desertification: Desertification is the process by which fertile land becomes dry, less productive and desert-like. It is mainly caused by human activities and climatic conditions.

20. Mention the causes of desertification.

  1. Deforestation
  2. Overgrazing
  3. Excessive farming
  4. Soil erosion
  5. Improper irrigation
  6. Drought
  7. Climate change
  8. Overuse of water
  9. Mining and other human activities

21. Is it possible to control desertification?

Yes, desertification can be controlled.

  1. Planting more trees.
  2. Controlling overgrazing.
  3. Using proper farming methods.
  4. Preventing soil erosion.
  5. Using water carefully.
  6. Developing proper irrigation systems.
  7. Protecting forests and grasslands.
  8. Creating awareness among people.

22. What is global warming? Mention its causes.

Global warming means the gradual increase in the average temperature of the Earth.

Causes:

  1. Burning of coal, petrol and natural gas.
  2. Deforestation.
  3. Smoke from industries and vehicles.
  4. Increase in greenhouse gases.
  5. Excessive use of fossil fuels.
  6. Some agricultural and industrial activities.

23. What are the probable consequences of global warming?

  1. Increase in Earth's temperature.
  2. Melting of glaciers and ice.
  3. Rise in sea level.
  4. Changes in rainfall.
  5. More floods and droughts.
  6. More heat waves.
  7. Damage to agriculture.
  8. Loss of wildlife and biodiversity.
  9. Changes in climate.
  10. Problems for human health and life.

24. Write how to control global warming.

  1. Reducing the use of fossil fuels.
  2. Using solar and wind energy.
  3. Planting more trees.
  4. Preventing deforestation.
  5. Using public transport.
  6. Saving electricity.
  7. Reducing pollution from industries and vehicles.
  8. Using energy-efficient machines.
  9. Following the principles of Reduce, Reuse and Recycle.
  10. Creating awareness about climate change.

25. Is there any environmental problem in your locality? If yes, then explain their causes and indicate some measures for their solution.

Yes, there are environmental problems in my locality.

Problems:

  • Garbage and plastic waste are common problems.
  • There can also be air and noise pollution because of vehicles.

Causes:

  1. People throw garbage in open places.
  2. Plastic bags are used too much.
  3. Waste is not properly collected or separated.
  4. The number of vehicles is increasing.
  5. People sometimes burn waste.

Solutions:

  1. Garbage should be collected regularly.
  2. Plastic use should be reduced.
  3. Waste should be separated into wet and dry waste.
  4. More trees should be planted.
  5. Public transport should be encouraged.
  6. People should be made aware of environmental protection.
