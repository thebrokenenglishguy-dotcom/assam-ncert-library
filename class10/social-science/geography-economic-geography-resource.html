Exercise

1. What is Economic Geography? What is its main subject matter? Mention the important branches of Economic Geography.

Economic Geography is the branch of Geography that studies economic activities of people and their distribution in different places.

Main subject matter:

  1. Production of goods
  2. Distribution of goods
  3. Exchange of goods
  4. Consumption of goods
  5. Use of resources

Important branches:

  1. Resource Geography
  2. Agricultural Geography
  3. Industrial Geography
  4. Transport Geography
  5. Trade Geography
  6. Population and Labour Geography

2. Write in brief about the scope of Economic Geography.

The scope of Economic Geography is very wide. It studies:

  1. Natural resources like land, water, forests and minerals.
  2. Agriculture and different types of farming.
  3. Industries and their location.
  4. Transport and communication.
  5. Trade and commerce.
  6. Distribution and use of resources.
  7. The relationship between people, resources and economic activities.

3. What do you mean by economic activities of man? What are such economic activities?

Economic activities are activities done by people to earn income and satisfy their needs.

  1. Agriculture
  2. Fishing
  3. Mining
  4. Manufacturing
  5. Trade
  6. Transport
  7. Banking
  8. Services

4. Write briefly about the subject matter of the important branches of Economic Geography.

  1. Resource Geography – studies resources, their distribution and use.
  2. Agricultural Geography – studies agriculture and farming activities.
  3. Industrial Geography – studies industries and their location.
  4. Transport Geography – studies transport systems and their distribution.
  5. Trade Geography – studies buying and selling of goods between different places.
  6. Population Geography – studies population and its relationship with economic activities.

5. Why is 'Resource Geography' known as an important branch of Economic Geography?

  1. Resources are necessary for human life.
  2. All economic activities depend on resources.
  3. It studies the distribution of resources.
  4. It studies how resources are used and conserved.
  5. It helps us understand the relationship between people and resources.
  6. Proper use of resources is important for economic development.

6. What is meant by 'Resource'? Mention its main characteristics.

A resource is anything that is useful to humans and helps them satisfy their needs.

  1. It must have utility or usefulness.
  2. It should be useful for human needs.
  3. Its value may change with time.
  4. Resources are not equally distributed everywhere.
  5. Resources can be natural or man-made.
  6. Some resources can be renewed, while others cannot.

7. 'Resource is Dynamic.' – Explain.

Resource is called dynamic because its value and usefulness can change with time.

  1. A thing may not be considered a resource in the past.
  2. New technology can make it useful.
  3. Human needs also change with time.
  4. Knowledge can help us discover new uses of things.
  5. For example, uranium became an important resource after the development of nuclear technology.
  6. Therefore, the meaning and importance of resources change with time.

8. Briefly discuss about the necessities of resources with examples.

  1. They help us satisfy our basic needs.
  2. We need resources for food, clothing and shelter.
  3. Industries need resources for production.
  4. Agriculture needs land and water.
  5. Transport needs fuel and minerals.
  6. Resources help in the economic development of a country.

Example: Coal and petroleum are used as sources of energy, while land and water are needed for agriculture.

9. Write in brief about the relationship between resource and man.

  • Humans and resources are closely related.
  • Humans use resources to satisfy their needs.
  • Humans also discover new resources and new uses.
  • Technology helps people use resources more effectively.
  • At the same time, humans must protect and conserve resources.
  • Therefore, there is a close relationship between human beings and resources.

10. Briefly discuss about the relationship between resource and science-technology.

  1. Technology helps us find new resources.
  2. It helps us use resources more efficiently.
  3. It can change a useless thing into a useful resource.
  4. Modern machines help in mining and agriculture.
  5. New technology also helps in recycling resources.
  6. Therefore, the development of science and technology increases the use and value of resources.

11. Write about classification of resources with examples.

1. Natural resources: Resources obtained from nature. Examples: water, forest, coal, minerals.

2. Man-made resources: Resources created by humans. Examples: roads, buildings, machines, irrigation canals.

3. Human resources: People and their knowledge, skills and abilities. Examples: teachers, doctors, engineers.

Resources can also be classified as renewable, non-renewable, biotic and abiotic.

12. What is meant by natural resource? Write briefly with examples.

Natural resources are resources that are obtained directly from nature. Humans do not create them.

Examples: water, air, forests, soil, minerals, wildlife, coal and petroleum.

13. What is man-made resource? Write in brief about its use with examples.

Man-made resources are resources created by humans using natural resources and technology.

Examples: roads, buildings, bridges, machines, irrigation canals and factories.

Uses:

  • They make our life easier.
  • They help in agriculture and industries.
  • They improve transport and communication.
  • They help in the economic development of a country.

15. What is meant by 'Conservation of Resources'? What is its necessity?

Conservation of Resources means protecting resources and using them carefully.

  1. Resources are limited.
  2. Some resources may become exhausted.
  3. Future generations will also need resources.
  4. It helps prevent wastage.
  5. It protects the environment.
  6. It supports sustainable development.

16. Write in brief about the methods of resource conservation.

  1. Reduce unnecessary use of resources.
  2. Reuse things whenever possible.
  3. Recycle waste materials.
  4. Use resources carefully and efficiently.
  5. Use renewable resources more.
  6. Protect forests and wildlife.
  7. Use modern technology to reduce wastage.
  8. Spread awareness about resource conservation.

17. Write in brief about the organizations associated with resource conservation and their role.

1. IUCN – International Union for Conservation of Nature. It works for the protection of nature and wildlife and prepares the Red List of threatened species.

2. WWF – World Wide Fund for Nature. It works to protect wildlife and the environment.

3. UNEP – United Nations Environment Programme. It works for environmental protection and sustainable development.

4. UNESCO – It works to protect important natural and cultural heritage sites.

18. Write short notes

(a) Resource

  • A resource is anything that is useful to humans.
  • It helps us satisfy our needs.
  • Resources may be natural, human or man-made.
  • Examples: water, land, forests, minerals and machines.

(b) Human Resource

  • Human resources mean people and their skills, knowledge and abilities.
  • People are important for the use and development of other resources.
  • Examples: doctors, teachers, engineers and workers.

(c) Wealth

  • Wealth means things that have economic value.
  • Wealth can be owned and used to earn income.
  • Examples include land, buildings, money and valuable goods.

(d) Renewable Resource

  • Renewable resources can be replaced or renewed naturally.
  • They can be used repeatedly if properly managed.
  • Examples: sunlight, wind, water and forests.

(e) Personal Resource

  • Personal resources are resources that belong to an individual person.
  • Examples: personal land, house, money and vehicle.

(f) National Resource

  • National resources belong to the country or nation.
  • They are used for the development of the country.
  • Examples: government forests, rivers, minerals and roads.

(g) Biotic Resource

  • Biotic resources come from living things.
  • Examples: plants, animals, forests and fish.

(h) Resource Conservation

  • Resource conservation means protecting and using resources carefully.
  • It prevents wastage.
  • It helps save resources for future generations.

(i) Resource Recycling

  • Resource recycling means processing used materials and using them again.
  • It reduces waste.
  • It saves natural resources.
  • Example: recycling paper, glass and metals.

(j) IUCN

  • IUCN stands for International Union for Conservation of Nature.
  • It works for the conservation of nature and wildlife.
  • It also prepares the Red List of threatened species.
