Very Short Answer type Questions

1. What was the eastern border of the Indus Valley civilization extended up to Ganga Valley?

The eastern border of the Indus Valley Civilization extended up to Meerut.

2. When was the town culture developed during the Indus Valley civilization?

The town culture developed around 4000 B.C.

3. What was the presumed time of writing of the Rig Veda?

The Rig Veda was probably written between 1500 B.C. and 1000 B.C.

4. The term Satyameva Jayate in the National symbol was taken from which book?

It was taken from the Mundaka Upanishad.

5. Which book did establish the term 'unity in diversity'?

The term was made popular by Jawaharlal Nehru's book The Discovery of India.

6. Greek-Roman techniques were used in which style of sculptures of India?

Greek-Roman techniques were used in the Gandhara style of sculpture.

7. What is the name of the famous book on political science written in ancient India?

The book is Arthashastra, written by Kautilya.

8. When was the Natyashastra by Bharat Muni written?

The Natyashastra was written between about 200 B.C. and the 2nd century A.D.

9. How many slokas are there in the Natyashastra?

There are about 600 slokas in the Natyashastra.

10. What is Heraka movement?

The Heraka Movement was started by Rani Gaidinliu. It aimed to protect and revive the traditional religion and culture of the Naga people.

11. Hornbill festival of Nagaland is based on which name of organism?

It is named after the Hornbill bird.

12. The traditional festival Chapchar Kut belongs to which state?

Mizoram.

13. What is the meaning of the word Mizo?

Mizo means people or inhabitants of the hills.

14. Which state of the North East has a matriarchal society?

Meghalaya.

15. What is the main theme of Manipuri dance?

The main themes are related to Lord Krishna, Radha and Vaishnavite religious stories.

16. What is the ancient name of Assam?

The ancient names of Assam were Pragjyotishpur and Kamrup.

17. From which Bodo word the name Assam is considered to be derived?

The name Assam is considered to be derived from the Bodo word Ha-chom.

18. What is Charyapada?

Charyapada is a collection of old songs, written between the 10th and 14th centuries. They are an important part of early Assamese literature.

19. When was the Charyapada written?

Charyapada was written between the 10th and 14th centuries A.D.

20. Under whose patronage did Madhab Kandali translate the Saptakanda Ramayana?

Madhab Kandali translated it under the patronage of Barahi King Mahamanikya.

21. Who was the first to introduce prose literature in Assamese?

Bhattadeva was the first to introduce prose literature in Assamese.

22. Who composed Zikir and Zari?

Ajan Peer, also known as Shah Milan, composed Zikir and Zari.

23. Who is Ajan Peer?

Ajan Peer was a Muslim saint and religious poet. His other name was Shah Milan. He is famous for composing Zikir and Zari.

24. When did Ajan Peer come to Assam?

Ajan Peer came to Assam during the reign of Ahom King Gadadhar Singha.

25. When was the stone gate of Da-Parbatia made?

The stone gate of Da-Parbatia was made in the 6th century A.D.

26. What was the duty of the Changrung Phukan?

Changrung Phukan supervised the construction and measurement of roads, palaces, temples and houses.

27. Which artist got Padmashree award for the contribution in folk song?

Pratima Pande Baruah received the Padma Shri for her contribution to folk songs.

28. What is the name of the first news magazine of Assam?

The first news magazine of Assam was Arunodoi. It was first published in 1846.

29. Which tribe of North East is a part of the Austric ethnic group?

The Khasi tribe of Meghalaya belongs to the Austric ethnic group.

30. Name the writers of the books.

Arthashastra — Kautilya
Hasthividyarnava — Sukumar Borkaith
Kirtan — Sankardeva
Naamghosa — Madhabdeva
Chordhora aru Pimpara Guchowa — Madhabdeva
Kaliya Daman — Sankardeva
Ramvijoy — Sankardeva
Katha Geeta — Bhattadeva
Rajatarangini — Kalhan
Bharat Sambhed — Jawaharlal Nehru.

  2. Huns

4. How does the geography of India play a role in creating cultural diversity of India?

  1. India is a very large country with mountains, plains, deserts, forests and coastal areas.
  2. Different regions have different climates and living conditions.
  3. Because of this, people developed different languages, food, clothes and customs.
  4. Therefore, India's geography helped create its cultural diversity.

6. What is written about religious tolerance in the 12th main stone carvings of Ashoka?

  1. People should not consider their own religion better than others.
  2. People should respect other religions.
  3. Ashoka supported peace, tolerance and unity.

7. What is multiculturalism?

Multiculturalism means the existence of different cultures in the same society. Different groups can keep their own language, religion, customs and traditions while living together with respect and understanding.

8. What are the divisions in the ancient Indian culture?

  1. Religious culture — religion, worship, festivals and customs.
  2. Social culture — food, dress, language, lifestyle and social customs.
  3. Art and cultural traditions — paintings, sculptures, music and other forms of art.

Long answer type Questions

1. Write briefly on the source and development of multi-culturalism in India.

  1. India has been a multicultural country since ancient times.
  2. Different groups brought their own languages, religions, food, dress and customs.
  3. India's large size and different geographical regions also helped create different cultures.
  4. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam and Christianity became part of Indian society.
  5. Different communities lived together and exchanged ideas and traditions.
  6. The Ramayana and Mahabharata helped connect different parts of India.
  7. Different rulers encouraged religious tolerance.
  8. Cultural exchange brought communities closer.
  9. This created the idea of Unity in Diversity.
  10. India's multiculturalism developed through the mixing and sharing of many cultures.

2. The soul of Indian culture and civilization is regarded to be existed in the Sanskrit literature of ancient India – discuss the meaning of the sentence.

  1. Sanskrit literature contains a large part of the knowledge and ideas of ancient India.
  2. Important works include the Vedas, Upanishads, Ramayana, Mahabharata and Puranas.
  3. Arthashastra gives information about politics and administration.
  4. Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita give information about medicine.
  5. Works of Aryabhata and Brahmagupta show India's knowledge of science and mathematics.
  6. Kapila and Patanjali show India's philosophical ideas.
  7. These books tell us about religion, society, science, medicine, politics and values.
  8. Therefore, Sanskrit literature gives us a clear picture of the life and culture of ancient India.

3. Write about religious traditions in India.

  1. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism developed in India.
  2. Later, Islam, Christianity and Judaism also became part of Indian society.
  3. Different gods, goddesses and methods of worship developed in different regions.
  4. Indian culture generally accepted tolerance and peaceful living.
  5. Emperor Ashoka strongly supported religious tolerance and respect for other religions.
  6. Different religions influenced Indian art, literature, music, dance, architecture and festivals.
  7. Thus, India developed both religious diversity and religious unity.

4. Write how the common and separate identity of the North-East is developed.

  1. The North-East has many tribes and communities, each with its own language, dress, food, festivals and customs.
  2. Many communities share common traditions and have exchanged culture and ideas.
  3. Hinduism, Christianity, Buddhism and traditional tribal religions are followed in different areas.
  4. Festivals, music, dance and food have influenced one another.
  5. The region has developed a shared North-Eastern identity.
  6. At the same time, each tribe continues to protect its own special cultural identity.

5. Write briefly about the religious environment in the North-East.

  1. The North-East has many religions and traditional beliefs.
  2. Hinduism has existed in places like Manipur and Tripura for a long time.
  3. Christianity spread widely in the hill areas during the British period.
  4. Many tribal communities still follow traditional religions.
  5. Some communities in Arunachal Pradesh worship Donyi-Polo, the Sun and Moon.
  6. Many people in Manipur follow Vaishnavism.
  7. The Hornbill Festival of Nagaland shows traditional Naga culture.
  8. The Heraka Movement tried to protect traditional Naga religious beliefs.
  9. Meghalaya has a large Christian population.
  10. Thus, the North-East has a rich mixture of different religions and traditional beliefs.

6. Assam has a mixed culture due to assimilation of Aryan and non-Aryan cultures — discuss briefly this aspect.

  1. Both Aryan and non-Aryan communities lived in Assam.
  2. Assam has many tribes with different languages and customs.
  3. Each tribe had its own food, dress, festivals and traditions.
  4. Over time, these communities came into contact with one another.
  5. Their languages and traditions influenced each other.
  6. Assamese language accepted words from different tribal languages.
  7. Assamese culture adopted many tribal customs, festivals, music and traditions.
  8. Therefore, Assamese culture is a result of the mixing of Aryan and non-Aryan cultures.
  9. This mixture made Assam's culture rich and diverse.

7. Write briefly on the heritage of paintings in Assam.

  1. Assam has a rich tradition of manuscript painting, mainly developed during the medieval period.
  2. The Satras helped in the development of painting.
  3. Many books were written and decorated with paintings on Sanchipat.
  4. Important illustrated books include Hasthividyarnava, Ananda Lahari, Chitra Bhagawat, Geet Govinda and Kumar Haran.
  5. These paintings often showed religious stories and local life.
  6. They are an important part of the cultural heritage of Assam.

8. Write about religious unity and diversity in Assam.

  1. Most people of Assam follow Hinduism, while Islam, Christianity, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism are also followed.
  2. Hinduism in Assam includes Shakta, Shaiva, Vaishnava and Saura traditions.
  3. Kamakhya Temple is an important Shakta centre.
  4. The Neo-Vaishnavite movement spread Vaishnavism in Assam.
  5. Poa Mecca at Hajo is an important Muslim religious place.
  6. Ajan Peer composed the famous religious songs Zikir and Zari.
  7. Some communities in eastern Assam follow Buddhism.
  8. The Ahom rulers generally followed a tolerant and secular approach.
  9. Therefore, people of different religions have lived together and contributed to Assam's culture.

9. Why are cultural diversity, assimilation and change taking place more rapidly in Assam than in the other states of the region?

  1. Assam has fertile land and rich natural resources.
  2. Many different communities came to Assam for trade, travel, pilgrimage and settlement.
  3. Assam was connected with other regions through important trade routes.
  4. Many people who came to Assam later settled here.
  5. Assam has many tribes and communities.
  6. These communities exchanged their languages, customs and traditions.
  7. Aryan and non-Aryan cultures mixed together.
  8. This created a mixed Assamese culture.
  9. Therefore, Assam has a very rich and changing cultural heritage.

10. Really the society is like a book for studying the folk culture — provide your views on this issue.

  1. Yes, society can be called a book for studying folk culture.
  2. Folk culture is mainly learned by watching and practising, and is not always written in books.
  3. We can understand folk culture through people's songs, dances and festivals.
  4. Food, dress, houses and ornaments also tell us about a community.
  5. Traditional farming, fishing and use of bamboo and cane are also part of folk culture.
  6. Different tribes have their own languages and traditions.
  7. By observing society, we can understand how traditions are passed from one generation to another.
  8. Therefore, society itself is an important source for studying folk culture.

Write short notes on

1. Sculptures of ancient India

  • Ancient India had a rich tradition of sculpture.
  • Sculptures were made from stone, bronze, copper and other materials.
  • Common subjects were Buddha, Mahavira, Hindu gods and goddesses and animals.
  • The three important styles were Gandhara, Mathura and Amaravati.
  • Gandhara sculpture was influenced by Greek-Roman art.
  • Ancient Indian sculptures show religious beliefs and artistic skill.

2. Indian paintings

  • Indian painting has a very old history.
  • Famous ancient wall paintings are found at Ajanta, Bagh and Chittanavassal.
  • Many paintings show Buddha, Mahavira, Hindu gods and scenes from religious stories.
  • During the Mughal period, small paintings became very popular.
  • Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan supported painters.
  • Books such as Jahangirnama and Padshahnama contain famous paintings.

3. Traditions of songs and dances in India

  • India has a very old tradition of music and dance.
  • The Natyashastra gives important information about Indian dance.
  • Indian dance uses body movements, gestures and facial expressions.
  • Indian dances are mainly classical and folk.
  • Indian music uses instruments such as sitar, tabla, flute, shehnai and veena.
  • Folk music uses dhol, taal, khol and nagara.
  • Songs and dances often tell stories from religion, mythology and daily life.

4. Sculptures of Assam

  • Assam has many beautiful stone and metal sculptures.
  • Many sculptures are found in and around temples.
  • Common subjects include Shiva, Vishnu, Ganesha and Surya, as well as lions, elephants and horses.
  • Important places include Da-Parbatia, Bamuni Pahar, Madan Kamdev and Surya Pahar.
  • Da-Parbatia sculptures belong to the 6th century and show Gupta influence.

5. Architectures of Assam

  • Assam has a rich tradition of architecture.
  • Many important buildings were built during the Ahom period.
  • Famous examples include Rang Ghar, Talatal Ghar and Kareng Ghar.
  • Many temples were also built in Assam.
  • Rang Ghar was used for watching festivals and cultural activities.
  • Talatal Ghar was a large multi-storeyed building.
  • Namdang Bridge is an example of an Ahom-period stone bridge.
  • Changrung Phukan supervised construction and measurement work.

6. Vaishnavite literature of Assam

  • Vaishnavite literature grew mainly because of Sankardeva and Madhabdeva.
  • They started the Neo-Vaishnavite movement in Assam.
  • Sankardeva wrote Kirtan and many Ankiya Naat plays.
  • Madhabdeva wrote Naamghosa.
  • They also composed many Borgeets.
  • Their works were based mainly on Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas and religious ideas.
  • Important works include Kaliya Daman, Ram Bijay, Kirtan and Naamghosa.
  • Their literature greatly influenced Assamese language and culture.

7. Folk songs of Assam

  • Assam has a very rich tradition of folk songs.
  • Bihu songs are among the most popular folk songs.
  • Folk songs are sung during festivals, marriage, harvesting and community gatherings.
  • They talk about love, nature, farming and daily life.
  • Different communities have their own songs, such as Bodo, Karbi and Mising songs.
  • Common instruments include dhol, pepa, gogona and toka.
  • Folk songs are an important part of Assamese culture.

8. Religious unity in Assam

  • Assam is home to people following many religions.
  • Hinduism is followed by the majority; Islam, Christianity, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism are also followed.
  • Kamakhya Temple is an important Hindu religious centre.
  • Poa Mecca at Hajo is an important Muslim religious place.
  • Ajan Peer gave Assam the religious songs Zikir and Zari.
  • Some communities follow Buddhism and traditional tribal religions.
  • The Ahom rulers supported different religious traditions.
  • Thus, Assam has a strong tradition of religious unity and tolerance.

9. Historical literature of Assam

  • Assam has a rich tradition of historical literature.
  • During the Ahom period, historical records called Buranjis were written.
  • They recorded the history of the Ahom kingdom, including kings, wars, administration, society, foreign relations, customs and traditions.
  • There were also Buranjis about other communities and neighbouring kingdoms.
  • These writings are very important for understanding the history of Assam.

10. Bihu festival

  • Bihu is the most important festival of Assam.
  • Bohag Bihu / Rongali Bihu: celebrated in April; it marks the beginning of the agricultural season and includes Bihu songs and dances.
  • Kati Bihu: celebrated in October; people light lamps in fields and pray for a good harvest.
  • Magh Bihu / Bhogali Bihu: celebrated in January; it marks the end of harvesting and includes feasts, Meji and community celebrations.
  • Bihu represents the life, culture, agriculture and unity of the people of Assam.
