Partition of Bengal (1905-1911 A.D.) and Swadeshi Movement

Class 10 • Social Science • Chapter 1

EXERCISE

Very short answer questions

1. In which Viceroy’s time did the partition of Bengal take place?

Answer:
  • The partition of Bengal took place during the time of Lord Curzon.

2. On which date in 1905 did the partition of Bengal come into force?

Answer:
  • The partition of Bengal came into force on 16 October 1905.

3. In which meeting was the proposal of giving up foreign commodities accepted?

Answer:
  • The proposal was accepted at the second protest meeting at Dinajpur.
  • It was held on 20 July 1905.

4. Who was the editor of the journal Jugantar published in the time of Swadeshi movement?

Answer:
  • Bhupendra Nath Dutta was the editor of Jugantar.

5. When was the institution named National Education Council (Jatiya Siksha Parisad) established in Bengal?

Answer:
  • It was established on 11 March 1906.

6. Who was the main introducer of the industry named Bengal Chemicals?

Answer:
  • Acharya Prafulla Chandra Ray was the main person behind Bengal Chemicals.

7. Under whose presidentship did the Conference of Indian National Congress accept the proposal of swaraj for Indians?

Answer:
  • The proposal of Swaraj was accepted under the presidentship of Dadabhai Naoroji.
  • It was accepted at the Calcutta session in 1906.

8. When did the unification of Bengal take place?

Answer:
  • The unification of Bengal took place on 12 December 1911.

Short/ long answer type questions

1. Mention three main geographical regions of Bengal which were annexed to the province West Bengal and Assam created after the partition of Bengal.

Answer:

The three main regions were:

  1. Dhaka
  2. Chattagram
  3. Rajshahi

Note: The textbook question appears to have a wording mistake. It refers to the new province East Bengal and Assam.

2. Write briefly about what were the intentions of the partition of Bengal.

Answer:
  1. They wanted to weaken the growing national movement in Bengal.
  2. They wanted to break Hindu-Muslim unity.
  3. They wanted to make it difficult for Indians to fight against British rule together.
  4. They wanted to weaken the leaders opposing the British.
  5. They wanted to increase their control over Bengal.

3. Why was the anti partition of Bengal movement named as Swadeshi movement? Discuss briefly.

Answer:
  1. People decided to boycott foreign goods, especially British goods.
  2. They started using Indian-made goods instead.
  3. People wanted to make India self-dependent.
  4. The movement encouraged people to support Indian industries.
  5. People from different religions and communities joined the movement.

4. Discuss the contributions of Swadeshi movement towards national education.

Answer:
  1. People started leaving government schools and joining national schools.
  2. The National Education Council was established on 11 March 1906.
  3. Bengal National College was established in 1906.
  4. Bengal Technical Institute was also established.
  5. Many national schools were opened.
  6. Students were encouraged to receive education that promoted love for the country.

5. How did the national industries develop during Swadeshi movement? Explain in short.

Answer:
  1. People stopped buying many foreign goods.
  2. They started buying Indian-made goods.
  3. New industries producing cloth, soap, sugar and other goods were started.
  4. Bengal Chemical and Pharmaceutical Company was started with the help of Acharya Prafulla Chandra Ray.
  5. Swadeshi shops and stores were opened to sell Indian goods.
  6. Indian banks and insurance companies also started developing.

6. What were the anti-movement measures taken by the British to dominate Swadeshi movement?

Answer:
  1. They tried to divide Hindus and Muslims.
  2. They encouraged Muslims to stay away from the movement.
  3. The Muslim League was formed in 1906, which helped the British divide political groups.
  4. The British banned the slogan “Vande Mataram” in Bengal.
  5. Students were stopped from taking part in the movement.
  6. Public meetings were restricted or banned.
  7. Many students and activists were punished and tortured.

7. Mention three results of Swadeshi movement.

Answer:
  1. National education grew and many national schools were established.
  2. Women started taking part in the national movement.
  3. People became more politically aware and took a greater part in the freedom struggle.

8. Mention three contributions of Swadeshi movement.

Answer:
  1. It increased political awareness among Indians.
  2. It encouraged women to participate in the freedom movement.
  3. It encouraged the growth of Indian industries and Indian-made goods.
  4. It also encouraged some young people to join revolutionary activities.

Write short notes on:

1. Kabiguru Rabindranath Tagore

  • Rabindranath Tagore was a great Indian poet and writer.
  • He strongly supported the Swadeshi Movement.
  • On 16 October 1905, he encouraged people to celebrate Raksha Bandhan to show Hindu-Muslim unity.
  • He encouraged Hindus and Muslims to live together peacefully.
  • He also helped promote Swadeshi goods.
  • His writings and songs created love for the country.
  • He received the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1913.

2. Margaret Elizabeth Noble (Bhagini Nivedita)

  • Margaret Elizabeth Noble was also known as Bhagini Nivedita.
  • She was born in Ireland.
  • She became a close follower of Swami Vivekananda.
  • She came to India and worked mainly in the field of education.
  • She supported Indian nationalism.
  • She also supported the Swadeshi Movement.
  • She worked for the development of Indian society and education.

3. Nawab Salimullah

  • Nawab Salimullah was the Nawab of Dhaka.
  • He was an important Muslim leader during the Partition of Bengal.
  • He supported the Partition of Bengal.
  • He supported the interests of the Muslim community in Eastern Bengal.
  • He played an important role in the formation of the All-India Muslim League in 1906.
  • The Muslim League was formally established at a meeting in Dhaka in December 1906.
