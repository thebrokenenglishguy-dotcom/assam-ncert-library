Chapter 3 — The Midnight Visitor

Robert Arthur

A. Read and Find Out

Q1. How is Ausable different from other secret agents?
Answer: Ausable was very fat and looked like an ordinary person. He did not look like the smart, slim and mysterious secret agents Fowler had read about. However, he was intelligent, calm and clever.
Q2. Who is Fowler and what is his first authentic thrill of the day?
Answer: Fowler was a young writer who wanted to experience the exciting life of a secret agent. His first real thrill came when he saw Max standing in Ausable’s room with a pistol in his hand.
Q3. How has Max got in?
Answer: Max had entered Ausable’s room using a passkey. He claimed that he did not know about the balcony.
Q4. How does Ausable say he got in?
Answer: Ausable said that Max had entered through the balcony. He explained that the next room had once been part of a large apartment and had a balcony extending under his window. This was a clever lie.

B. Think About It

Q1. “Ausable did not fit any description of a secret agent Fowler had ever read.” What do secret agents in books and films look like? How is Ausable different?
Answer: Secret agents in books and films usually look smart, slim, strong and mysterious. They often wear stylish clothes and carry weapons. Ausable was very fat and looked ordinary. This difference surprised Fowler.
Q2. How does Ausable make Max believe that there is a balcony attached to his room? What makes the story convincing?
Answer: Ausable calmly describes a balcony that supposedly belongs to the next apartment and extends under his window. He explains that the rooms were once part of a large apartment. His detailed and confident explanation makes Max believe the story.
Q3. When do you think Ausable thought up his plan for getting rid of Max? Had he planned it earlier or did he use events as they happened?
Answer: Ausable thought of his plan when he saw Max standing in his room with a pistol. He had not planned everything beforehand. He quickly used the situation by inventing a story about the balcony and pretending that the police were coming.

C. Talk About It

Q1. Ausable shows great presence of mind. Give examples that show how he thinks quickly and acts calmly.
Answer: Ausable showed great presence of mind when he faced Max, who was holding a pistol. Instead of becoming frightened, he calmly invented a story about a balcony and the police. His clever thinking made Max jump out of the window. This shows that remaining calm during danger can help us solve difficult situations.
Q2. What would you do in the following situations?
Answer:
1. A small fire starts in your kitchen: I would alert my family, switch off the gas if safe, and use a suitable fire extinguisher. I would never throw water on an oil or electrical fire.

2. A child starts to choke: I would call for emergency medical help and give suitable first aid. I would not blindly put my fingers inside the child’s mouth.

3. An electrical appliance gives sparks: I would switch off the main electricity supply if safe, stay away and call an electrician.

4. A bicycle knocks down a pedestrian: I would help the injured person, call an ambulance if needed and inform the police.

5. It rains continuously for more than twenty-four hours: I would stay in a safe place, avoid flooded roads and follow weather warnings.

6. A family member does not return home on time: I would try to contact the person, inform other family members and contact the police if necessary.

D. Important Additional Questions

Q1. Who was Ausable?
Answer: Ausable was a secret agent who lived in a French hotel. He was very fat and did not look like a typical spy. However, he was intelligent and clever.
Q2. Why was Fowler disappointed after meeting Ausable?
Answer: Fowler expected a secret agent to be mysterious and adventurous. Ausable looked like an ordinary, fat man, so Fowler was disappointed.
Q3. Who was Max?
Answer: Max was another secret agent and an enemy of Ausable. He entered Ausable’s room with a pistol to steal an important report about new missiles.
Q4. Why had Max come to Ausable’s room?
Answer: Max had come to take an important report about new missiles before Ausable could receive it.
Q5. What was the important report about?
Answer: The report was about some new missiles. It was an important document that could affect the course of history.
Q6. How did Ausable react when he saw Max with a pistol?
Answer: Ausable remained calm and did not show fear. He spoke normally and quickly thought of a plan to get rid of Max.
Q7. Why did Ausable say that the police were coming?
Answer: Ausable said that the police were coming to frighten Max. He wanted Max to believe that he would be caught if he stayed in the room.
Q8. Why did Max jump out of the window?
Answer: Max believed that a balcony was outside the window. When he heard the knocking and thought the police had arrived, he became frightened and jumped out to escape.
Q9. Who was actually knocking at the door?
Answer: Henry, the waiter, was knocking at the door. He had brought the drinks Ausable had ordered.
Q10. Was there really a balcony outside Ausable’s window?
Answer: No, there was no balcony outside Ausable’s window. Ausable had invented the story to fool Max.
Q11. Was there really a police officer outside the door?
Answer: No, there was no police officer. The person knocking was Henry, the waiter.
Q12. How did Ausable save himself and Fowler from Max?
Answer: Ausable invented a story about a balcony and pretended that the police were coming. Max believed him and jumped out of the window. Ausable defeated him without using physical force.
Q13. Why was Fowler surprised at the end?
Answer: Fowler was surprised because Max had jumped out of the window believing that there was a balcony. He then discovered that there was no balcony and no police officer. Ausable had defeated Max through clever thinking.
Q14. What happened to Max at the end of the story?
Answer: Max jumped out of the window thinking that he would land on the balcony. However, there was no balcony, so he fell. The story does not describe what happened to him after that.
Q15. What is the main message of the story?
Answer: The story teaches us that intelligence and presence of mind can be more powerful than physical strength. A person can overcome danger by remaining calm and thinking wisely.
Q16. Why is the title “The Midnight Visitor” appropriate?
Answer: The title is appropriate because Max secretly entered Ausable’s room at night. He came as an unexpected visitor to steal an important report and created a dangerous situation.

E. Character Sketches

Q1. Write a character sketch of Ausable.
Answer: Ausable was a secret agent who lived in a French hotel. He was very fat and did not look like a typical spy. He was intelligent, calm and quick-witted. When Max entered his room with a pistol, Ausable did not panic. Instead, he invented a story about a balcony and the police. His clever plan made Max jump out of the window. Ausable proves that intelligence and presence of mind are more important than physical appearance.
Q2. Write a character sketch of Fowler.
Answer: Fowler was a young writer who wanted to experience the exciting life of a secret agent. At first, he was disappointed because Ausable did not look like a typical spy. When Max entered the room with a pistol, Fowler experienced his first real thrill. He was surprised by Ausable’s intelligence and cleverness.
Q3. Write a character sketch of Max.
Answer: Max was a secret agent who wanted to steal an important report about new missiles. He was slim and had a crafty-looking face. He entered Ausable’s room with a pistol. However, he believed Ausable’s false story about the balcony and jumped out of the window. His lack of careful thinking led to his downfall.

F. Vocabulary and Word Meanings

Espionage: Secret work of spying.
Musty: Having an unpleasant old smell.
Passkey: A key that opens a lock.
Authentic: Real or genuine.
Thrill: A feeling of excitement.
Disillusioned: Disappointed after discovering the truth.
Prosaic: Ordinary or unexciting.
Wheezily: Breathing with difficulty.
Slender: Thin or slim.
Crafty: Clever in a dishonest way.
Inflection: A change in the tone of the voice.
Commanding: Giving orders confidently.
Swiftly: Quickly.
Piercingly: In a sharp, high-pitched way.
Balcony: A platform outside an upper-floor room.
Mysterious: Difficult to understand or explain.
