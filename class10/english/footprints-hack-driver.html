Chapter 8 — The Hack Driver

Sinclair Lewis

← Back to Library ← Back to Footprints Without Feet

A. Read and Find Out

Q1. Why is the lawyer sent to New Mullion? What does he first think about the place?
Answer: The lawyer is sent to New Mullion to serve a summons on Oliver Lutkins, who is needed as a witness in a court case. At first, he expects the village to be beautiful and peaceful, but he is disappointed by its muddy streets and ordinary wooden shops.
Q2. Who befriends him? Where does he take him?
Answer: A friendly hack driver named Bill Magnuson befriends him. Bill takes the lawyer around the village to places such as Fritz’s shop, Gustaff’s barber shop, Gray’s barber shop and Lutkins’ mother’s farm in search of Oliver Lutkins.
Q3. What does he say about Lutkins?
Answer: Bill says that Lutkins is a clever and dishonest man who often plays poker and avoids paying his debts. He also says that Lutkins is difficult to catch because he is always moving from one place to another.
Q4. What more does Bill say about Lutkins and his family?
Answer: Bill says that Lutkins is very clever at avoiding people who want their money back. He also describes Lutkins’ mother as a strong, frightening and sharp-tongued woman who can be difficult to deal with.
Q5. Does the narrator serve the summons that day?
Answer: No, the narrator fails to serve the summons because he cannot find Oliver Lutkins. He later discovers that the hack driver himself was Oliver Lutkins.
Q6. Who is Lutkins?
Answer: Lutkins is actually Bill Magnuson, the friendly hack driver who takes the lawyer around the village. He hides his real identity and fools the lawyer throughout the day.

B. Think About It

Q1. When the lawyer reached New Mullion, did Bill know that he was looking for Lutkins? When do you think Bill came up with his plan for fooling the lawyer?
Answer: Yes, Bill knew from the beginning that the lawyer was looking for Oliver Lutkins. He probably planned to fool the lawyer as soon as he discovered that the lawyer did not know what Lutkins looked like. He then pretended to help him while hiding his real identity.
Q2. Lutkins openly takes the lawyer all over the village. How is it that no one lets out the secret? Can you find other such subtle ways in which Lutkins manipulates the lawyer?
Answer: The villagers know who Bill really is, but they do not reveal his identity because they are amused by the trick and play along with him. Lutkins also manipulates the lawyer by pretending to be helpful, taking him to places where he has supposedly just missed himself, making false claims about his debts and poker games, and describing his own mother as a frightening woman.
Q3. Why do you think Lutkins’ neighbours were anxious to meet the lawyer?
Answer: Lutkins had told his neighbours about the lawyer who was searching for him. They were curious and amused because they wanted to see the person whom Lutkins had fooled so cleverly.
Q4. After his first day’s experience with the hack driver, the lawyer thinks of returning to New Mullion to practise law. Do you think he would have reconsidered this idea after his second visit?
Answer: Yes, he would certainly have reconsidered his decision. During his second visit, he discovered that the friendly hack driver had fooled him completely. This experience would have made him realise that village life was not as simple and honest as he had imagined.
Q5. Do you think the lawyer was gullible? How could he have avoided being taken for a ride?
Answer: Yes, the lawyer was gullible because he trusted Bill without checking his identity. He could have avoided being fooled by asking other people about Lutkins, checking his appearance and verifying Bill’s identity before trusting him.

C. Talk About It

Q1. Do we come across persons like Lutkins only in fiction or do we encounter them in real life as well? Can you give examples from fiction, or narrate an incident that you have read in the newspaper, or an incident from real life?
Answer: We come across people like Lutkins in real life as well. Some people pretend to be helpful and trustworthy to cheat others. For example, fraudsters sometimes pretend to be bank officials and ask people for confidential information to steal their money.
Q2. Who is a con man, or a confidence trickster?
Answer: A con man or confidence trickster is a person who gains someone’s trust through lies and tricks them into giving away money or other valuable things.
