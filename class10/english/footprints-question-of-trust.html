Chapter 4 — A Question of Trust

Victor Canning

A. Read and Find Out

Q1. What does Horace Danby like to collect?
Answer: Horace Danby likes to collect rare and expensive books. He steals money every year to buy these books.
Q2. Why does he steal every year?
Answer: Horace steals every year because he needs money to buy rare and expensive books. He steals only once a year and takes enough money to last for twelve months.
Q3. Who is speaking to Horace Danby?
Answer: A young woman dressed in red is speaking to Horace. She pretends to be the owner of the house, but she is actually a thief.
Q4. Who is the real culprit in the story?
Answer: The young woman dressed in red is the real culprit. She tricks Horace into opening the safe and steals the jewels. She is also responsible for his arrest.

B. Think About It

Q1. Did you begin to suspect, before the end of the story, that the lady was not the person Horace Danby took her to be? If so, at what point did you realise it, and how?
Answer: Yes, I began to suspect the lady when she asked Horace to open the safe for her. She behaved like a clever thief and showed no fear. I realised that she was not the real owner because she easily fooled Horace and took the jewels.
Q2. What are the subtle ways in which the lady manages to deceive Horace Danby into thinking she is the lady of the house? Why does Horace not suspect that something is wrong?
Answer: The lady speaks confidently and behaves like the owner of the house. She knows about the dog, the rooms and the safe. She dresses well and acts naturally. Horace does not suspect her because he believes everything she says and is afraid of being caught.
Q3. “Horace Danby was good and respectable — but not completely honest.” Why is this description right? Why cannot he be categorised as a typical thief?
Answer: Horace was a good and respectable man in his daily life. He was polite and successful in his business. However, he stole money every year to buy expensive books. He was not a typical thief because he stole only once a year and did not steal to live a luxurious life.
Q4. Horace Danby was a meticulous planner but still he faltered. Where did he go wrong and why?
Answer: Horace went wrong by trusting the young woman without checking her identity. He removed his gloves while giving her a cigarette lighter and left his fingerprints on the safe. He was fooled by her clever acting and was eventually arrested.

C. Talk About It

Q1. Do you think Horace Danby was unfairly punished, or that he deserved what he got?
Answer: I think Horace deserved his punishment because stealing is a crime. Although he stole only to buy books, his reason did not make his actions right. Everyone must respect the law.
Q2. Do intentions justify actions? Would you do something wrong if you thought your ends justified the means? Are there situations in which it is excusable to act less than honestly?
Answer: No, good intentions cannot always justify wrong actions. A person should not break the law to achieve a personal goal. I would not steal or cheat even if I wanted something badly. Honesty and fairness are important in every situation.

D. Important Additional Questions

Q1. Who was Horace Danby?
Answer: Horace Danby was a fifty-year-old unmarried man. He was considered a good and respectable citizen. He owned a successful business and had two helpers. However, he was also a thief who robbed a safe every year.
Q2. What was Horace Danby’s hobby?
Answer: Horace’s hobby was collecting rare and expensive books. He loved reading and wanted to buy three interesting books that were coming up for sale.
Q3. How did Horace plan his robberies?
Answer: Horace carefully studied the house for two weeks. He examined its rooms, electric wiring, paths and garden. He chose a time when the family was away and planned everything before entering the house.
Q4. Why did Horace wear gloves while opening the safe?
Answer: Horace wore gloves to avoid leaving his fingerprints at the crime scene. He wanted to prevent the police from identifying him.
Q5. How did Horace enter the house at Shotover Grange?
Answer: Horace entered the house through the garden. He used the key that the housekeeper had left hanging outside the kitchen door.
Q6. How did Horace keep the dog quiet?
Answer: Horace called the dog by its name, Sherry, and spoke to it lovingly. This made the dog friendly and quiet.
Q7. Why did Horace sneeze inside the house?
Answer: Horace suffered from hay fever. The flowers inside the house caused him to sneeze repeatedly.
Q8. How did the young woman trick Horace?
Answer: The young woman pretended to be the owner of the house. She told Horace that she had forgotten the numbers needed to open the safe. She convinced him to open it and take out the jewels for her.
Q9. Why did Horace remove his gloves?
Answer: Horace removed his gloves to give the young woman his cigarette lighter. He wanted to please her so that she would allow him to escape.
Q10. What happened to Horace after the robbery?
Answer: Horace was arrested by the police for robbing Shotover Grange. His fingerprints were found all over the room because he had opened the safe without wearing gloves.
Q11. Why did nobody believe Horace’s story?
Answer: Nobody believed Horace because the real owner denied that she had asked him to open the safe. Horace had no proof that the young woman had tricked him.
Q12. What happened to Horace after his arrest?
Answer: Horace was sent to prison. Later, he became an assistant librarian in the prison. He often remembered the clever young woman who had tricked him.
Q13. Why was Horace angry when someone talked about honour among thieves?
Answer: Horace was angry because he himself had been cheated by another thief. He believed that thieves should keep their promises, but the young woman had tricked him.
Q14. Why is the title “A Question of Trust” appropriate?
Answer: The title is appropriate because the story is about trust and betrayal. Horace trusted the young woman and believed that she was the owner of the house. She cheated him and stole the jewels. The story teaches us not to trust strangers blindly.
Q15. What is the main message of the story?
Answer: The story teaches us that crime never pays. It also shows that we should not trust strangers blindly. A person who cheats others may also be cheated by someone else.

E. Character Sketches

Q1. Write a character sketch of Horace Danby.
Answer: Horace Danby was a fifty-year-old unmarried man. He was polite, intelligent and careful. He owned a successful business and was respected by others. He loved collecting rare books. However, he was not completely honest because he robbed a safe every year to buy books. He was a careful planner, but his overconfidence and blind trust in the young woman led to his arrest.
Q2. Write a character sketch of the young woman.
Answer: The young woman was clever, confident and dishonest. She dressed in red and pretended to be the owner of Shotover Grange. She spoke confidently and tricked Horace into opening the safe. She took the jewels and escaped without getting caught. She was a clever criminal who used Horace’s weakness to achieve her goal.

F. Vocabulary and Word Meanings

Respectable: Worthy of respect.
Meticulous: Very careful.
Culprit: A person who commits a crime.
Burglar: A person who enters a building to steal.
Jewellery: Ornaments made of precious materials.
Fingerprints: Marks left by fingers.
Safe: A strong box used to protect valuables.
Robbery: The act of stealing.
Inconvenience: Trouble or difficulty.
Desperate: Having little hope.
Suspect: To think someone may be guilty.
Faltered: Lost confidence or made a mistake.
Assistant librarian: A person who helps in a library.
