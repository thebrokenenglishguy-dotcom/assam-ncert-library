Chapter 5 — Footprints without Feet

H. G. Wells

Questions & Answers

A. Read and Find Out

Q1. How did the invisible man first become visible?
Answer: Griffin became visible when he stepped into some mud. The mud stuck to his feet and left footprints on the ground. This helped people see where he was walking.
Q2. Why was he wandering the streets?
Answer: Griffin had become invisible after swallowing certain rare drugs. He set fire to his landlord’s house and removed his clothes to escape without being seen. As a result, he became homeless, without clothes or money, and wandered the streets.
Q3. Why does Mrs Hall find the scientist eccentric?
Answer: Mrs Hall finds Griffin eccentric because of his strange appearance, unusual behaviour and bad temper. He does not like talking to anyone and wants to be left alone. He also wears bandages and dark glasses to hide his invisible face.
Q4. What curious episode occurs in the study?
Answer: A clergyman and his wife hear noises in their study early in the morning. When they enter the room, they find that the money has mysteriously disappeared, but nobody is there. Griffin has secretly stolen the money.
Q5. What other extraordinary things happen at the inn?
Answer: Mrs Hall finds that the furniture in her room starts moving by itself. The bedroom chair suddenly attacks her, and the hatstand pushes her out of the room. She believes that the room is haunted by spirits, but Griffin is actually responsible.

B. Think About It

Q1. “Griffin was rather a lawless person.” Comment.
Answer: Griffin was a brilliant scientist, but he was also a lawless person. He set fire to his landlord’s house, stole money and clothes, and attacked people. He misused his scientific discovery for selfish purposes. Therefore, he was intelligent but had no respect for the law.
Q2. How would you assess Griffin as a scientist?
Answer: Griffin was a highly intelligent and talented scientist. He discovered how to make the human body invisible through scientific experiments. However, he misused his discovery for stealing and harming others. He was a great scientist but lacked moral values and responsibility.

C. Talk About It

Q1. Would you like to become invisible? What advantages and disadvantages do you foresee, if you did?
Answer: Yes, I would like to become invisible because it would be an interesting experience. I could observe things without being noticed and explore new places. However, there would also be disadvantages. People would not be able to see me, and I might face difficulties in communicating with others. I would also have to be careful not to misuse this power.
Q2. Are there forces around us that are invisible, for example, magnetism? Are there aspects of matter that are ‘invisible’ or not visible to the naked eye? What would the world be like if you could see such forces or such aspects of matter?
Answer: Yes, many forces and things around us are invisible. Examples include magnetism, gravity, air and radio waves. Atoms and molecules are also too small to be seen with our naked eyes. If we could see these things, the world would look very different and interesting. We could understand many natural phenomena more easily.
Q3. What makes glass or water transparent (what is the scientific explanation for this)? Do you think it would be scientifically possible for a man to become invisible, or transparent?
Answer: Glass and water are transparent because they allow most visible light to pass through them without scattering it much. Making a human body completely invisible is extremely difficult because the body contains many different tissues that absorb and scatter light. Although scientists are researching materials that can hide objects under certain conditions, making an entire human body invisible is not currently possible.
