Class 9 Science — Chapter 3

Atoms and Molecules | Questions and Answers

This page answers the questions visible in the supplied textbook screenshots. The final exercise questions 1–11 are included. Where a screenshot cuts off part of a question, the answer follows the standard complete NCERT question.

In-text Questions — Laws of Chemical Combination

1. In a reaction, 5.3 g of sodium carbonate reacted with 6 g of acetic acid. The products were 2.2 g of carbon dioxide, 0.9 g of water and 8.2 g of sodium acetate. Show that these observations agree with the law of conservation of mass.

Solution:
Total mass of reactants = 5.3 + 6.0 = 11.3 g.
Total mass of products = 2.2 + 0.9 + 8.2 = 11.3 g.
The total mass is the same on both sides. Therefore, the observations agree with the law of conservation of mass.

2. Hydrogen and oxygen combine in the ratio of 1:8 by mass to form water. What mass of oxygen gas is required to react completely with 3 g of hydrogen gas?

Solution: Hydrogen : oxygen = 1 : 8 by mass.
For 3 g of hydrogen, oxygen needed = 3 × 8 = 24 g.

3. Which postulate of Dalton’s atomic theory is the result of the law of conservation of mass?

Answer: Atoms can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. They are only rearranged.

4. Which postulate of Dalton’s atomic theory can explain the law of definite proportions?

Answer: In a given compound, the relative number and kinds of atoms are constant. Therefore, the elements in a compound are always present in a fixed proportion by mass.

In-text Questions — Atoms and Molecules

1. Define the atomic mass unit.

Answer: One atomic mass unit (1 u) is defined as one-twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom.

2. Why is it not possible to see an atom with the naked eye?

Answer: Atoms are extremely small, much smaller than objects that our naked eyes can see. Therefore, we cannot see individual atoms with the naked eye.

3. Write the formulae of (i) aluminium sulphate, (ii) calcium chloride, (iii) potassium sulphate, (iv) potassium nitrate and (v) calcium carbonate.

Answer:
  1. Aluminium sulphate — Al₂(SO₄)₃
  2. Calcium chloride — CaCl₂
  3. Potassium sulphate — K₂SO₄
  4. Potassium nitrate — KNO₃
  5. Calcium carbonate — CaCO₃

4. What is meant by the term chemical formula?

Answer: A chemical formula represents a substance using the symbols of its elements. It shows which elements are present and the number or ratio of their atoms.

5. How many atoms are present in (i) an H₂S molecule and (ii) a PO₄³⁻ ion?

Answer:
  1. H₂S: 2 hydrogen + 1 sulphur = 3 atoms.
  2. PO₄³⁻: 1 phosphorus + 4 oxygen = 5 atoms.

In-text Questions — Writing Chemical Formulae

1. Write the formulae of (i) sodium oxide, (ii) aluminium chloride, (iii) sodium sulphide and (iv) magnesium hydroxide.

Answer:
  1. Sodium oxide — Na₂O
  2. Aluminium chloride — AlCl₃
  3. Sodium sulphide — Na₂S
  4. Magnesium hydroxide — Mg(OH)₂

2. Write the names of compounds represented by: (i) Al₂(SO₄)₃, (ii) CaCl₂, (iii) K₂SO₄, (iv) KNO₃ and (v) CaCO₃.

Answer:
  1. Al₂(SO₄)₃ — Aluminium sulphate
  2. CaCl₂ — Calcium chloride
  3. K₂SO₄ — Potassium sulphate
  4. KNO₃ — Potassium nitrate
  5. CaCO₃ — Calcium carbonate

In-text Questions — Molecular and Formula Unit Mass

1. Calculate the molecular masses of H₂, O₂, Cl₂, CO₂, CH₄, C₂H₆, C₂H₄, NH₃ and CH₃OH.

Solution: Use H = 1 u, C = 12 u, O = 16 u, Cl = 35.5 u and N = 14 u.
  1. H₂ = 2 × 1 = 2 u
  2. O₂ = 2 × 16 = 32 u
  3. Cl₂ = 2 × 35.5 = 71 u
  4. CO₂ = 12 + (2 × 16) = 44 u
  5. CH₄ = 12 + (4 × 1) = 16 u
  6. C₂H₆ = (2 × 12) + (6 × 1) = 30 u
  7. C₂H₄ = (2 × 12) + (4 × 1) = 28 u
  8. NH₃ = 14 + (3 × 1) = 17 u
  9. CH₃OH = 12 + (4 × 1) + 16 = 32 u

2. Calculate the formula unit masses of ZnO, Na₂O and K₂CO₃. Given: Zn = 65 u, Na = 23 u, K = 39 u, C = 12 u and O = 16 u.

Solution:
  1. ZnO = 65 + 16 = 81 u
  2. Na₂O = (2 × 23) + 16 = 62 u
  3. K₂CO₃ = (2 × 39) + 12 + (3 × 16) = 78 + 12 + 48 = 138 u

3. If one mole of carbon atoms weighs 12 grams, what is the mass (in grams) of one atom of carbon?

Solution: One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ atoms.
Mass of one carbon atom = 12 ÷ (6.022 × 10²³) g ≈ 1.99 × 10⁻²³ g.

4. Which has more atoms: 100 g of sodium or 100 g of iron? (Na = 23 u, Fe = 56 u)

Solution:
Moles of sodium = 100 ÷ 23 ≈ 4.35 mol.
Moles of iron = 100 ÷ 56 ≈ 1.79 mol.
Since sodium has more moles in the same mass, 100 g of sodium has more atoms.

Textbook Exercises

1. A 0.24 g sample of a compound of oxygen and boron contains 0.096 g of boron and 0.144 g of oxygen. Calculate the percentage composition by weight.

Solution:
Boron percentage = (0.096 ÷ 0.24) × 100 = 40%.
Oxygen percentage = (0.144 ÷ 0.24) × 100 = 60%.

2. When 3.0 g of carbon is burnt in 8.00 g of oxygen, 11.00 g of carbon dioxide is produced. What mass of carbon dioxide will be formed when 3.00 g of carbon is burnt in 50.00 g of oxygen? Which law of chemical combination will govern your answer?

Answer: 3.00 g of carbon needs only 8.00 g of oxygen to react completely. Oxygen is present in excess, so 3.00 g of carbon forms 11.00 g of carbon dioxide. This follows the law of constant proportions (and conservation of mass for the reaction).

3. What are polyatomic ions? Give examples.

Answer: Polyatomic ions are groups of two or more bonded atoms that carry an overall charge and act as a single unit. Examples: ammonium (NH₄⁺), hydroxide (OH⁻), nitrate (NO₃⁻), sulphate (SO₄²⁻) and carbonate (CO₃²⁻).

4. Write the chemical formulae of (a) magnesium chloride, (b) calcium oxide, (c) copper nitrate, (d) aluminium chloride and (e) calcium carbonate.

Answer:
  1. Magnesium chloride — MgCl₂
  2. Calcium oxide — CaO
  3. Copper nitrate — Cu(NO₃)₂ (assuming copper(II) nitrate, as commonly intended)
  4. Aluminium chloride — AlCl₃
  5. Calcium carbonate — CaCO₃

5. Give the names of the elements present in (a) quick lime, (b) hydrogen bromide, (c) baking powder and (d) potassium sulphate.

Answer:
  1. Quick lime (CaO): calcium and oxygen.
  2. Hydrogen bromide (HBr): hydrogen and bromine.
  3. Baking powder is a mixture, commonly containing sodium hydrogen carbonate and an edible acid such as tartaric acid; its elements depend on the exact ingredients. For sodium hydrogen carbonate: sodium, hydrogen, carbon and oxygen.
  4. Potassium sulphate (K₂SO₄): potassium, sulphur and oxygen.

6. Calculate the molar masses of (a) ethyne, C₂H₂, (b) sulphur molecule, S₈, (c) phosphorus molecule, P₄ (P = 31 u), (d) hydrochloric acid, HCl, and (e) nitric acid, HNO₃.

Solution: Use C = 12, H = 1, S = 32, P = 31, Cl = 35.5, N = 14 and O = 16.
  1. C₂H₂ = (2 × 12) + (2 × 1) = 26 g mol⁻¹
  2. S₈ = 8 × 32 = 256 g mol⁻¹
  3. P₄ = 4 × 31 = 124 g mol⁻¹
  4. HCl = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5 g mol⁻¹
  5. HNO₃ = 1 + 14 + (3 × 16) = 63 g mol⁻¹

7. What is the mass of (a) 1 mole of nitrogen atoms, (b) 4 moles of aluminium atoms (Al = 27 u), and (c) 10 moles of sodium sulphite (Na₂SO₃)?

Solution:
  1. 1 mole of nitrogen atoms = 14 g.
  2. 4 moles of aluminium = 4 × 27 = 108 g.
  3. Molar mass of Na₂SO₃ = (2 × 23) + 32 + (3 × 16) = 126 g mol⁻¹. Mass of 10 moles = 10 × 126 = 1260 g.

8. Convert into moles: (a) 12 g of oxygen gas, (b) 20 g of water, and (c) 22 g of carbon dioxide.

Solution: Number of moles = given mass ÷ molar mass.
  1. O₂: 12 ÷ 32 = 0.375 mol.
  2. H₂O: 20 ÷ 18 ≈ 1.11 mol.
  3. CO₂: 22 ÷ 44 = 0.5 mol.

9. What is the mass of (a) 0.2 mole of oxygen atoms and (b) 0.5 mole of water molecules?

Solution:
  1. Oxygen atoms have molar mass 16 g mol⁻¹: 0.2 × 16 = 3.2 g.
  2. Water has molar mass 18 g mol⁻¹: 0.5 × 18 = 9 g.

10. Calculate the number of molecules of sulphur (S₈) present in 16 g of solid sulphur.

Solution: Molar mass of S₈ = 8 × 32 = 256 g mol⁻¹.
Moles of S₈ = 16 ÷ 256 = 0.0625 mol.
Number of molecules = 0.0625 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.76 × 10²² molecules (approximately).

11. Calculate the number of aluminium ions present in 0.051 g of aluminium oxide. (Al = 27 u)

Solution: Formula of aluminium oxide = Al₂O₃. Molar mass = (2 × 27) + (3 × 16) = 102 g mol⁻¹.
Moles of Al₂O₃ = 0.051 ÷ 102 = 0.0005 mol.
Each formula unit contains 2 aluminium ions, so moles of aluminium ions = 2 × 0.0005 = 0.001 mol.
Number of aluminium ions = 0.001 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 6.022 × 10²⁰ ions.
