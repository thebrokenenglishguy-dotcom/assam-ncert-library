Class 9 Science — Chapter 8

Motion | Questions and Answers

Simple-English answers to the in-text questions and textbook exercises visible in the supplied screenshots.

In-text Questions

1. Can an object travel a distance and have zero displacement?

Yes. If it returns to its starting point, displacement is zero. Example: one complete round of a circular track.

2. Farmer walks around a square field of side 10 m in 40 s. Find displacement after 2 min 20 s.

Time = 140 s; rounds = 140/40 = 3.5. After 3.5 rounds, he is at the opposite corner. Displacement = diagonal = √(10²+10²) = 10√2 ≈ 14.1 m.

3. Which statement is true about displacement: it cannot be zero, or its magnitude is greater than distance?

Neither. Displacement can be zero, and its magnitude is never greater than the distance travelled.

4. Distinguish between speed and velocity.

SpeedVelocity
Distance per unit time.Displacement per unit time.
Scalar; magnitude only.Vector; magnitude and direction.

5. When is magnitude of average velocity equal to average speed?

When an object travels in a straight line in one direction without turning back.

6. What does an odometer measure?

The total distance travelled by a vehicle.

7. What does the path look like in uniform motion?

Uniform motion means equal distances in equal time intervals. For straight-line uniform motion, the path is a straight line.

8. A spaceship signal takes 5 minutes to reach Earth. Find distance if light speed is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

Time = 300 s. Distance = speed × time = 3 × 10⁸ × 300 = 9 × 10¹⁰ m.

9. When is acceleration uniform and non-uniform?

Uniform: velocity changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals. Non-uniform: velocity changes by unequal amounts in equal time intervals.

10. Bus slows from 80 km/h to 60 km/h in 5 s. Find acceleration.

u = 22.22 m/s; v = 16.67 m/s. a = (v−u)/t = (16.67−22.22)/5 = −1.11 m/s².

11. A train starts from rest and reaches 40 km/h in 10 min. Find acceleration.

v = 11.11 m/s; t = 600 s. a = (v−u)/t = 11.11/600 = 0.0185 m/s².

12. Nature of distance-time graphs for uniform and non-uniform motion?

Uniform motion: straight line. Non-uniform motion: usually a curve or a line with changing slope.

13. What does a distance-time graph parallel to the time axis mean?

Distance does not change; the object is at rest.

14. What does a speed-time graph parallel to the time axis mean?

The object moves at constant speed.

15. What does the area below a velocity-time graph represent?

Displacement during that time interval.

16. Bus starts from rest, acceleration 0.1 m/s² for 2 minutes. Find final speed and distance.

t = 120 s. (a) v = u+at = 0.1×120 = 12 m/s. (b) s = ut+½at² = ½×0.1×120² = 720 m.

17. Train speed 90 km/h, acceleration −0.5 m/s². Find stopping distance.

u = 25 m/s, v = 0. v²−u² = 2as, so s = (0−625)/(−1) = 625 m.

18. Trolley starts from rest and accelerates at 2 cm/s² for 3 s. Find velocity.

v = u+at = 0+2×3 = 6 cm/s.

19. Racing car starts from rest with acceleration 4 m/s² for 10 s. Find distance.

s = ut+½at² = ½×4×10² = 200 m.

20. Stone thrown upwards at 5 m/s; acceleration 10 m/s² downward. Find maximum height and time.

At top v=0. Taking upward positive: 0=5−10t, so t=0.5 s. Also 0²−5²=2(−10)s, so height=1.25 m.

Textbook Exercises

1. Athlete completes one round of a circular track of diameter 200 m in 40 s. Find distance and displacement after 2 min 20 s.

Time = 140 s = 3.5 rounds. Distance = 3.5×π×200 = 700π ≈ 2,200 m. Displacement = diameter = 200 m.

2. Joseph runs 300 m from A to B in 150 s, then 100 m back in 60 s. Find average speed and velocity (a) A to B, (b) A to C.

(a) Average speed = 300/150 = 2 m/s; average velocity = 2 m/s towards B. (b) Total distance = 400 m; time = 210 s; average speed = 1.90 m/s. Net displacement = 200 m towards B; average velocity = 0.95 m/s towards B.

3. Abdul’s average speed to school is 20 km/h and back is 30 km/h. Find average speed for the trip.

Equal distances: average speed = 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂) = 2×20×30/50 = 24 km/h.

4. Motorboat starts from rest and accelerates at 3.0 m/s² for 8 s. Find distance.

s = ½at² = ½×3×8² = 96 m.

5. Car at 52 km/h stops in 5 s; another at 3 km/h stops in 10 s. Which travels farther after braking?

Distance = area under speed-time triangle. First: ½×5×(52×5/18) ≈ 36.1 m. Second: ½×10×(3×5/18) ≈ 4.17 m. The first car travels farther.

6. Distance-time graph for A, B and C: (a) fastest? (b) are all ever at same point? (c) C’s distance when B passes A? (d) B’s distance when it passes C?

(a) B, because it has the steepest slope. For (b)–(d), inspect the lines’ intersection points on the original graph. At (c), read C’s position at the A–B intersection and subtract C’s starting position. At (d), read B’s position at the B–C intersection.

The graph is too small in the screenshot to read the intersections accurately, so exact numerical values would be unreliable.

7. Ball dropped from 20 m; acceleration 10 m/s². Find impact velocity and time.

v²=u²+2as=400, so v=20 m/s downward. From s=½at², 20=5t², so t=2 s.

8. Car speed-time graph: (a) distance in first 4 s? (b) which part is uniform motion?

(a) Distance is the area under the graph from 0 to 4 s; the screenshot grid is not clear enough for a reliable exact area. (b) The nearly horizontal section, about 6–10 s, represents approximately uniform speed.

9. Are these situations possible? (a) constant acceleration with zero velocity (b) acceleration with uniform speed (c) acceleration perpendicular to direction of motion.

(a) Yes: a ball at the highest point has momentarily zero velocity but gravity still acts. (b) Yes: uniform circular motion has constant speed but changing direction. (c) Yes: centripetal acceleration is perpendicular to instantaneous velocity in circular motion.

10. Satellite orbit radius 42,250 km; period 24 h. Find speed.

Distance per revolution = 2πr ≈ 265,465 km. Speed = 265,465/24 ≈ 11,061 km/h ≈ 3.07 km/s.
