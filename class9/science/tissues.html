Class 9 Science — Chapter 6

Tissues | Questions and Answers

Answers are written in simple English. This page covers the in-text questions and all 15 exercise questions visible in the supplied screenshots.

In-text Questions

1. What is a tissue?

Answer: A tissue is a group of cells that are similar in structure and work together to perform a particular function.

2. What is the utility of tissues in multicellular organisms?

Answer: Tissues allow cells to specialise in different jobs. This division of labour makes body functions more efficient.

3. Which of the two onions has longer roots? Why?

Answer: The onion whose root tips are intact and have not been cut will have longer roots. The tips contain apical meristem, where cells divide and make roots grow.

4. Do the roots continue growing even after we have removed their tips?

Answer: Root growth is greatly reduced or stops at the cut tip because the apical meristem responsible for lengthwise growth has been removed. Other parts of the root may still grow.

5. Why would the tips stop growing in jar 2 after we cut them?

Answer: Cutting off the tips removes the apical meristem, the region where cells divide actively. Therefore, the roots cannot continue growing in length at those tips.

6. Name types of simple tissues.

Answer: Parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma.

7. Where is apical meristem found?

Answer: It is found at the growing tips of roots and shoots.

8. Which tissue makes up the husk of coconut?

Answer: Sclerenchyma tissue.

9. What are the constituents of phloem?

Answer: Sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma and phloem fibres.

10. Name the tissue responsible for movement in our body.

Answer: Muscular tissue.

11. What does a neuron look like?

Answer: A neuron has a cell body containing a nucleus, many short branches called dendrites, and one long fibre called the axon. The axon may have branched endings.

12. Give three features of cardiac muscles.

Answer:
  1. They are striated and branched.
  2. Their cells usually have one centrally placed nucleus.
  3. They contract and relax rhythmically without conscious control and do not tire easily.

13. What are the functions of areolar tissue?

Answer: Areolar tissue fills spaces inside organs, supports internal organs, binds the skin to muscles and helps in tissue repair.

Textbook Exercises

1. Define the term “tissue”.

Answer: A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a particular function.

2. How many types of elements together make up the xylem tissue? Name them.

Answer: Four types of elements make up xylem: tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma and xylem fibres.

3. How are simple tissues different from complex tissues in plants?

Answer: Simple tissues consist of one type of similar cells performing similar functions, such as parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma. Complex tissues contain different types of cells that work together, mainly to transport substances. Xylem and phloem are complex tissues.

4. Differentiate between parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma on the basis of their cell wall.

5. What are the functions of the stomata?

Answer: Stomata allow exchange of gases, such as carbon dioxide and oxygen, and control the loss of water vapour through transpiration. Guard cells open and close the stomata.

7. What is the specific function of the cardiac muscle?

Answer: Cardiac muscle contracts and relaxes rhythmically to pump blood throughout the body.

9. Draw a labelled diagram of a neuron.

Answer: Draw a neuron and label these parts: dendrites, cell body (cyton), nucleus, axon, myelin sheath (if shown), and nerve endings.

10. Name the following tissues.

(a) Tissue that forms the inner lining of our mouth: Squamous epithelial tissue.
(b) Tissue that connects muscle to bone in humans: Tendon.
(c) Tissue that transports food in plants: Phloem.
(d) Tissue that stores fat in our body: Adipose tissue.
(e) Connective tissue with a fluid matrix: Blood.
(f) Tissue present in the brain: Nervous tissue.

11. Identify the type of tissue in the following: skin, bark of tree, bone, lining of kidney tubule, vascular bundle.

Answer:
  • Skin: epithelial tissue (stratified squamous epithelium).
  • Bark of tree: protective tissue, mainly cork.
  • Bone: connective tissue.
  • Lining of kidney tubule: cuboidal epithelial tissue.
  • Vascular bundle: complex conducting tissues, xylem and phloem.

12. Name the regions in which parenchyma tissue is present.

Answer: Parenchyma is found in the soft parts of plants, such as the cortex and pith of stems and roots, the mesophyll of leaves, and the pulp of fruits.

13. What is the role of the epidermis in plants?

Answer: The epidermis protects the plant from injury, infection and water loss. Its cuticle reduces water loss, while stomata allow gas exchange. Root epidermal cells may form root hairs to absorb water and minerals.

14. How does the cork act as a protective tissue?

Answer: Cork cells are dead and closely packed, with no intercellular spaces. Their walls contain suberin, which makes them largely waterproof and helps protect the plant from water loss, injury and infection.

15. Complete the chart of permanent tissues.

Answer:
  • Permanent tissue → Simple: Parenchyma, Collenchyma, Sclerenchyma.
  • Permanent tissue → Complex: Xylem, Phloem.
