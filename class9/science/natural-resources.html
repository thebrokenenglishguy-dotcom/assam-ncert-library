Chapter 14 — Natural Resources

Complete chapter notes, important concepts, in-text questions and NCERT exercise answers in simple English.

14.1 The Breath of Life: Air

1. What are natural resources?

Natural resources are materials and forms of energy available in nature that support life. The main resources discussed in this chapter are air, water and soil, along with energy from the Sun.

2. What are the three major parts of the Earth that support life?

  • Lithosphere: The outer solid crust of the Earth, including land and rocks.
  • Hydrosphere: All water on Earth, including oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater and ice.
  • Atmosphere: The blanket of air surrounding the Earth.

The life-supporting zone where these three interact is called the biosphere.

3. What is the biosphere?

The biosphere is the part of the Earth where life exists. It includes parts of the land, water and air where living organisms are found.

4. What are biotic and abiotic components of the biosphere?

  • Biotic components: Living organisms such as plants, animals and microorganisms.
  • Abiotic components: Non-living parts such as air, water and soil.

5. What is air made up of?

Air is a mixture of gases. It contains mainly nitrogen and oxygen, along with small amounts of carbon dioxide, water vapour and other gases. The composition of air is linked to life on Earth.

6. How is the atmosphere of Earth different from the atmospheres of Venus and Mars?

Earth's atmosphere contains mostly nitrogen and oxygen. The atmospheres of Venus and Mars contain a very high proportion of carbon dioxide, about 95–97%. This difference is important for life and climate.

7. How is carbon dioxide removed from the atmosphere?

Green plants use carbon dioxide during photosynthesis to make glucose. Many marine animals also use dissolved carbonates in seawater to make their shells. These processes help keep the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere low.

14.1.1 The Role of the Atmosphere in Climate Control

8. How does the atmosphere act like a blanket?

The atmosphere is a poor conductor of heat. It slows down the escape of heat from the Earth and keeps the average temperature fairly steady during the day and throughout the year. It also prevents the temperature from rising too much during the day.

9. Why does the Moon have a very large range of temperature?

The Moon has almost no atmosphere. Therefore, it cannot retain and distribute heat like the Earth’s atmosphere. Its temperature can become extremely hot in sunlight and extremely cold in darkness.

10. Why does air get heated mainly from the Earth's surface?

The Earth's surface absorbs energy from the Sun and becomes warm. It then transfers heat to the air above it. Air can also be heated by water bodies and by the activities of living organisms.

11. What are convection currents in air?

When air is heated, it expands, becomes less dense and rises. Cooler, denser air moves in to take its place. This movement sets up convection currents in the atmosphere.

12. Describe Activity 14.1 and its conclusion.

Place a beaker of water, a beaker of soil and a closed bottle containing a thermometer in bright sunlight for about three hours. Measure their temperatures and compare them with readings taken in shade. The activity shows that land and water heat at different rates and that air temperature is also affected by the surroundings. The comparison should be made under the same conditions.

14.1.2 The Movement of Air: Winds

13. What causes wind?

Uneven heating of the Earth's surface causes differences in air temperature and pressure. Warm air rises and creates a low-pressure region. Cooler air from a high-pressure region moves towards it. This movement of air is called wind.

14. How are winds formed near coastal areas during the day?

During the day, land heats up faster than sea water. Air above the land becomes warmer and rises, creating low pressure. Cooler air from the sea moves towards the land. This is called a sea breeze.

15. How are winds formed near coastal areas at night?

At night, land cools faster than sea water. The air above the sea remains warmer and rises. Cooler air from the land moves towards the sea. This is called a land breeze.

16. What is the difference between sea breeze and land breeze?

  • Sea breeze: Blows from sea to land during the day because land heats faster.
  • Land breeze: Blows from land to sea at night because land cools faster.

17. How do mountains affect the movement of winds?

Mountains can change the direction and speed of winds. They may block, guide or force air to rise. The Himalayas influence the movement of winds and rainfall over the Indian subcontinent.

18. What other factors influence winds?

The rotation of the Earth and the presence of mountain ranges also influence wind direction and movement.

19. Describe Activity 14.2 (smoke and a candle).

A candle is placed inside a wide-mouthed beaker or bottle. An incense stick is held near the mouth and then slightly above the candle. The smoke shows the direction of air movement: cooler air enters near the lower opening, gets heated by the candle and rises, carrying smoke out through the upper region. This demonstrates convection currents.

14.1.3 Rain

20. How is rain formed?

  1. Water evaporates from oceans, rivers, lakes and other water bodies.
  2. Warm air rises carrying water vapour.
  3. As the air rises, it expands and cools.
  4. Water vapour condenses into tiny water droplets around dust and other particles.
  5. The droplets join and become larger.
  6. When they become heavy enough, they fall as rain.

21. What is condensation?

Condensation is the process in which water vapour cools and changes into tiny liquid water droplets.

22. What are the nuclei of cloud droplets?

Very small particles such as dust and other suspended particles in air can provide a surface on which water vapour condenses. They act as nuclei for the formation of cloud droplets.

23. What are the different forms of precipitation?

Depending on the temperature of the air, water may fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail.

24. What decides rainfall patterns in India?

Rainfall patterns are strongly influenced by the prevailing winds, especially the south-west and north-east monsoons. Depressions in the Bay of Bengal can also bring rain to different regions.

25. Describe Activity 14.3 (cloud formation in a bottle).

Put a little water in an empty bottle, close it and shake it so the inside becomes moist. Open it, allow a little incense smoke to enter, close it tightly and squeeze the bottle. Then release it. A fog-like cloud can appear when pressure is reduced because the air cools and water vapour condenses on smoke particles. The smoke provides condensation nuclei.

14.1.4 Air Pollution

26. What is air pollution?

Air pollution is an increase in harmful substances in the air to levels that can damage living organisms and the environment.

27. How do fossil fuels cause air pollution?

Coal and petroleum contain small amounts of nitrogen and sulphur. When they burn, oxides of nitrogen and sulphur are produced. Burning fuels also releases suspended particles and unburnt hydrocarbons.

28. What is acid rain?

Oxides of sulphur and nitrogen dissolve in rainwater and form acids. Rain containing these acids is called acid rain. It can harm plants, aquatic life, buildings and soil.

29. What is smog?

Smog is polluted air in which smoke and other pollutants mix with fog. It reduces visibility and can cause breathing problems.

30. What are the harmful effects of air pollution?

  • It can cause allergies and breathing problems.
  • It increases the risk of serious illnesses, including cancer and heart diseases.
  • It reduces visibility, especially in cold weather.
  • It can damage plants, animals and the wider environment.

31. Why are lichens used as indicators of air pollution?

Lichens are very sensitive to air pollutants such as sulphur dioxide. They are less common in heavily polluted places and can grow more easily where the air is cleaner. Comparing lichens near and far from roads can give an indication of air quality.

32. Name three human activities that can increase air pollution.

Burning coal and petroleum in vehicles and industries, burning waste or crop residues, and cutting or burning forests can increase air pollution.

14.2 Water: A Wonder Liquid

33. Where is water found on Earth?

Water is found in oceans and seas, rivers, lakes, ponds, underground, in glaciers and ice-caps, and as water vapour in the atmosphere. Most of the Earth's water is salty. Fresh water is found in ice-caps, glaciers, rivers, lakes, ponds and underground sources.

34. Why is fresh water not easily available everywhere?

Much of the Earth's water is salty or locked in ice-caps and glaciers. The amount of fresh water available in rivers, lakes and underground sources varies from place to place and by season.

35. Why do organisms need water?

All cellular processes take place in a water medium. Chemical reactions inside cells occur between substances dissolved in water. Water also transports substances from one part of the body to another. Organisms need water to stay alive.

36. Why do land organisms need fresh water?

Land organisms cannot easily remove the large amount of dissolved salts present in seawater. They therefore need fresh water to maintain the correct water balance in their bodies.

37. How does water availability affect biodiversity?

Water affects both the number of individuals and the variety of species that can survive in an area. Regions with suitable water availability can support more kinds of plants and animals. Temperature and soil also matter, but water is a major resource.

38. What is water harvesting?

Water harvesting is the collection and storage of rainwater or surface runoff for later use or for recharging groundwater. Examples include rooftop rainwater collection, storage tanks, ponds and recharge pits.

14.2.1 Water Pollution

39. What is water pollution?

Water pollution is the contamination of water bodies by harmful substances or changes that make the water unsafe for organisms or unsuitable for use.

40. What are the main causes of water pollution described in the chapter?

  • Fertilisers and pesticides washed from farms into water bodies.
  • Sewage from towns and cities.
  • Industrial waste released into rivers and lakes.
  • Hot water released by industries after cooling operations.

41. How does the addition of undesirable substances affect water bodies?

Fertilisers, pesticides, poisonous chemicals and disease-causing microbes can harm aquatic organisms. Some pollutants can cause diseases such as cholera.

42. Why is dissolved oxygen important in water?

Aquatic animals and plants use dissolved oxygen for respiration. If pollution reduces the amount of dissolved oxygen, aquatic organisms may be harmed or may die.

43. How does hot water released by industries affect aquatic life?

It raises the temperature of the water. Aquatic organisms can survive only within a certain temperature range. A sudden temperature change can harm them and affect their breeding. Eggs and larvae are especially sensitive.

44. Give three ways to prevent water pollution.

  • Treat sewage and industrial waste before releasing it.
  • Reduce the excessive use of fertilisers and pesticides and prevent farm runoff.
  • Do not dump rubbish, oils or chemicals into rivers, lakes and ponds.

14.3 Mineral Riches in the Soil

45. Why is soil an important natural resource?

Soil supports plant growth and provides minerals and nutrients to living organisms. It also provides a habitat for many organisms and helps store and filter water.

46. How is soil formed?

Soil forms slowly when rocks break down into small particles through physical, chemical and biological processes. The broken rock particles mix with decayed organic matter called humus and living organisms.

47. What are the main factors that break rocks into soil?

  • Sun: Rocks expand when heated and contract when cooled. Repeated expansion and contraction causes cracks and breaks rocks into smaller pieces.
  • Water: Water enters cracks. When it freezes, it expands and widens the cracks. Flowing water also wears rocks down and carries particles away.
  • Wind: Wind carries sand and small particles that rub against rocks and wear them down.
  • Living organisms: Lichens and mosses grow on rocks and release substances that help break them down. Plant roots enter cracks and widen them as they grow.

48. What is humus?

Humus is the dark organic matter formed from decayed remains of plants and animals. It adds nutrients to soil and improves its structure.

49. What is topsoil?

Topsoil is the upper layer of soil. It contains soil particles, humus and many living organisms. It is important for plant growth and biodiversity.

50. Why is humus important for soil?

Humus makes soil more porous, allowing air and water to enter. It also adds nutrients and helps create a suitable environment for soil organisms.

51. What determines the type and quality of soil?

The average size of soil particles helps determine the type of soil. The amount of humus, mineral nutrients and microscopic organisms affects soil quality and fertility. The parent rock and the depth of the soil also matter.

52. How can excessive use of fertilisers and pesticides damage soil?

Long-term excessive use can damage soil structure and kill microorganisms that recycle nutrients. It can also harm earthworms and other organisms that help maintain fertile soil.

53. What is soil pollution?

Soil pollution is the addition or removal of substances in soil in a way that harms its fertility, structure or living organisms. Excessive chemicals and removal of useful soil components can cause it.

Soil Erosion

54. What is soil erosion?

Soil erosion is the removal of the top fertile layer of soil by agents such as flowing water and wind.

55. What causes soil erosion?

Flowing water and strong winds carry away fine soil particles. Deforestation and removal of vegetation make erosion faster because the soil is no longer held together by plant roots.

56. How do plants prevent soil erosion?

Plant roots hold soil particles together. Vegetation also slows down flowing water and reduces the force of wind near the ground. This helps prevent the topsoil from being washed or blown away.

57. What are methods of reducing soil erosion?

  • Plant trees and maintain vegetation cover.
  • Use terrace farming on slopes.
  • Practise contour ploughing.
  • Use shelter belts or rows of trees to reduce wind speed.
  • Avoid overgrazing and deforestation.

58. Describe Activity 14.11 (soil erosion in two trays).

Fill two identical trays with soil. Grow grass or small plants in one tray and leave the other bare. Tilt both trays at the same angle and pour equal amounts of water over them. More soil is carried away from the bare tray. This shows that plant cover reduces soil erosion.

14.4 Biogeochemical Cycles

59. What are biogeochemical cycles?

Biogeochemical cycles are the repeated movement and exchange of matter between the living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) parts of the biosphere. They help maintain balance in nature.

14.4.1 The Water Cycle

60. What is the water cycle?

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere and back to the land and water bodies.

61. Explain the water cycle.

  1. The Sun heats water in oceans, rivers, lakes and other water bodies.
  2. Water evaporates and enters the atmosphere as water vapour. Plants also release water vapour through transpiration.
  3. Water vapour rises, cools and condenses to form clouds.
  4. Water falls as rain, snow or other forms of precipitation.
  5. Some water flows over the land into rivers and seas. Some seeps into the soil and becomes groundwater.
  6. Groundwater may return to the surface through springs or wells, and the cycle continues.

62. Why do rivers carry nutrients to the sea?

As water flows over or through rocks and soil, it dissolves and carries some minerals and nutrients. Rivers transport these substances towards the sea, where marine organisms can use them.

14.4.2 The Nitrogen Cycle

63. Why is nitrogen important for living organisms?

Nitrogen is needed to make proteins, nucleic acids such as DNA and RNA, and vitamins. These substances are essential for growth and life processes.

64. Why can most organisms not use atmospheric nitrogen directly?

Atmospheric nitrogen gas is very stable. Most organisms cannot convert it directly into useful compounds. It must first be changed into compounds such as nitrates and nitrites that plants can absorb.

65. What is nitrogen fixation?

Nitrogen fixation is the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into nitrogen-containing compounds that can enter the soil and be used by living organisms.

66. How do nitrogen-fixing bacteria help plants?

Some bacteria live freely in soil, while others live in root nodules of leguminous plants such as peas and beans. They convert atmospheric nitrogen into compounds that plants can use.

67. How does lightning help in nitrogen fixation?

The high temperatures and pressures produced during lightning allow atmospheric nitrogen to react with oxygen and form nitrogen oxides. These dissolve in rainwater and reach the soil as acids and nitrates, which can be used by plants.

68. Explain the nitrogen cycle.

  1. Atmospheric nitrogen is fixed by bacteria and by lightning into usable nitrogen compounds.
  2. Plants absorb nitrates and nitrites and use them to make proteins and other nitrogen compounds.
  3. Animals obtain nitrogen by eating plants or other animals.
  4. When plants and animals die, decomposers convert nitrogen-containing matter into ammonia. This is called ammonification.
  5. Nitrifying bacteria convert ammonia into nitrites and then nitrates. This is called nitrification.
  6. Denitrifying bacteria convert some nitrates back into nitrogen gas, returning it to the atmosphere.

69. What are ammonification, nitrification and denitrification?

  • Ammonification: Conversion of nitrogen-containing organic matter into ammonia.
  • Nitrification: Conversion of ammonia into nitrites and then nitrates by bacteria.
  • Denitrification: Conversion of nitrates into nitrogen gas by bacteria, returning nitrogen to the atmosphere.

14.4.3 The Carbon Cycle

70. Where is carbon found on Earth?

Carbon is found in the atmosphere as carbon dioxide, in water as dissolved carbonates and bicarbonates, in rocks and minerals, and in living organisms as part of carbohydrates, proteins, fats and nucleic acids. It is also found in fossil fuels such as coal and petroleum.

71. Explain the carbon cycle.

  1. Green plants take in carbon dioxide during photosynthesis and convert it into glucose and other organic compounds.
  2. Animals obtain carbon by eating plants or other animals.
  3. Plants and animals release carbon dioxide during respiration.
  4. When organisms die, decomposers break down their remains and return carbon compounds to the environment.
  5. Some carbon remains stored for long periods in fossil fuels, rocks and carbonate shells.
  6. Combustion of wood and fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide back into the atmosphere.

72. How does combustion affect the carbon cycle?

Burning fuels such as coal, petroleum and wood releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. Large-scale burning of fossil fuels increases atmospheric carbon dioxide.

14.4.3 (i) The Greenhouse Effect

73. What is the greenhouse effect?

The greenhouse effect is the warming of the Earth's surface and lower atmosphere because certain gases absorb and re-radiate heat. It helps keep the Earth warm enough for life, but an increase in greenhouse gases can cause extra warming.

74. Name some greenhouse gases.

Carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour, nitrous oxide and ozone are greenhouse gases.

75. What is global warming?

Global warming is the long-term rise in the average temperature of the Earth. An increase in greenhouse gases, especially due to human activities such as burning fossil fuels and deforestation, increases the amount of heat retained by the atmosphere.

76. What may be the consequences of global warming?

  • Melting of glaciers and polar ice.
  • Rise in sea level and flooding of low-lying coastal areas.
  • Changes in rainfall patterns and more frequent or intense extreme weather in some regions.
  • Disturbance of habitats, agriculture and biodiversity.

14.4.4 The Oxygen Cycle

77. In what forms is oxygen found in nature?

Oxygen is found as oxygen gas in the atmosphere, as part of water, in carbon dioxide, in minerals in the Earth's crust, and in biological molecules such as carbohydrates, proteins, nucleic acids and fats.

78. Explain the oxygen cycle.

Green plants release oxygen during photosynthesis. Living organisms use oxygen during respiration. Oxygen is also used in combustion and in the formation of oxides. Photosynthesis returns oxygen to the atmosphere and helps maintain its level.

79. Name three processes that use oxygen from the atmosphere.

Respiration, combustion and the formation of oxides of nitrogen use oxygen.

80. Which major process returns oxygen to the atmosphere?

Photosynthesis returns oxygen to the atmosphere.

14.5 Ozone Layer

81. What is ozone?

Ozone is a form of oxygen whose molecule contains three oxygen atoms. Its chemical formula is O3.

82. Where is the ozone layer found and why is it important?

The ozone layer is found in the upper atmosphere, mainly in the stratosphere. It absorbs much of the harmful ultraviolet radiation from the Sun and protects living organisms on Earth.

83. Why is ozone harmful near the Earth's surface but useful in the upper atmosphere?

Ozone is a poisonous, reactive gas when present near the ground, where it can harm living organisms and the respiratory system. High in the atmosphere, it is useful because it absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation.

84. What causes depletion of the ozone layer?

Some human-made chemicals, especially chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), reach the upper atmosphere and react with ozone. These reactions reduce the amount of ozone and can cause severe thinning of the ozone layer.

85. What could happen if the ozone layer becomes thinner?

More harmful ultraviolet radiation would reach the Earth's surface. This can damage living cells and increase risks to human health, plants and other organisms.

Important In-text Questions — Answers

1. How is our atmosphere different from the atmospheres on Venus and Mars?

Earth's atmosphere contains mostly nitrogen and oxygen, while the atmospheres of Venus and Mars contain about 95–97% carbon dioxide.

2. How does the atmosphere act as a blanket?

It slows the escape of heat from the Earth and prevents extreme temperature changes between day and night.

3. What causes winds?

Uneven heating of the Earth's surface creates differences in air pressure. Air moves from high-pressure areas towards low-pressure areas, producing winds.

4. How are clouds formed?

Warm moist air rises, expands and cools. Water vapour condenses on tiny particles in the air to form droplets. These droplets gather to form clouds.

5. List three human activities that would lead to air pollution.

Burning fossil fuels in vehicles and industries, burning waste or crop residues, and deforestation or forest fires.

6. Why do organisms need water?

Water provides the medium for cellular reactions and helps transport dissolved substances through the body.

7. What is the major source of fresh water in the city, town or village where you live?

This depends on the locality. Common sources include rivers, reservoirs, lakes, groundwater from wells or tube wells, and treated municipal water supplies. Check the source used in your own area.

8. Do you know of any activity which may be polluting this water source?

Possible sources include untreated sewage, dumping of garbage, washing and bathing near water sources, farm runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides, and industrial waste. The correct answer depends on local observation.

9. How is soil formed?

Soil forms when rocks are broken down by heat and cooling, water, wind and living organisms. The small rock particles mix with humus.

10. What is soil erosion?

Soil erosion is the removal of the fertile top layer of soil by wind or flowing water.

11. What are the methods of preventing or reducing soil erosion?

Plant trees, maintain grass cover, practise terrace farming and contour ploughing, use shelter belts and prevent deforestation and overgrazing.

12. What are the different states in which water is found during the water cycle?

Water is found as liquid water in water bodies and rain, as water vapour in the atmosphere, and as solid ice or snow.

13. Name two biologically important compounds that contain both oxygen and nitrogen.

Proteins and nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) contain both oxygen and nitrogen.

14. List any three human activities which would lead to an increase in the carbon dioxide content of air.

Burning fossil fuels, deforestation and burning forests or biomass on a large scale.

15. What is the greenhouse effect?

It is the warming caused when greenhouse gases absorb and re-radiate heat, slowing the loss of heat from the Earth.

16. What are the two forms of oxygen found in the atmosphere?

Oxygen is found mainly as diatomic oxygen (O2) and ozone (O3).

NCERT Exercise — Questions and Answers

1. Why is the atmosphere essential for life?

The atmosphere provides oxygen for respiration and carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. It protects life from harmful solar radiation, helps maintain a suitable temperature and supports weather and the water cycle.

2. Why is water essential for life?

Water is needed for cellular chemical reactions, digestion, transport of dissolved substances and regulation of body processes. All known life depends on water.

3. How are living organisms dependent on the soil? Are organisms that live in water totally independent of soil as a resource?

Plants obtain water and mineral nutrients from soil and use it for support. Many soil organisms also depend on soil for food and shelter. Organisms living in water are not totally independent of soil because nutrients from land are carried into water bodies by rivers, and aquatic ecosystems also depend on minerals and nutrients from sediments and surrounding land.

4. You have seen weather reports on television and in newspapers. How do you think we are able to predict the weather?

Weather is predicted by collecting information about temperature, air pressure, humidity, wind speed and direction, clouds and rainfall. Weather stations, satellites, radar and computer models are used to study these observations and forecast future conditions.

5. Many human activities lead to increasing levels of pollution of air, water bodies and soil. Do you think that isolating these activities to specific and limited areas would help in reducing pollution?

Keeping polluting activities away from homes, farms and sensitive ecosystems can reduce people's exposure and limit the spread of some pollutants. However, isolation alone is not enough. Industries must treat waste and emissions, follow environmental rules and dispose of pollutants safely. Air and water can carry pollution beyond the site, so prevention and treatment are essential.

6. Write a note on how forests influence the quality of our air, soil and water resources.

Forests absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen during photosynthesis. Trees trap dust and help improve air quality. Their roots hold soil together and reduce soil erosion. Forests slow surface runoff, help water seep into the ground and reduce flooding. They also support the water cycle and provide habitats for many organisms.

Quick Revision

  • The biosphere is the life-supporting zone where land, water and air interact.
  • The atmosphere controls temperature and supports winds and rainfall.
  • Uneven heating of land and water causes winds and sea/land breezes.
  • Water is essential for cellular reactions and transport.
  • Air, water and soil pollution harm organisms and ecosystems.
  • Soil forms slowly from weathered rocks mixed with humus.
  • Vegetation protects topsoil from erosion.
  • Water, nitrogen, carbon and oxygen move through natural cycles.
  • The greenhouse effect keeps Earth warm; excess greenhouse gases cause global warming.
  • The ozone layer absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation; CFCs can deplete it.
