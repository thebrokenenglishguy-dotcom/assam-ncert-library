Class 9 Science — Chapter 11

Work and Energy | Questions and Answers

Answers are written in simple English and points. The chapter explanations and question topics follow the supplied NCERT textbook pages. Numerical answers show the formula and main steps. Use g = 10 m/s² where the question says so; otherwise use the value given.

Important Concepts and Formulae

1. What is work in science?

  1. Work is done when a force acts on an object and the object moves in the direction of the force.
  2. Both force and displacement are needed for work to be done.
  3. If there is no displacement, work done is zero.

2. What is the formula for work?

Work done = Force × Displacement
W = F × sHere, F is the force and s is the displacement in the direction of the force. This formula is used when force and displacement are in the same direction.

3. What are positive, negative and zero work?

  • Positive work: Force and displacement are in the same direction.
  • Negative work: Force acts opposite to displacement.
  • Zero work: There is no displacement, or the force is perpendicular to displacement.

4. What is the SI unit of work?

The SI unit is joule (J). One joule is the work done when a force of 1 newton moves an object by 1 metre in the direction of the force.1 J = 1 N × 1 m

5. What is energy?

Energy is the capacity of an object to do work. Its SI unit is also joule (J).

6. What are the main forms of energy mentioned in this chapter?

  1. Mechanical energy (kinetic and potential energy).
  2. Heat energy.
  3. Chemical energy.
  4. Electrical energy.
  5. Light energy.

7. What is kinetic energy?

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object because of its motion. A moving car, a rolling stone and flowing water have kinetic energy.Kinetic energy, Eₖ = ½mv²Here m is mass in kg and v is speed in m/s.

8. What is potential energy?

Potential energy is the energy possessed by an object because of its position or shape. A raised object, a stretched rubber band and a compressed spring can have potential energy.

9. What is gravitational potential energy?

It is the energy an object has because it is raised to a height above the ground or a chosen reference level.Potential energy, Eₚ = mghHere m = mass, g = acceleration due to gravity and h = height.

10. What is the law of conservation of energy?

  1. Energy can neither be created nor destroyed.
  2. It can only change from one form to another.
  3. The total energy of a system remains constant during the transformation.

11. What is mechanical energy?

Mechanical energy is the sum of the kinetic energy and potential energy of an object.Mechanical energy = Kinetic energy + Potential energy

12. What is power?

Power is the rate of doing work or the rate of transfer of energy.Power = Work done / Time taken
P = W/tIts SI unit is watt (W).

13. Define one watt.

One watt is the power of an agent that does 1 joule of work in 1 second.1 W = 1 J/s

14. What is the commercial unit of electrical energy?

The commercial unit is kilowatt-hour (kWh). One kilowatt-hour is the energy used by a 1 kW device in one hour.1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ JOne unit of electricity means 1 kWh.

15. Important formula summary

Work: W = Fs
Kinetic energy: Eₖ = ½mv²
Potential energy: Eₚ = mgh
Power: P = W/t
Mechanical energy: E = Eₖ + Eₚ
1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

In-text Questions and Answers

1. When do we say that work is done?

Work is done when a force acts on an object and the object is displaced in the direction of the force.

2. Write an expression for work done when a force acts in the direction of displacement.

W = F × sWork done is equal to force multiplied by displacement in the direction of the force.

3. Define 1 joule of work.

One joule is the work done when a force of 1 N moves an object through 1 m in the direction of the force.

4. A pair of bullocks exerts a force of 140 N on a plough. The field being ploughed is 15 m long. How much work is done?

W = F × s = 140 × 15 = 2100 JAnswer: 2100 J.

5. A force of 5 N moves an object through 2 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.

W = F × s = 5 × 2 = 10 JAnswer: 10 J.

6. A force of 7 N acts on an object. Its displacement is 8 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.

W = 7 × 8 = 56 JAnswer: 56 J.

7. What work is done when the force on an object is zero? What work is done when displacement is zero?

  1. If force is zero, work done is zero.
  2. If displacement is zero, work done is also zero.

8. When a porter lifts a luggage bag, which force does positive work and which force does negative work?

  1. The force applied by the porter acts upwards, in the direction of displacement. It does positive work.
  2. Gravity acts downwards, opposite to displacement. It does negative work.

9. A porter lifts a 15 kg luggage bag through a height of 1.5 m. Calculate the work done by him. Take g = 10 m/s².

W = mgh = 15 × 10 × 1.5 = 225 JAnswer: 225 J.

10. What is the work done by a force when the object moves opposite to the force?

Work done is negative because the force acts opposite to the direction of displacement.

11. What is energy? What is its SI unit?

Energy is the capacity to do work. Its SI unit is joule (J).

12. Give examples of objects that possess kinetic energy.

A moving car, a flying aircraft, flowing water, a rolling stone, a speeding bullet and blowing wind possess kinetic energy because they are moving.

13. What happens to the kinetic energy of an object when its speed increases?

Kinetic energy increases with the square of speed. If speed becomes twice, kinetic energy becomes four times.

14. An object of mass 15 kg moves at 4 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.

Eₖ = ½mv² = ½ × 15 × 4²
= 7.5 × 16 = 120 JAnswer: 120 J.

15. A car of mass 1500 kg increases its speed from 30 km/h to 60 km/h. Find the work done.

  1. Initial speed = 30 × 5/18 = 25/3 m/s.
  2. Final speed = 60 × 5/18 = 50/3 m/s.
  3. Work done = change in kinetic energy.
W = ½m(v² − u²)
= ½ × 1500 × [(50/3)² − (25/3)²]
= 156,250 JAnswer: 156,250 J.

16. What is the potential energy of an object?

It is the energy possessed by an object because of its position or configuration (shape).

17. How does a stretched rubber band get energy?

We do work while stretching it. This energy is stored in the rubber band as potential energy.

18. How does a toy car get energy when its key is wound?

Work is done while winding the key. Energy is stored in the spring as potential energy. When released, this energy changes into kinetic energy and the car moves.

19. What happens when an object is lifted to a greater height?

Its gravitational potential energy increases because its height increases.

20. Find the potential energy of a 10 kg object at a height of 6 m. Take g = 9.8 m/s².

Eₚ = mgh = 10 × 9.8 × 6 = 588 JAnswer: 588 J.

21. An object of mass 12 kg has potential energy 480 J. Find its height. Take g = 10 m/s².

Eₚ = mgh
480 = 12 × 10 × h
h = 480/120 = 4 mAnswer: 4 m.

22. What is the law of conservation of energy?

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only change from one form to another. The total energy remains the same.

23. What happens to the potential and kinetic energy of a freely falling object?

  1. At the top, potential energy is maximum and kinetic energy is zero if the object is released from rest.
  2. As it falls, potential energy decreases.
  3. Its kinetic energy increases.
  4. Ignoring air resistance, the sum of both energies remains constant.

24. What is the relationship between kinetic energy and potential energy during free fall?

mgh + ½mv² = constantThe loss of potential energy is equal to the gain in kinetic energy when air resistance is ignored.

25. What is the power of an agent?

Power tells us how quickly an agent does work or transfers energy. It is work divided by time.

26. Two girls climb a rope to a height of 8 m. Each has a weight of 400 N. Girl A takes 20 s and Girl B takes 50 s. Find their powers.

  1. Work done by each = weight × height = 400 × 8 = 3200 J.
  2. Girl A: P = 3200/20 = 160 W.
  3. Girl B: P = 3200/50 = 64 W.
Girl A has greater power because she does the same work in less time.

27. A boy of mass 50 kg runs up 45 steps in 9 s. Each step is 15 cm high. Find his power. Take g = 10 m/s².

  1. Height = 45 × 0.15 = 6.75 m.
  2. Work = mgh = 50 × 10 × 6.75 = 3375 J.
  3. Power = 3375/9 = 375 W.
Answer: 375 W.

28. A 60 W bulb is used for 6 hours per day. Find the energy used in one day in units.

60 W = 0.06 kW
Energy = Power × Time = 0.06 × 6 = 0.36 kWhAnswer: 0.36 unit.

Textbook Exercise — Questions and Answers

1. Decide whether work is done in each situation. Give reasons.

  1. Suma is swimming in a pond: Yes. She applies force and moves through the water.
  2. A donkey carries a load on its back: In the usual situation of walking on a level road, the upward force on the load is perpendicular to its horizontal displacement. So the work done by the carrying force on the load is zero.
  3. A windmill lifts water from a well: Yes. The windmill applies force and the water moves upwards.
  4. A green plant carries out photosynthesis: No mechanical work is done on an object by a force causing displacement in this description. The plant converts light energy into chemical energy.
  5. An engine pulls a train: Yes. The pulling force moves the train.
  6. Food grains dry in the sun: No mechanical work is described. Heat from sunlight helps water evaporate.
  7. A sailboat moves due to wind energy: Yes. Wind applies force and the boat moves.

2. An object is thrown at an angle to the ground. It moves along a curved path and returns to the ground. The initial and final points are on the same horizontal line. What is the work done by gravity?

  1. Gravity acts vertically downwards.
  2. The object starts and ends at the same height.
  3. So the net vertical displacement is zero.
Work done by gravity = mg × (change in height) = 0Answer: Zero joule.

3. A battery lights a bulb. Describe the energy changes.

  1. Chemical energy in the battery changes into electrical energy.
  2. The bulb changes electrical energy into light energy and heat energy.

4. A force acting on a 20 kg mass changes its velocity from 5 m/s to 2 m/s. Calculate the work done.

W = change in kinetic energy
= ½m(v² − u²)
= ½ × 20 × (2² − 5²)
= 10 × (4 − 25) = −210 JAnswer: −210 J. The negative sign means the force removes kinetic energy.

5. A mass of 10 kg moves from point A to point B on a horizontal table. What is the work done by gravity?

Gravity acts vertically downwards, but displacement is horizontal. The angle between them is 90°. Therefore, work done by gravity is zero joule.

6. The potential energy of a freely falling object decreases. Does this violate conservation of energy? Why?

No. The lost potential energy changes into kinetic energy. The total mechanical energy remains constant if air resistance is ignored.

7. What are the energy transformations when you ride a bicycle?

  1. Chemical energy from food is used by the muscles.
  2. Muscular energy does work on the pedals.
  3. The bicycle gains kinetic energy.
  4. Some energy also changes into heat and sound due to friction.

8. Does energy transfer take place when you push a huge rock but it does not move? Where does your energy go?

  1. No mechanical work is done on the rock because it does not move.
  2. Your muscles still use chemical energy.
  3. Much of this energy changes into heat inside your body.

9. A household uses 250 units of electrical energy in one month. How much energy is this in joules?

1 unit = 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
250 units = 250 × 3.6 × 10⁶
= 9 × 10⁸ JAnswer: 9 × 10⁸ J.

10. A 40 kg object is raised to a height of 5 m. Find its potential energy. If it falls, find its kinetic energy when it is halfway down. Take g = 10 m/s².

  1. Initial potential energy = mgh = 40 × 10 × 5 = 2000 J.
  2. Halfway down, its height is 2.5 m. Remaining potential energy = 40 × 10 × 2.5 = 1000 J.
  3. Loss of potential energy = 2000 − 1000 = 1000 J.
  4. Ignoring air resistance, this becomes kinetic energy.
Answer: Potential energy initially = 2000 J; kinetic energy halfway down = 1000 J.

11. What is the work done by gravity on a satellite moving around the Earth? Justify your answer.

For a circular orbit, gravity acts towards the centre of the Earth while the satellite's instantaneous displacement is along the tangent. The force is perpendicular to displacement. Therefore, gravity does zero work on the satellite in a circular orbit.

12. Can an object be displaced when no force acts on it? Think and explain.

Yes. An object that is already moving can continue to move with constant velocity in a straight line if no net force acts on it. This follows from Newton's first law of motion.

13. A person holds a bundle of hay over his head for 30 minutes and gets tired. Has he done work on the bundle?

No mechanical work is done on the bundle because it has no displacement. The person gets tired because his muscles use energy to hold it in position.

14. An electric heater is rated 1500 W. How much energy does it use in 10 hours?

1500 W = 1.5 kW
Energy = Power × Time = 1.5 × 10 = 15 kWhAnswer: 15 units = 15 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = 5.4 × 10⁷ J.

15. Explain conservation of energy in a pendulum. Why does it finally come to rest? Is energy destroyed?

  1. At the highest point, the bob has maximum potential energy and minimum kinetic energy.
  2. As it moves down, potential energy changes into kinetic energy.
  3. At the lowest point, kinetic energy is maximum.
  4. As it rises, kinetic energy changes back into potential energy.
  5. Air resistance and friction gradually change mechanical energy into heat and sound, so the pendulum stops.
  6. Energy is not destroyed. It is transferred to the surroundings in other forms.

16. An object of mass m moves with constant velocity v. How much work should be done to bring it to rest?

  1. Initial kinetic energy = ½mv².
  2. Final kinetic energy = 0.
  3. Work done = change in kinetic energy.
W = 0 − ½mv² = −½mv²The required work by the stopping force is negative. Its magnitude is ½mv².

17. Calculate the work required to stop a car of mass 1500 kg moving at 60 km/h.

  1. Speed = 60 × 5/18 = 50/3 m/s.
  2. Final kinetic energy = 0.
W = −½mv²
= −½ × 1500 × (50/3)²
= −208,333.3 J (approximately)Answer: −2.08 × 10⁵ J. The stopping force does negative work. The magnitude is about 2.08 × 10⁵ J.

18. In each diagram, decide whether work done by force F is positive, negative or zero. Displacement is from west to east.

  1. First diagram: Force is vertical while displacement is horizontal. The angle is 90°, so work is zero.
  2. Second diagram: Force and displacement point in the same direction. Work is positive.
  3. Third diagram: Force acts opposite to displacement. Work is negative.

19. Can acceleration be zero even when several forces act on an object? Do you agree?

Yes. If the forces balance each other, their resultant force is zero. Then acceleration is zero. The object may remain at rest or move with constant velocity.

20. Find the energy in kWh used in 10 hours by four devices of power 500 W each.

  1. Total power = 4 × 500 = 2000 W = 2 kW.
  2. Energy = power × time = 2 × 10 = 20 kWh.
Answer: 20 units.

21. A freely falling object eventually stops on reaching the ground. What happens to its kinetic energy?

Its kinetic energy changes into other forms of energy, such as heat, sound and energy used to deform the object or the ground. It is transferred to the surroundings; it is not destroyed.

Quick Revision

Remember these points

  1. Work needs force and displacement.
  2. Work = force × displacement in the direction of force.
  3. Work and energy are measured in joules.
  4. Kinetic energy is due to motion: ½mv².
  5. Potential energy is due to position or shape; at a height, mgh.
  6. Power is work done per second and is measured in watts.
  7. 1 unit of electricity = 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
  8. Energy changes form, but total energy is conserved.

Note: In questions about satellites, the zero-work answer applies to circular motion where gravity is perpendicular to the instantaneous displacement. In a non-circular orbit, gravity can do work over parts of the motion.
