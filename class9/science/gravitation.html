Gravitation | Questions and Answers

Simple-English answers to the in-text questions and all textbook exercise questions visible in the supplied screenshots. Numerical answers show the main formula and working.

Important Concepts and Formulae

1. Universal law of gravitation

Every object in the universe attracts every other object. The force is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.F = Gm₁m₂/r²Here, G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg².

2. Free fall and acceleration due to gravity

Free fall is the motion of an object when gravity is the only force acting on it. Near Earth's surface, acceleration due to gravity is approximately g = 9.8 m/s² downward. Ignoring air resistance, objects of different masses fall with the same acceleration.

3. Equations of motion under gravity

v = u + gts = ut + ½gt²v² = u² + 2gsChoose downward as positive for a falling object. For an object thrown upward, if upward is positive, use acceleration a = −g.

4. Mass and weight

Mass is the amount of matter in an object and is measured in kilograms. Weight is the gravitational force acting on the object: W = mg, measured in newtons. Mass stays the same from place to place, but weight changes with gravity.

5. Thrust and pressure

Thrust is force acting perpendicular to a surface. Pressure is thrust per unit area: Pressure = Thrust / AreaSI unit: pascal (Pa), equal to N/m². For the same force, a smaller area produces greater pressure.

6. Buoyancy and Archimedes' principle

Buoyancy is the upward force a fluid exerts on an object immersed in it. Archimedes' principle states that an immersed object experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it. An object generally floats if its average density is less than the fluid's density, and sinks if its density is greater.

7. Density and relative density

Density = Mass / VolumeRelative density = Density of substance / Density of waterRelative density has no unit because it is a ratio.

In-text Questions

1. State the universal law of gravitation.

Every object attracts every other object with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres. The force acts along the line joining the centres.

2. Write the formula for gravitational force between Earth and an object on its surface.

F = GMm/R²Here M is Earth's mass, m is the object's mass and R is Earth's radius. Near the surface, this force is the object's weight, mg.

3. Calculate the gravitational force between Earth and the Moon. Earth mass = 6 × 10²⁴ kg, Moon mass = 7.4 × 10²² kg, distance = 3.84 × 10⁵ km, G = 6.7 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg².

Distance = 3.84 × 10⁸ m.F = G Mm/r² = (6.7×10⁻¹¹)(6×10²⁴)(7.4×10²²)/(3.84×10⁸)²F ≈ 2.0 × 10²⁰ N.

4. Why does the Earth not move noticeably towards the Moon, even though the Moon attracts it?

The Earth and Moon pull each other with equal forces. But Earth's mass is much greater, so its acceleration is very small. Both bodies move around their common centre of mass.

5. Why do tides occur due to the Moon and the Sun?

The gravitational attraction of the Moon and the Sun pulls on Earth's oceans. This causes the sea level to rise and fall, producing tides.

6. What happens to gravitational force if (i) one mass doubles, (ii) distance doubles and then triples, (iii) both masses double?

(i) Force doubles. (ii) At twice the distance, force becomes 1/4; at three times the distance, it becomes 1/9. (iii) Force becomes four times as large.

7. What is the importance of the universal law of gravitation?

It explains the force that binds us to Earth, the Moon's motion around Earth, planets' motion around the Sun, and tides due to the Moon and Sun.

8. What is acceleration due to gravity?

It is the acceleration of an object when it falls under Earth's gravitational force alone. Near Earth's surface, it is about 9.8 m/s² downward.

9. What do we call the gravitational force between Earth and an object?

It is called the object's weight.

10. Why might a gold buyer's friend disagree with the weight of gold bought at the poles when measured at the equator?

Weight depends on g. Since g is slightly greater at the poles than at the equator, the same gold has slightly less weight at the equator. Its mass remains unchanged.

11. Why does a sheet of paper fall more slowly than a crumpled paper ball?

The flat sheet experiences more air resistance because it has a larger surface area. The crumpled paper experiences less air resistance and falls faster.

12. Moon gravity is 1/6 of Earth's. Find the weight of a 10 kg object on the Moon and Earth.

Earth weight = mg = 10×9.8 = 98 N. Moon weight = 98/6 = 16.3 N (approximately).

13. A ball is thrown vertically upward at 49 m/s. Find (i) maximum height and (ii) total time to return.

At the highest point v = 0 and g = 9.8 m/s².Height = u²/(2g) = 49²/(2×9.8) = 122.5 mTime to rise = u/g = 5 s; total time = 10 s

14. A stone is released from a tower 19.6 m high. Find its speed just before impact.

u = 0, h = 19.6 m.v² = 2gh = 2×9.8×19.6; v = 19.6 m/s downward

15. A stone is thrown upward at 40 m/s. Take g = 10 m/s². Find maximum height, net displacement and total distance.

Maximum height = u²/(2g) = 1600/20 = 80 m. It returns to its starting point, so net displacement = 0 m and total distance = 80 + 80 = 160 m.

16. Find the gravitational force between Earth and Sun. Earth mass = 6 × 10²⁴ kg, Sun mass = 2 × 10³⁰ kg, distance = 1.5 × 10¹¹ m.

F = GMm/r² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(6×10²⁴)(2×10³⁰)/(1.5×10¹¹)²F ≈ 3.56 × 10²² N.

17. A stone is dropped from a 100 m tower while another is thrown upward from the ground at 25 m/s. When and where do they meet?

Both have the same downward acceleration, so their relative acceleration is zero. Initial separation is 100 m and their closing speed is 25 m/s. Time = 100/25 = 4 s. Height of the upward-thrown stone then = 25×4 − ½×9.8×4² = 21.6 m above ground.

18. A ball thrown upward returns to the thrower after 6 s. Find (a) initial velocity, (b) maximum height, (c) position after 4 s. Take g = 9.8 m/s².

(a) Time up = 3 s, so u = gt = 9.8×3 = 29.4 m/s. (b) Height = u²/(2g) = 44.1 m. (c) Height after 4 s = ut − ½gt² = 29.4×4 − 4.9×16 = 39.2 m above the starting point.

19. In what direction does buoyant force act on an object immersed in a liquid?

Vertically upward, opposite to the force of gravity.

20. Why does a plastic block released under water rise to the surface?

Its average density is lower than water, so the buoyant force is greater than its weight. The net force is upward.

21. A substance has mass 50 g and volume 20 cm³. Will it float or sink in water of density 1 g/cm³?

Density = 50/20 = 2.5 g/cm³. This is greater than water's density, so it sinks.

22. A sealed packet has mass 500 g and volume 350 cm³. Will it float or sink in water? What mass of water would it displace if fully submerged?

Packet density = 500/350 ≈ 1.43 g/cm³, greater than water, so it sinks. If fully submerged, it displaces 350 cm³ of water, whose mass is 350 g.

Questions on Mass, Weight, Pressure and Buoyancy

1. What is the difference between mass and weight?

Mass measures the amount of matter and is measured in kg; it does not change with location. Weight is the gravitational force, W = mg, measured in newtons; it changes when g changes.

2. Why is an object's weight on the Moon one-sixth of its weight on Earth?

The Moon's gravitational acceleration is approximately one-sixth of Earth's. Since W = mg and the object's mass is unchanged, its weight is one-sixth as much.

3. Why is it difficult to hold a school bag with a thin, strong strap?

The same force acts over a smaller area, creating greater pressure on the hand or shoulder and causing discomfort.

4. What is buoyancy?

Buoyancy is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an object partly or fully immersed in it.

5. Why does an object float or sink in water?

An object floats when the buoyant force can balance its weight; this generally happens when its average density is less than water's. An object with density greater than water usually sinks.

6. You find your mass to be 42 kg on a weighing machine. Is your mass more or less than 42 kg?

Your mass is approximately 42 kg, subject to the machine's accuracy. Mass does not depend on location; a weighing machine may infer mass from your weight.

7. A cotton bag and an iron bar each show 100 kg on a weighing machine. Which is heavier?

If both readings are taken accurately under the same conditions, they have equal weight readings. However, air buoyancy affects cotton more because it displaces more air, so the true masses may not be exactly equal; the iron bar's true mass can be slightly greater.

8. Example: A 5 kg wooden block measures 40 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm. Find pressure on the table when placed on (a) 20 cm × 10 cm side, (b) 40 cm × 20 cm side.

Weight = mg = 5×9.8 = 49 N. (a) Area = 0.2×0.1 = 0.02 m²; pressure = 49/0.02 = 2450 Pa. (b) Area = 0.4×0.2 = 0.08 m²; pressure = 49/0.08 = 612.5 Pa.

9. What is relative density? Example: relative density of silver is 10.8 and water density is 10³ kg/m³. Find silver density.

Relative density = substance density / water density. Therefore silver density = 10.8×10³ = 10,800 kg/m³.

10. Why does a cork float and an iron nail sink in water?

Cork is less dense than water, so the buoyant force can balance its weight before it is fully submerged. Iron is denser than water, so its weight is greater than the buoyant force when submerged and it sinks.

For falling objects, this chapter often ignores air resistance. Real objects can fall differently when air resistance is significant.
