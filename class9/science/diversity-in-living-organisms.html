Class 9 Science — Chapter 7

Diversity in Living Organisms | Questions and Answers

Simple-English answers to the in-text questions and all six end-of-chapter exercise questions visible in the supplied screenshots.

In-text Questions

1. Why do we classify organisms?

Answer: We classify organisms to organise the huge variety of living things into groups. It makes their study, identification and comparison easier.

2. Give three examples of the range of variations that you see in life forms around you.

Answer: Examples include differences in size (bacteria and trees), habitat (fish in water and birds in air) and body structure (plants, insects and mammals).

3. Which is a more basic characteristic for classifying organisms: (a) the place where they live, or (b) the kind of cells they are made of? Why?

Answer: The kind of cells they are made of is more basic. Habitat can change or be shared by unrelated organisms, while cell type—such as prokaryotic or eukaryotic—reflects a fundamental difference in body organisation.

4. What is the primary characteristic on which the broad division of organisms is made?

Answer: The broad division is based mainly on the basic type of cell: whether the organism is prokaryotic or eukaryotic. Cell organisation and other major features are also considered.

5. On what basis are plants and animals put into different categories?

Answer: They are classified using differences in body design and organisation, mode of nutrition, and other structural and functional features. Plants generally make their own food, while animals obtain food from other organisms.

6. Which organisms are called primitive and how are they different from advanced organisms?

Answer: Primitive organisms have a simpler body design and fewer specialised structures. Advanced organisms have a more complex, specialised body organisation. “Primitive” does not mean that an organism is unsuccessful or inferior.

7. Will advanced organisms be the same as complex organisms? Why?

Answer: In this chapter’s context, advanced organisms generally have more complex body designs. Evolution does not mean that every organism becomes more complex; simple organisms can also survive successfully.

8. What is the criterion for classifying organisms as belonging to kingdom Monera or Protista?

Answer: The main criterion is cell type. Monera contains prokaryotic organisms whose cells lack a true nucleus, while Protista contains unicellular eukaryotes with a well-defined nucleus.

9. In which kingdom will you place an organism that is single-celled, eukaryotic and photosynthetic?

Answer: Kingdom Protista, for example a unicellular photosynthetic organism such as Chlamydomonas.

10. In the hierarchy of classification, which group has the smallest number of organisms with the maximum common characteristics, and which has the largest number?

Answer: Species is the smallest group and its members share many characteristics. Kingdom is the broadest group and contains the largest number of organisms.

11. Which division among plants has the simplest organisms?

Answer: Thallophyta. These plants have a simple body that is not differentiated into true roots, stems and leaves.

12. How are pteridophytes different from phanerogams?

Answer: Pteridophytes have vascular tissues but do not produce seeds; they reproduce by spores. Phanerogams produce seeds and include gymnosperms and angiosperms.

13. How do gymnosperms and angiosperms differ from each other?

Answer: Gymnosperms produce naked seeds that are not enclosed in fruits. Angiosperms are flowering plants whose seeds are enclosed within fruits.

14. How do poriferan animals differ from coelenterate animals?

Answer: Poriferans, such as sponges, have many pores through which water flows and generally lack true tissues. Coelenterates (cnidarians), such as Hydra, have organised tissues, a body cavity and tentacles with stinging cells.

15. How do annelid animals differ from arthropods?

Answer: Annelids have segmented bodies but no jointed legs or hard external skeleton. Arthropods have jointed appendages and a hard exoskeleton.

Textbook Exercises

1. What are the advantages of classifying organisms?

Answer:

  • It makes the study of living organisms easier and systematic.
  • It helps us identify organisms.
  • It helps us understand similarities and differences between organisms.
  • It helps us understand evolutionary relationships.

2. How would you choose between two characteristics to be used for developing a hierarchy in classification?

Answer: Choose the characteristic that reflects a more basic difference in body design and has greater importance in evolution. For example, cell type is more basic than habitat because organisms living in the same habitat may have very different cell structures.

3. Explain the basis for grouping organisms into five kingdoms.

Answer: R. H. Whittaker grouped organisms into Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia using these main criteria:

  1. Cell type: prokaryotic or eukaryotic.
  2. Number of cells: unicellular or multicellular.
  3. Presence or absence of a cell wall.
  4. Mode of nutrition: making food, absorbing it or ingesting it.
  5. Body organisation and evolutionary relationships.

4. What are the major divisions in the Plantae? What is the basis for these divisions?

Answer: The major divisions are:

  1. Thallophyta: body not differentiated into true roots, stems and leaves.
  2. Bryophyta: simple land plants without well-developed vascular tissues.
  3. Pteridophyta: have vascular tissues and true roots, stems and leaves, but no seeds.
  4. Gymnosperms: seed-producing plants with naked seeds.
  5. Angiosperms: flowering plants with seeds enclosed in fruits.

The divisions are based on body differentiation, vascular tissues, seed formation and whether seeds are enclosed in fruits.

5. How are the criteria for deciding divisions in plants different from the criteria for deciding the subgroups among animals?

Answer: Plant divisions are based on features such as body structure, vascular tissues, seed formation and flowers. Animal subgroups are based on features such as body symmetry, body segmentation, body cavity, skeleton, backbone and other body-organisation features.

6. Explain how animals in Vertebrata are classified into further subgroups.

Answer: Vertebrates are classified into five main groups:

  1. Pisces: fish; aquatic animals that breathe mainly through gills.
  2. Amphibia: can live in water and on land; skin is generally moist.
  3. Reptilia: dry, scaly skin; most lay eggs on land.
  4. Aves: birds with feathers and forelimbs modified into wings.
  5. Mammalia: have hair and mammary glands; most give birth to young ones.

These groups are distinguished by features such as habitat, skin covering, limbs, respiration, reproduction and body temperature regulation.
