Class 9 Science — Chapter 1

Matter in Our Surroundings | Questions and Answers

Answers are written in simple English from the questions visible in the textbook screenshots supplied for this chapter.

In-text Questions — Page 3

1. Which of the following are matter: chair, air, love, smell, hate, almonds, thought, cold, lemon water, smell of perfume?

Answer: Chair, air, almonds, lemon water and the smell of perfume are considered matter. They are made of particles and have mass and occupy space. Love, hate and thought are feelings or ideas, while cold is a sensation.

2. Why does the smell of hot sizzling food reach us several metres away, while we must go close to smell cold food?

Answer: At a higher temperature, smell particles have more kinetic energy and diffuse faster through air. Therefore, the smell of hot food reaches us from farther away.

3. A diver can cut through water in a swimming pool. Which property of matter does this show?

Answer: It shows that particles of matter have spaces between them and that the force of attraction between liquid particles is not very strong. The particles can move aside when the diver passes through water.

4. What are the characteristics of particles of matter?

Answer:
  1. Particles of matter are extremely small.
  2. There are spaces between the particles.
  3. Particles are continuously moving.
  4. Particles attract one another.

In-text Questions — Page 6

1. Arrange these in increasing order of density: air, exhaust from chimneys, honey, water, chalk, cotton and iron.

Answer: Air → exhaust from chimneys → cotton → water → honey → chalk → iron. This is the usual approximate order expected in the textbook exercise.

2(a). Tabulate the differences between the three states of matter.

Answer:
  • Solid: Fixed shape and volume; particles are tightly packed; strong attraction; almost incompressible.
  • Liquid: Fixed volume but no fixed shape; takes the shape of its container; particles can slide past one another.
  • Gas: No fixed shape or volume; fills its container; particles are far apart, move freely and are easily compressed.

2(b). Comment on rigidity, compressibility, fluidity, filling a gas container, shape, kinetic energy and density.

Answer:
  • Rigidity: Ability to keep a fixed shape; solids are rigid.
  • Compressibility: Ability to reduce volume under pressure; gases are highly compressible.
  • Fluidity: Ability to flow; liquids and gases can flow.
  • Filling a container: Gas particles move freely and spread to fill the whole container.
  • Shape: Solids have a fixed shape; liquids and gases take the shape of their container.
  • Kinetic energy: It is generally lowest in solids and highest in gases.
  • Density: Mass per unit volume; generally, solids have higher density than liquids and gases.

3. Give reasons: (a) A gas fills its container completely. (b) A gas exerts pressure on the container walls. (c) A wooden table is a solid. (d) It is easy to move a hand through air but not through a block of wood.

Answer:
  1. Gas particles move rapidly in all directions, so they spread throughout the container.
  2. Gas particles continuously collide with the walls and exert force on them. Force per unit area is pressure.
  3. A wooden table has a fixed shape and volume and is rigid, so it is a solid.
  4. Air particles are far apart and have weak attraction, while particles in wood are tightly packed with strong attraction.

4. Liquids generally have lower density than solids, but ice floats on water. Why?

Answer: Ice has an open structure with more space between its particles than liquid water. Therefore, ice is less dense than water and floats on it.

In-text Questions — Page 9

1. Convert the temperatures to Celsius: (a) 300 K (b) 573 K.

Formula: °C = K − 273.
(a) 300 − 273 = 27°C.
(b) 573 − 273 = 300°C.

2. What is the physical state of water at (a) 250°C and (b) 100°C?

Answer: (a) At 250°C, water is gas (steam) at normal atmospheric pressure. (b) At 100°C, water can exist as both liquid water and steam during boiling at normal atmospheric pressure.

3. Why does temperature remain constant during a change of state?

Answer: The heat supplied is used to overcome the forces of attraction between particles rather than to increase their kinetic energy. This heat is called latent heat, so the temperature remains constant until the change of state is complete.

4. Suggest a method to liquefy atmospheric gases.

Answer: Cool the gases and apply high pressure. Lowering the temperature and increasing the pressure can bring gas particles closer together and convert the gases into liquids.

In-text Questions — Evaporation

1. Why does a desert cooler cool better on a hot, dry day?

Answer: High temperature and low humidity make water evaporate faster. Evaporation takes heat from the surroundings, so the air becomes cooler.

2. How does water in an earthen pot (matka) become cool in summer?

Answer: An earthen pot has tiny pores. A little water comes through these pores and evaporates, taking heat from the remaining water and cooling it.

3. Why does our palm feel cold when we put acetone, petrol or perfume on it?

Answer: These liquids evaporate quickly. During evaporation, they take heat from our palm, making it feel cold.

4. Why can we sip hot tea or milk faster from a saucer than from a cup?

Answer: A saucer spreads the liquid over a larger surface area. This increases evaporation and cools the tea or milk faster.

5. What type of clothes should we wear in summer?

Answer: We should wear light-coloured, loose cotton clothes. Cotton absorbs sweat and helps it evaporate, cooling our body.

Textbook Exercise — Page 12

1. Convert to Celsius: (a) 293 K (b) 470 K.

Answer: °C = K − 273. (a) 293 − 273 = 20°C. (b) 470 − 273 = 197°C.

2. Convert to Kelvin: (a) 25°C (b) 373°C.

Answer: K = °C + 273. (a) 25 + 273 = 298 K. (b) 373 + 273 = 646 K.

3. Give reasons: (a) Naphthalene balls disappear with time without leaving a solid. (b) We can smell perfume several metres away.

Answer: (a) Naphthalene changes directly from solid to vapour by sublimation. (b) Perfume vapour particles diffuse through air and reach our nose.

4. Arrange water, sugar and oxygen in increasing order of force of attraction between particles.

Answer: Oxygen → water → sugar.

5. What is the physical state of water at (a) 25°C (b) 0°C (c) 100°C?

Answer: (a) At 25°C, water is liquid. (b) At 0°C, ice and liquid water can coexist. (c) At 100°C, liquid water and steam can coexist during boiling, at normal atmospheric pressure.

6. Give two reasons to justify: (a) Water at room temperature is a liquid. (b) An iron almirah is a solid at room temperature.

Answer: (a) Water has a fixed volume but no fixed shape; it flows and takes the shape of its container. (b) An iron almirah has a fixed shape and volume and is rigid.

7. Why is ice at 273 K more effective in cooling than water at the same temperature?

Answer: Ice absorbs additional heat from its surroundings to melt. This latent heat of fusion makes ice remove more heat than water at the same temperature.

8. What produces more severe burns: boiling water or steam?

Answer: Steam produces more severe burns because it releases extra latent heat when it condenses on the skin.

9. Name A, B, C, D, E and F in the diagram showing changes of state.

Answer: A — Melting (solid to liquid); B — Vaporisation (liquid to gas); C — Condensation (gas to liquid); D — Freezing/solidification (liquid to solid); E — Sublimation (solid directly to gas); F — Deposition (gas directly to solid).
