Class 9 Science — Chapter 9

Force and Laws of Motion | Questions and Answers

Simple-English answers to the in-text questions, worked examples and exercise questions visible in the supplied textbook screenshots. Calculations use SI units unless stated otherwise.

Important Concepts and Formulae

1. Force

A force is a push or pull. It can change an object's speed, direction, state of rest or motion, or shape. Forces may be balanced or unbalanced.

2. Balanced and unbalanced forces

Balanced forces: Equal and opposite forces have zero resultant force, so they do not change the state of motion. Unbalanced forces: The resultant force is not zero and changes an object's velocity.

3. Newton's first law and inertia

An object remains at rest or continues moving with uniform velocity in a straight line unless an unbalanced external force acts on it. Inertia is the tendency to resist a change in motion. Mass measures inertia; greater mass means greater inertia.

4. Momentum

Momentum: p = mvMomentum is the product of mass and velocity. SI unit: kg m/s. Momentum has the same direction as velocity.

5. Newton's second law

The rate of change of momentum is proportional to the applied net force and takes place in the force's direction. For constant mass:F = ma = m(v − u)/tOne newton is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s².

6. Newton's third law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Action and reaction act on two different bodies, so they do not cancel each other on one body.

7. Conservation of momentum

When no external net force acts on a system, its total momentum remains constant. For two objects:m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

In-text Questions

1. Which has more inertia: (a) a rubber ball or a stone of the same size, (b) a bicycle or a train, (c) a five-rupee coin or a one-rupee coin?

(a) The stone, because it has greater mass. (b) The train, because it has greater mass. (c) The five-rupee coin, assuming it has greater mass than the one-rupee coin.

2. A football is kicked by one player to a teammate, kicked towards the goal, collected by the goalkeeper, and kicked towards a player of the goalkeeper's team. How many times does its velocity change? Who supplies the force?

The velocity changes four times: when the first player kicks it, when the teammate kicks it, when the goalkeeper stops/catches it, and when the goalkeeper kicks it again. The players and the goalkeeper supply the forces.

3. Why may leaves detach from a tree when its branch is shaken vigorously?

The branch moves suddenly, but the leaves tend to remain in their state of rest due to inertia. This relative motion can break the leaf stalks, causing the leaves to fall.

4. Why do you fall forward when a moving bus brakes and backward when it starts suddenly?

When the bus brakes, the lower part of the body slows with the bus while the upper part tends to keep moving forward. When the bus starts, the lower part moves forward but the upper part tends to remain at rest, so we fall backward.

Worked Examples Shown in the Screenshots

Example 9.1 — A 5 kg object speeds up from 3 m/s to 7 m/s in 2 s. Find force. What is the final speed if the same force acts for 5 s?

F = m(v − u)/t = 5(7 − 3)/2 = 10 NFor 5 s, v = u + Ft/m = 3 + (10×5)/5 = 13 m/s.

Example 9.2 — Which requires greater force: 2 kg at 5 m/s² or 4 kg at 2 m/s²?

F₁ = 2×5 = 10 N. F₂ = 4×2 = 8 N. Therefore, accelerating the 2 kg mass at 5 m/s² requires the greater force.

Example 9.3 — A 1000 kg motorcar moving at 108 km/h stops in 4 s. Find braking force.

108 km/h = 30 m/s. a = (0−30)/4 = −7.5 m/s². F = ma = 1000×(−7.5) = −7500 N. The force acts opposite to the motion.

Example 9.4 — A 5 N force gives accelerations of 10 m/s² and 20 m/s² to two separate masses. Find their combined acceleration when tied together.

m₁ = F/a₁ = 5/10 = 0.50 kg; m₂ = 5/20 = 0.25 kg. Combined mass = 0.75 kg. Acceleration = F/m = 5/0.75 = 6.67 m/s².

Example 9.5 — A 20 g ball slows from 20 cm/s to rest in 10 s. Find the force of the table.

Mass = 0.020 kg; acceleration = (0−0.20)/10 = −0.020 m/s². F = ma = 0.020×(−0.020) = −0.0004 N. The negative sign means the frictional force opposes the motion.

Example 9.6 — A 20 g bullet travels at 150 m/s from a 2 kg pistol. Find recoil velocity.

Initial total momentum is zero. By conservation of momentum: 0.02×150 + 2v = 0. Therefore v = −1.5 m/s. The pistol recoils in the direction opposite to the bullet.

Example 9.7 — A 40 kg girl jumps at 5 m/s onto a stationary 3 kg cart. Find their common velocity.

Initial momentum = 40×5 + 3×0 = 200 kg m/s. Combined mass = 43 kg. v = 200/43 = 4.65 m/s, in the direction of the jump.

Two hockey players collide and move together: masses 60 kg and 55 kg; initial velocities +5 m/s and −6 m/s. Find their final velocity.

Initial momentum = 60×5 + 55×(−6) = −30 kg m/s. Combined mass = 115 kg. v = −30/115 = −0.26 m/s. They move in the second player's original direction.

Questions after the section on Inertia

1. Which has more inertia: a stone or rubber ball of the same size; a bicycle or train; a five-rupee or one-rupee coin?

The stone, the train, and (assuming the usual masses) the five-rupee coin have more inertia because each has greater mass than its comparison object.

2. In the football example, identify the number of velocity changes and the agents supplying force.

There are four changes: the first kick, the second kick, the goalkeeper stopping the ball, and the goalkeeper's kick. The players and goalkeeper provide the forces.

3. Why do leaves detach when a branch is shaken?

Due to inertia, leaves tend to remain at rest while the branch moves suddenly. The leaf stalks may break.

4. Why do passengers fall forward when a bus brakes and backward when it accelerates from rest?

Because of inertia of motion in the first case and inertia of rest in the second case.

Questions after the section on Newton's Third Law

1. If action is always equal to reaction, how can a horse pull a cart?

The horse pushes the ground backward, and the ground pushes the horse forward by friction. This forward force can pull the cart. The action and reaction act on different bodies.

2. Why is it difficult for a fireman to hold a hose that ejects a large amount of water at high velocity?

The water is pushed forward at high speed. By Newton's third law, the hose experiences an equal and opposite backward force, so the fireman must resist it.

3. A 4 kg rifle fires a 50 g bullet at 35 m/s. Find the rifle's recoil velocity.

Bullet mass = 0.05 kg. Initial momentum is zero. 0.05×35 + 4v = 0, so v = −1.75/4 = −0.4375 m/s. The rifle recoils backwards.

4. Objects A and B have masses 100 g and 200 g and move in the same direction at 2 m/s and 1 m/s. After collision, B moves at 1.67 m/s. Find A's velocity.

Using momentum conservation: (0.1×2) + (0.2×1) = 0.1v + (0.2×1.67). Thus 0.4 = 0.1v + 0.334, so v ≈ 0.66 m/s in the original direction.

Textbook Exercises

1. Can an object with zero net external unbalanced force have non-zero velocity?

Yes. It can move with a constant non-zero velocity in a straight line. The velocity's magnitude and direction remain constant.

2. Why does dust come out when a carpet is beaten with a stick?

The carpet moves suddenly, but dust particles tend to remain at rest due to inertia. They separate from the carpet and fall off.

3. Why should luggage on a bus roof be tied with a rope?

When the bus starts, stops, or turns suddenly, the luggage tends to maintain its previous state of motion due to inertia. A rope helps prevent it from sliding or falling.

4. A cricket ball rolls on level ground and stops. Choose the correct reason.

(c) There is a force on the ball opposing the motion. Friction between the ball and ground, along with air resistance, slows it down.

5. A truck starts from rest and travels 400 m in 20 s with constant acceleration. Find acceleration and force if its mass is 7 tonnes.

s = ut + ½at²; 400 = ½a×400, so a = 2 m/s². Mass = 7000 kg. F = ma = 7000×2 = 14,000 N.

6. A 1 kg stone moving at 20 m/s stops after 50 m on ice. Find friction force.

v² = u² + 2as gives 0 = 400 + 100a, so a = −4 m/s². F = ma = −4 N; friction magnitude is 4 N opposite to motion.

7. An 8000 kg engine pulls five wagons of 2000 kg each. Engine force is 40,000 N and friction is 5000 N. Find net force and acceleration.

Net force = 40,000−5,000 = 35,000 N. Total mass = 8000 + 5×2000 = 18,000 kg. a = 35,000/18,000 = 1.94 m/s².

8. A 1500 kg car must stop with acceleration −1.7 m/s². Find the braking force.

F = ma = 1500×(−1.7) = −2550 N. Magnitude = 2550 N, opposite to motion.

9. What is the momentum of an object of mass m moving with velocity v?

(d) mv.

10. A 200 N force moves a cabinet at constant velocity. What is the friction force?

Net force is zero at constant velocity, so friction is 200 N opposite to the applied force.

11. Two 1.5 kg objects move in opposite directions at 2.5 m/s and stick together. Find their final velocity.

Their equal and opposite momenta cancel: 1.5×2.5 + 1.5×(−2.5) = 0. Combined mass = 3 kg, so final velocity = 0 m/s.

12. Why is the argument that action and reaction cancel on a parked truck incorrect?

Action and reaction act on different bodies, so they do not cancel each other on the truck. The truck stays at rest because the applied push is balanced by other forces, including static friction with the road, until the push is strong enough to produce a net force.

13. A 200 g hockey ball moving at 10 m/s is returned along its path at 5 m/s. Find change in momentum.

Take the original direction as positive. m = 0.2 kg, u = +10 m/s, v = −5 m/s. Δp = m(v−u) = 0.2(−15) = −3 kg m/s. Magnitude = 3 kg m/s.

14. A 10 g bullet moving at 150 m/s stops in 0.03 s inside a wooden block. Find penetration distance and force on the bullet.

Average velocity = (150+0)/2 = 75 m/s. Distance = 75×0.03 = 2.25 m. Force = 0.01×(0−150)/0.03 = −50 N, opposite to the bullet's motion.

15. A 1 kg object moving at 10 m/s sticks to a stationary 5 kg block. Find momentum before and after, and final velocity.

Momentum before = 1×10 = 10 kg m/s. Momentum after is also 10 kg m/s. Combined mass = 6 kg, so v = 10/6 = 1.67 m/s.

16. A 100 kg object speeds up from 5 m/s to 8 m/s in 6 s. Find initial and final momentum and force.

Initial momentum = 100×5 = 500 kg m/s. Final momentum = 100×8 = 800 kg m/s. F = m(v−u)/t = 100×3/6 = 50 N.

17. Comment on the claims about a motorcar and an insect colliding.

The motorcar and insect exert equal and opposite forces on each other. They experience equal and opposite changes in momentum over the same contact time. The insect has much smaller mass, so its acceleration and velocity change are much larger, which usually causes severe damage to it.

18. A 10 kg dumb-bell falls 80 cm. Take downward acceleration as 10 m/s². How much momentum does it transfer to the floor?

h = 0.8 m. v² = 2gh = 2×10×0.8 = 16, so v = 4 m/s downward. Momentum just before impact = mv = 10×4 = 40 kg m/s downward. (The momentum transferred on impact depends on the stopping process; 40 kg m/s is the incoming momentum.)

Additional Exercises

A1. Distance-time table: distance = 0, 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343 m at times 0–7 s. (a) Describe acceleration. (b) What can be inferred about force?

The values follow s = t³. Velocity v = 3t² and acceleration a = 6t, so acceleration is increasing. For constant mass, net force F = ma also increases.

A2. Two people push a 1200 kg car at constant velocity; three people produce acceleration 0.2 m/s². Find each person's force.

Let each person's force be F. With two people, friction balances 2F. With three people, net force is 3F−2F = F = ma = 1200×0.2 = 240 N. Each person pushes with 240 N.

A3. A 500 g hammer moving at 50 m/s stops a nail in 0.01 s. Find the force of the nail on the hammer.

Mass = 0.5 kg. F = m(v−u)/t = 0.5(0−50)/0.01 = −2500 N. The force is opposite to the hammer's motion.

A4. A 1200 kg car slows from 90 km/h to 18 km/h in 4 s. Find acceleration, change in momentum and force.

u = 25 m/s; v = 5 m/s. a = (5−25)/4 = −5 m/s². Δp = m(v−u) = 1200(−20) = −24,000 kg m/s. F = ma = −6000 N.

Reminder: the sign of a force or momentum change shows direction. A negative result means the direction is opposite to the direction chosen as positive.
