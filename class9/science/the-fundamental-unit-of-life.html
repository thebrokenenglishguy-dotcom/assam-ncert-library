Class 9 Science — Chapter 5

The Fundamental Unit of Life | Questions and Answers

Answers are in simple English. This page covers the in-text questions and all 10 textbook exercise questions visible in the supplied screenshots.

In-text Questions

1. Who discovered cells, and how?

Answer: Robert Hooke discovered cells in 1665. He observed a thin slice of cork through a self-designed microscope and saw tiny box-like compartments. He called them “cells”.

2. Why is the cell called the structural and functional unit of life?

Answer: All living organisms are made of cells. A cell forms the basic structure of an organism and performs essential life processes. Therefore, it is called the structural and functional unit of life.

3. How do substances like CO₂ and water move in and out of the cell? Discuss.

Answer: Carbon dioxide moves across the cell membrane by diffusion, from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration. Water moves through the selectively permeable membrane by osmosis, from a region with more water (dilute solution) to a region with less water (concentrated solution).

4. Why is the plasma membrane called a selectively permeable membrane?

Answer: It allows some substances to pass through it but prevents or limits the passage of other substances. Therefore, it is called selectively permeable.

5. Fill in the gaps in the table showing differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

6. Name the two organelles that contain their own genetic material.

Answer: Mitochondria and plastids (such as chloroplasts).

7. If the organisation of a cell is destroyed due to some physical or chemical influence, what will happen?

Answer: The cell will not be able to perform its functions properly. Its organelles may stop working, and the cell may die.

8. Why are lysosomes known as suicide bags?

Answer: Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes. If a cell is badly damaged, these enzymes may be released and digest the cell itself. For this reason, lysosomes are called suicide bags.

9. Where are proteins synthesised inside the cell?

Answer: Proteins are synthesised by ribosomes.

Textbook Exercises

2. How is a prokaryotic cell different from a eukaryotic cell?

Answer:
  • Prokaryotic cells are generally smaller; eukaryotic cells are generally larger.
  • Prokaryotic cells have no true nucleus or nuclear membrane; eukaryotic cells have a well-defined nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane.
  • Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotes have them.
  • Prokaryotes usually have a single chromosome; eukaryotes have more than one chromosome.

3. What would happen if the plasma membrane ruptures or breaks down?

Answer: The cell contents would leak out, and harmful substances could enter. The cell would lose its ability to maintain its internal environment and would likely die.

4. What would happen to the life of a cell if there was no Golgi apparatus?

Answer: The cell would not be able to properly modify, package and transport many proteins and lipids. The formation of lysosomes and the secretion of materials would also be affected.

5. Which organelle is known as the powerhouse of the cell? Why?

Answer: The mitochondrion is called the powerhouse of the cell because it releases energy from food during cellular respiration and produces ATP, which the cell uses for its activities.

6. Where do the lipids and proteins constituting the cell membrane get synthesised?

Answer: Lipids are mainly synthesised in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER), while proteins are synthesised by ribosomes, many of which are attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER).

7. How does an Amoeba obtain its food?

Answer: Amoeba surrounds food with its pseudopodia and engulfs it. The food is enclosed in a food vacuole, where digestive enzymes break it down.

8. What is osmosis?

Answer: Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration (dilute solution) to a region of lower water concentration (concentrated solution).

9. Carry out the potato-cup osmosis experiment and answer the questions.

(i) Explain why water gathers in the hollowed portions of cups B and C.
In cups B and C, sugar or salt makes the solution inside more concentrated than the surrounding water. Water enters the potato tissue by osmosis and collects in the hollowed portions.

(ii) Why is potato A necessary for this experiment?
Cup A is the control. It has no sugar or salt, so it helps show that water does not collect simply because the potato has been hollowed out.

(iii) Explain why water does not gather in the hollowed portions of cups A and D.
In A, there is no solute to create the concentration difference needed for net osmosis. In D, boiling destroys the normal selectively permeable properties of the cell membranes, so osmosis does not occur properly.

10. Which type of cell division is required for growth and repair of the body, and which type is involved in formation of gametes?

Answer: Mitosis is used for growth and repair of the body. Meiosis produces gametes for sexual reproduction.
