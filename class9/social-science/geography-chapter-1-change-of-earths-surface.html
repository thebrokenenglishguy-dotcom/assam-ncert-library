Exercise — Very Short Answers

1. Give an outline of the distribution of continents and oceans of the world.

The earth's surface is covered by about 71% water and 29% land. The seven continents are Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia. The five oceans are the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic Oceans. The larger landmasses are mainly in the Northern Hemisphere, while the Southern Hemisphere has a larger share of water.

2. Explain why there has been change over the earth's surface.

The earth's surface changes because of two groups of forces: endogenic forces that act from inside the earth, and exogenic forces that act on its surface. Earthquakes, volcanic activity, weathering, erosion and deposition continuously change the landforms.

3. What is meant by exogenic factor? Mention some exogenic factors.

Exogenic factors are natural forces and processes that act on or near the earth's surface. Examples include running water, wind, glaciers, sea waves, temperature changes and living organisms.

4. Why are earthquakes and volcanic eruption called endogenic factors?

They originate from forces and movements inside the earth. Therefore, earthquakes and volcanic eruptions are called endogenic factors.

5. What do you mean by a river basin? Draw a diagram of a river basin.

A river basin is the area of land drained by a main river and all its tributaries. The boundary separating one river basin from another is called a watershed.

6. What is a tributary? Name two major tributaries of the Brahmaputra.

A tributary is a smaller river or stream that joins a larger river. Two major tributaries of the Brahmaputra are the Subansiri and the Manas.

7. Give an outline of river erosion.

River erosion is the wearing away of the river bed and banks by flowing water and the materials carried by it. It takes place through hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution. It can deepen, widen and lengthen a river valley.

8. What do you mean by lateral erosion of a river?

Lateral erosion is the wearing away of the sides or banks of a river channel. It makes the river valley wider.

Descriptive Answers

9. What is ox-bow lake? Draw diagram to show how it is formed.

An ox-bow lake is a crescent-shaped water body formed when a meander loop of a river is cut off from the main channel. Erosion on the outer banks and deposition on the inner banks make the meander neck narrower. During a flood, the river may cut through the neck and take a straighter course. Deposition then seals the ends of the old loop, leaving an ox-bow lake.

10. What is floodplain? Write how it is formed.

A floodplain is a flat, fertile area of land beside a river. During floods, the river overflows its banks and spreads water and fine sediments over nearby land. When the water slows down, it deposits these sediments. Repeated floods gradually build a floodplain.

11. Write how and where sandbars are formed in a river channel.

Sandbars are long or curved deposits of sand and gravel formed inside a river channel. They develop where the river's speed decreases and it can no longer carry all its sediment, such as on the inner side of bends or in wider, shallower parts of the channel.

12. How are the deltas formed?

A delta forms at the mouth of a river when it enters a sea or lake and loses speed. The river deposits the sand, silt and clay it carries. These deposits build up over time, often dividing the river into smaller distributaries and forming a delta-shaped area.

13. Write why the wind action is strong in the deserts.

Wind action is strong in deserts because rainfall is very low, vegetation is sparse and the ground is often dry and covered with loose sand and dust. There are few plants to hold the soil and few obstacles to slow the wind.

14. What is meant by deflation?

Deflation is the process in which wind lifts and removes loose, fine particles such as dust and sand from the ground. It may lower the land surface and leave coarser stones behind.

15. How are the sand dunes formed?

When wind carrying sand meets an obstacle or loses speed, it drops the sand. The deposited sand gradually accumulates into a mound or ridge called a sand dune. Dunes may change their shape and position as the wind blows.

16. What is inselberg?

An inselberg is an isolated hill or rocky mountain that rises sharply above a surrounding plain. It is commonly found in dry regions and may remain after the surrounding softer rocks have been worn away.

17. Define glacier and state how it differs from a river.

A glacier is a large, slowly moving mass of ice formed by the accumulation and compaction of snow over many years. A river is flowing liquid water, usually moving in a channel. A glacier moves very slowly as ice and commonly erodes valleys into a U-shape; a river flows as water and commonly forms V-shaped valleys in its upper course.

18. What is moraine? Draw a diagram to show different types of moraine.

A moraine is a deposit of unsorted rock fragments, sand and clay carried and left behind by a glacier. The main types are lateral moraine (along the sides), medial moraine (where two glaciers join), terminal or end moraine (at the glacier's farthest point) and ground moraine (spread beneath the ice).

19. Write how a glacial horn is formed.

A glacial horn is a sharp, pyramid-shaped mountain peak. It forms when three or more glaciers erode a mountain from different sides. Their cirques cut backwards into the mountain until steep ridges and a pointed peak remain.

20. What is a coast?

A coast is the zone where the land meets the sea or ocean. It includes the shoreline and nearby land and water areas affected by sea waves and tides.

21. What is a beach? State its importance.

A beach is a gently sloping area along the coast covered mainly with sand, pebbles or other loose materials deposited by waves. Beaches are important for tourism and recreation, provide habitats for coastal plants and animals, and can help reduce wave energy and protect the coast from erosion.

22. Write how sea-waves bring about change in the coasts.

Sea waves change coasts through erosion, transportation and deposition. They erode rocks to form sea cliffs, caves, arches and wave-cut platforms. They carry sand and pebbles along the shore and deposit them to form beaches, spits and bars. These processes continually change the coastline.

24. Think and write about the probable changes of the earth's surface in future.

The earth's surface will continue to change. Rivers may widen valleys, shift their courses and build new floodplains and deltas. Wind may move sand dunes in deserts. Glaciers may shrink as temperatures rise, changing mountain landscapes and sea levels. Sea waves may erode some coasts and deposit sand along others. Earthquakes, volcanic eruptions and movements of the earth's crust may also create or change landforms. Human activities such as mining, construction and deforestation will bring further changes.
