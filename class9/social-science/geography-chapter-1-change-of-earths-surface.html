Exercise — Very Short Answers

1. Give an outline of the distribution of continents and oceans of the world.

  1. About 71% of the earth is covered with water.
  2. About 29% of the earth is covered with land.
  3. There are seven continents: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia.
  4. There are five oceans: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic.

2. Explain why there has been change over the earth's surface.

  1. The earth's surface is always changing.
  2. Forces from inside the earth cause earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.
  3. Forces on the surface, like rivers, wind, glaciers and sea waves, also change the land.

3. What is meant by exogenic factor? Mention some exogenic factors.

  1. Exogenic factors are forces that work on the earth's surface.
  2. They break, wear away and move rocks and soil.
  3. Examples: rivers, wind, glaciers and sea waves.

4. Why are earthquakes and volcanic eruption called endogenic factors?

  1. They are caused by forces inside the earth.
  2. They start below the earth's surface.
  3. So, they are called endogenic factors.

5. What do you mean by a river basin? Draw a diagram of a river basin.

  1. A river basin is the area drained by a main river and its tributaries.
  2. All rainwater and streams in this area flow towards the river system.
  3. The high land around it forms its boundary. This boundary is called a watershed.

6. What is a tributary? Name two major tributaries of the Brahmaputra.

  1. A tributary is a small river that joins a bigger river.
  2. Two major tributaries of the Brahmaputra are the Subansiri and the Manas.

7. Give an outline of river erosion.

  1. River erosion means the wearing away of the river bed and banks.
  2. Fast-flowing water breaks and removes rocks and soil.
  3. The river also carries away the broken materials.
  4. River erosion can make a valley deeper and wider.

8. What do you mean by lateral erosion of a river?

  1. Lateral erosion means the wearing away of the sides or banks of a river.
  2. It makes the river valley wider.

Descriptive Answers

9. What is ox-bow lake? Draw diagram to show how it is formed.

  1. An ox-bow lake is a crescent-shaped lake near a river.
  2. It forms when a river bend becomes very large.
  3. The river slowly erodes the outer banks and deposits soil on the inner banks.
  4. During a flood, the river may cut across the narrow neck and leave the old bend separated.
  5. The cut-off bend becomes an ox-bow lake.

10. What is floodplain? Write how it is formed.

  1. A floodplain is a flat area of land beside a river.
  2. During floods, river water spreads over the nearby land.
  3. The water leaves behind fine soil and sand.
  4. After many floods, these deposits form a wide, fertile plain.

11. Write how and where sandbars are formed in a river channel.

  1. A sandbar is a raised deposit of sand in a river channel.
  2. It forms when the river slows down and drops the sand it carries.
  3. Sandbars are often found near river bends or in wide, shallow parts of a river.

12. How are the deltas formed?

  1. A delta forms at the mouth of a river, where it enters a sea or lake.
  2. The river slows down at this point.
  3. It drops the sand, silt and mud that it carries.
  4. These deposits build up and form a delta.
  5. The river may divide into smaller channels called distributaries.

13. Write why the wind action is strong in the deserts.

  1. Deserts receive very little rainfall.
  2. There are very few trees and plants.
  3. The soil and sand are dry and loose.
  4. There are fewer obstacles to stop the wind.
  5. So, wind can easily lift and move sand and dust.

14. What is meant by deflation?

  1. Deflation is the removal of loose dust and sand by wind.
  2. The wind lifts and carries these small particles away.
  3. It may lower the land surface over time.

15. How are the sand dunes formed?

  1. Wind carries loose sand from one place to another.
  2. When the wind slows down or meets an obstacle, it drops the sand.
  3. The sand collects in the form of a mound or ridge.
  4. This mound or ridge is called a sand dune.

16. What is inselberg?

  1. An inselberg is an isolated hill or rocky mountain.
  2. It rises above a flat area of land.
  3. It is often found in dry regions.

17. Define glacier and state how it differs from a river.

  1. A glacier is a large mass of ice that moves slowly down a slope.
  2. It forms when snow collects and turns into ice over many years.
  3. A glacier moves as ice, but a river flows as liquid water.
  4. Glaciers often form U-shaped valleys, while rivers often form V-shaped valleys in their upper course.

18. What is moraine? Draw a diagram to show different types of moraine.

  1. A moraine is a deposit of rocks, stones, sand and soil left by a glacier.
  2. Lateral moraine is found along the sides of a glacier.
  3. Medial moraine is found in the middle when two glaciers join.
  4. Terminal moraine is left at the end of a glacier.
  5. Ground moraine is spread below the glacier.

19. Write how a glacial horn is formed.

  1. A glacial horn is a sharp, pointed mountain peak.
  2. It forms when glaciers move down different sides of a mountain.
  3. They slowly cut and wear away the mountain from different sides.
  4. The remaining peak becomes steep and pointed.

20. What is a coast?

  1. A coast is the area where the land meets the sea or ocean.
  2. It includes the nearby land and the edge of the sea.

21. What is a beach? State its importance.

  1. A beach is a stretch of land along the sea covered with sand or small stones.
  2. People use beaches for отдых and tourism.
  3. Beaches provide homes for some plants and animals.
  4. They can reduce the force of waves and help protect the coast.

22. Write how sea-waves bring about change in the coasts.

  1. Sea waves break and wear away coastal rocks.
  2. They can form sea cliffs, caves and other coastal features.
  3. Waves carry sand and small stones along the coast.
  4. They drop these materials and form beaches and sandbars.

  • Wind: Inselberg, Sand dune.
  • Glacier: Lateral moraine, Horn, U-shaped valley.
  • Sea-wave: Beach, Coast.

24. Think and write about the probable changes of the earth's surface in future.

  1. Rivers may change their courses and form new floodplains and deltas.
  2. Wind may move sand dunes from one place to another.
  3. Glaciers may melt and change mountain areas and sea levels.
  4. Sea waves may wear away some coasts and build beaches in other places.
  5. Earthquakes, volcanoes and human activities may also change the land.
