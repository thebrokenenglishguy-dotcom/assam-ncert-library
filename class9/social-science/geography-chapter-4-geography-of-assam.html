Exercise — Questions and Answers

1. What are the physiographic divisions of Assam?

  1. The Brahmaputra Valley.
  2. The Barak Valley.
  3. The Karbi Anglong Plateau.
  4. The Barail Range and the Southern Hills, including the Dima Hasao region.

2. What are the physiographic regions of the Brahmaputra Valley?

  1. The northern foothill and piedmont belt.
  2. The northern alluvial plain.
  3. The Brahmaputra river and its floodplain, including chars and chaporis.
  4. The southern alluvial plain and foothill areas.
  5. The valley is a long, low-lying plain between the Himalayas and the hills of Meghalaya and Karbi Anglong.

3. Write four names each of the north-bank and south-bank tributaries of the Brahmaputra river.

  1. North-bank tributaries: Subansiri, Ranganadi, Jia Bharali and Manas.
  2. South-bank tributaries: Burhi Dihing, Disang, Dikhow and Dhansiri.

4. Give a brief description of the Brahmaputra plain region.

  1. It is a long and wide alluvial plain in Assam.
  2. The Brahmaputra flows from east to west through this valley.
  3. Its soil is formed by deposits of sand, silt and clay brought by rivers.
  4. It is suitable for farming and supports a large population.
  5. Floods, riverbank erosion and the formation of sandbars are common problems.

5. Give a brief description of the floodplain region of the Brahmaputra.

  1. It is the low land beside the Brahmaputra and its tributaries.
  2. It is covered by water during floods.
  3. Floods deposit fresh and fertile silt on the land.
  4. It contains wetlands, grasslands, chars and chaporis.
  5. It supports farming, fishing and wildlife, but floods can damage houses and crops.

6. Write about the geographic location and origin of Majuli.

  1. Majuli is a large river island in the Brahmaputra in Assam.
  2. It lies mainly between the Brahmaputra's main channel and its northern channel system near the Subansiri.
  3. It was formed over time by river deposition and changes in river channels.
  4. It is known for its satras, culture, agriculture and river-island life.
  5. Riverbank erosion has reduced its area over the years.

7. Write briefly about the boundaries and characteristics of the Barak Valley.

  1. The Barak Valley lies in southern Assam.
  2. It includes Cachar, Hailakandi and Sribhumi (formerly Karimganj) districts.
  3. It is surrounded by hills of Meghalaya, Manipur, Mizoram and the North Cachar region, with Bangladesh to the south-west.
  4. The Barak River and its tributaries flow through the valley.
  5. It has fertile plains, tea gardens, wetlands and a warm, humid monsoon climate.

8. Write briefly about the boundaries and characteristics of the Karbi Plateau.

  1. The Karbi Plateau lies in central Assam, mainly in Karbi Anglong district.
  2. It is an extension of the Meghalaya Plateau.
  3. It is surrounded by the Brahmaputra Valley and the hills of Meghalaya and Nagaland.
  4. It has hills, valleys, forests and many streams.
  5. Agriculture, forests and mineral resources are important to the region.

9. Write briefly the characteristics of the Barail Range and Southern Hills.

  1. The Barail Range is a hill range in the southern part of Assam.
  2. It separates parts of the Brahmaputra Valley from the Barak Valley.
  3. The Southern Hills include the hill areas of Dima Hasao and adjoining highlands.
  4. The region has steep slopes, deep valleys, forests and heavy rainfall.
  5. It is inhabited by several indigenous communities and has rich biodiversity.

10. What is the type of Assam’s climate? Write briefly about its characteristics.

  1. Assam has a humid monsoon or sub-tropical monsoon climate.
  2. Summers are warm and humid, with frequent rain.
  3. Winters are generally cool and dry.
  4. Most rainfall occurs during the south-west monsoon season.
  5. Hills, height and distance from the Bay of Bengal cause local climate differences.

11. What is the main reason behind occurrence of rainfall in Assam during summer season?

  1. The main reason is the south-west monsoon.
  2. Moist winds from the Bay of Bengal move towards Assam.
  3. When these winds meet hills and other raised land, they are forced to rise.
  4. The rising air cools and forms clouds, causing heavy rainfall.

12. Discuss briefly about the geographical factors influencing the climate of Assam.

  1. Location: Assam lies in a humid monsoon region.
  2. Relief: The Himalayas and surrounding hills influence wind and rainfall.
  3. Monsoon winds: Moist south-west monsoon winds bring most of the rain.
  4. Altitude: Hill areas are generally cooler than the plains.
  5. Rivers and vegetation: The Brahmaputra Valley's rivers, wetlands and forests add moisture to the local environment.

13. What are the main seasons of Assam? Write in brief about the seasonal variation in the distribution of temperature and rainfall in Assam.

  1. Winter (December–February): Cool and mostly dry, with low rainfall.
  2. Pre-monsoon or summer (March–May): Temperature rises and thunderstorms with rain are common.
  3. Monsoon (June–September): Heavy rainfall occurs across much of Assam.
  4. Retreating monsoon or post-monsoon (October–November): Rainfall decreases and the weather becomes cooler and drier.
  5. Rainfall and temperature differ between the plains and the hill districts.

14. Write the names of places in Assam witnessing the highest and lowest rainfall along with their amount of rainfall.

  1. Rainfall varies greatly from one part of Assam to another.
  2. Areas in and around the southern hill districts, such as Haflong and the Barail hills, receive very heavy rainfall.
  3. Some western and central parts of the Brahmaputra Valley receive comparatively less rainfall.
  4. Exact highest and lowest places and annual amounts depend on the period and weather station used.
  5. For the examination, use the specific place names and figures given in your textbook or teacher's rainfall table; do not mix Assam data with Mawsynram or Cherrapunji, which are in Meghalaya.

15. Why does the amount of rainfall not uniform in all places of Assam?

  1. Hills force moist winds to rise and produce more rain.
  2. Places far from the main path of moist winds may receive less rain.
  3. Wind direction and strength change during the monsoon.
  4. Altitude and the shape of the land affect rainfall.
  5. Local storms also cause differences from place to place.

16. Write in brief about the spatial distribution of rainfall in Assam.

  1. Rainfall is generally high in Assam because of the monsoon.
  2. The southern hill areas and parts of the north-eastern region receive heavy rainfall.
  3. The Brahmaputra Valley also receives substantial monsoon rain.
  4. Rainfall is comparatively lower in some western and central parts of the valley.
  5. The amount changes from year to year and from one locality to another.

17. Give a short account of different types of soil found in Assam.

  1. Alluvial soil: Found in the Brahmaputra and Barak valleys; very important for farming.
  2. New alluvial soil: Found near rivers and floodplains, where fresh silt is deposited.
  3. Old alluvial soil: Found on older terraces and higher plains.
  4. Hill soil: Found in the hill districts; its depth and fertility vary with slope and vegetation.
  5. Lateritic and red soil: Found in some upland and hill areas and may be less fertile without proper care.

18. What are the reasons behind soil erosion in Assam?

  1. Heavy monsoon rainfall causes water to wash away topsoil.
  2. Floods and strong river currents erode riverbanks.
  3. Deforestation removes the roots that hold soil together.
  4. Unplanned road building, mining and construction expose loose soil.
  5. Overgrazing and improper farming on slopes increase erosion.

19. What are the different types of forest in Assam?

  1. Tropical wet evergreen forests.
  2. Tropical semi-evergreen forests.
  3. Tropical moist deciduous forests.
  4. Sub-tropical and montane forests in hill areas.
  5. Riverine forests, grasslands and swamp vegetation along rivers and wetlands.

20. What is rain forest? Describe the characteristics of rain forest in Assam.

  1. A rain forest is a dense forest that grows in areas with high rainfall and humidity.
  2. Many trees remain green throughout the year.
  3. The forests have tall trees, climbers, shrubs, ferns and many other plants.
  4. They provide habitat for elephants, primates, birds and many insects.
  5. In Assam, evergreen and semi-evergreen rain forests are found mainly in the hill regions and parts of the eastern and southern districts.

21. Write in brief about the importance and characteristics of riverine vegetation.

  1. Riverine vegetation grows along riverbanks, floodplains and wetlands.
  2. It includes grasses, reeds, shrubs and trees that can tolerate wet soil and floods.
  3. It provides food and shelter for animals and birds.
  4. Its roots help hold riverbanks and reduce soil erosion.
  5. It protects wetlands and supports the ecological balance of river areas.

22. State the location and land area of Kaziranga National Park.

  1. Kaziranga National Park lies in central Assam, mainly in Golaghat and Nagaon districts, with parts extending into adjoining districts.
  2. It lies on the southern bank of the Brahmaputra River.
  3. The original notified park area was about 430 square kilometres.
  4. The protected landscape has since been expanded through additions; always note whether a question asks for the original area or the expanded area.
  5. It is famous for the one-horned rhinoceros, elephants, wild water buffalo and tigers.

23. Write the names and location of the national parks of Assam.

  1. Kaziranga: Golaghat, Nagaon, Sonitpur and adjoining areas.
  2. Manas: Along the Bhutan border in western Assam.
  3. Nameri: Sonitpur district, near the Arunachal Pradesh border.
  4. Orang: Darrang and Sonitpur districts, on the north bank of the Brahmaputra.
  5. Dibru-Saikhowa: Dibrugarh and Tinsukia districts.
  6. Dehing Patkai: Dibrugarh and Tinsukia districts.
  7. Raimona: Kokrajhar district, near the Bhutan border.

24. Write how the wildlife sanctuaries maintain the ecological balance.

  1. They protect wild animals and their natural habitats.
  2. They help conserve endangered species and biodiversity.
  3. They protect forests, wetlands and water sources.
  4. They maintain food chains and the balance between plants and animals.
  5. They support research, education and responsible nature tourism.

25. Write about the changes in administrative boundaries in Assam during the post-independence period.

  1. After independence, Assam's boundaries changed as new states and Union Territories were formed in the North-East.
  2. Naga Hills became a separate administrative unit and later Nagaland became a state in 1963.
  3. Meghalaya became an autonomous state in 1970 and a full state in 1972.
  4. Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh became Union Territories in 1972 and full states in 1987.
  5. Within Assam, new districts and subdivisions were created over time for easier administration.

27. What is the land area of Assam? Where is the state’s capital located? Write the names of the largest and smallest districts of Assam in respect of land area.

  1. Assam has an area of about 78,438 square kilometres.
  2. The capital is Dispur, which is part of Guwahati in Kamrup Metropolitan district.
  3. Karbi Anglong is the largest district by area.
  4. Kamrup Metropolitan is among the smallest districts by area in the current district arrangement.
  5. District areas can change when administrative boundaries are reorganised.

28. What is the population of Assam according to 2011 Census? Write the names of the largest and smallest districts of Assam in terms of population size.

  1. Assam's population in the 2011 Census was 3,12,05,576 (about 3.12 crore).
  2. Among the 27 districts used in the 2011 Census, Nagaon had the largest population: 28,23,768.
  3. Dima Hasao had the smallest population: 2,14,102.
  4. These are 2011 district figures and should not be confused with population figures for today's reorganised districts.

Source: Assam government district-wise 2011 Census table.

Short Notes

29. Write short notes:

(a) Assam and Monsoon Climate

  1. Assam has a humid monsoon climate.
  2. Most rainfall occurs from June to September.
  3. Winters are cooler and comparatively dry.
  4. Hills and the Brahmaputra Valley influence local rainfall and temperature.

(b) South-West Monsoon Wind

  1. These winds blow from the south-west during the summer monsoon.
  2. They carry moisture from the Bay of Bengal towards Assam.
  3. They bring most of the state's annual rainfall.

(c) North-East Monsoon Wind

  1. These winds generally blow from land towards the sea during the cooler months.
  2. They are usually drier by the time they reach Assam.
  3. They are linked with the retreating monsoon season.

(d) Summer Season of Assam

  1. It generally lasts from March to May.
  2. Temperature and humidity increase.
  3. Pre-monsoon thunderstorms and heavy showers are common.

(e) Pre-Monsoon Season of Assam

  1. It occurs mainly during March, April and May.
  2. Local thunderstorms, often called Bordoisila, are common.
  3. These storms bring rain and help prepare the land for the monsoon.

(f) Spatial Distribution of Rainfall in Assam

  1. Rainfall is not equal in all parts of Assam.
  2. Hill slopes and some north-eastern areas receive heavy rain.
  3. Some western and central valley areas receive comparatively less rain.
  4. Relief and monsoon wind direction are important causes.

(g) Administrative Divisions of Assam

  1. Assam is divided into districts for administration.
  2. Districts are further divided into subdivisions, revenue circles and development blocks.
  3. District boundaries and names may change through government decisions.

(h) District Level Population of Assam

  1. The 2011 Census recorded 3,12,05,576 people in Assam.
  2. Nagaon was the most populated district in the 2011 district arrangement.
  3. Dima Hasao was the least populated district in that Census.
  4. Population density was higher in many valley districts than in the hill districts.

(i) BTAD

  1. BTAD means Bodoland Territorial Areas Districts.
  2. It was an administrative arrangement under the Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC).
  3. It covered four districts: Kokrajhar, Chirang, Baksa and Udalguri.
  4. The arrangement was created to provide greater self-governance to the Bodo areas.
  5. The area is now commonly referred to as the Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR).

Exam note: Q28 specifically asks for 2011 Census data, so use the 2011 figures. For Q26 and Q27, use the present administrative names and boundaries as directed by your teacher or latest textbook.
