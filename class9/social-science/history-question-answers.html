Chapter 1 — Advent of the Europeans into India

1. Who was the first Portuguese sailor to discover the sea route between India and the West? When and where in India did he arrive for the first time?
Answer: Vasco da Gama discovered the sea route. He reached Calicut on the Malabar Coast in 1498.
2. Who was the English sailor able to move round the earth through the sea route?
Answer: Francis Drake was the English sailor who circumnavigated the earth.
3. Who was the English sailor who entered India for the first time with a petition letter and when?
Answer: John Mildenhall came to India in 1599 with a petition letter for trade privileges.
4. When and where did the East India Company initially establish?
Answer: The English East India Company was established in 1600 by a charter of Queen Elizabeth I. It initially traded in the Spice Islands.
5. Write two objectives of the East India Company to permanently rule in India.
Answer: Its main aims were to expand trade and profits and to gain political and territorial control so that British interests could be protected.
6. When and where in India was the first trade centre of the English established?
Answer: The first English trading centre in India was established at Surat in 1613.
7. What is Fort William?
Answer: Fort William was the fortified English settlement at Calcutta, built to protect the Company's trade centre.
8. Which were the two main divisions of the British administrative period of dependent India?
Answer: They were the period of East India Company rule and the period of British Crown rule.
9. Who, when and with what objective was the Government of India Act enacted?
Answer: The British Parliament enacted the Government of India Act in 1858 to transfer the administration of India from the East India Company to the British Crown.
10. When and with what objective was the Indian Councils Act enacted?
Answer: The Indian Councils Act, 1861 was enacted to reorganise the legislative councils and to associate Indians with the law-making process.

Short / Long Answer Questions

1. What were the efforts made by the British to establish trade relation with India? Analyse the steps by which they succeeded.
Answer: The English first explored sea routes to India. Queen Elizabeth I granted a charter to the East India Company in 1600. Hawkins sought permission from Jahangir, and Sir Thomas Roe secured important trading privileges. The Company established factories and fortified settlements. Later it used military power, diplomacy and political interference to expand from trade to territorial rule.
2. How did the East India Company establish three trade centres at three important places in India?
Answer: The Company obtained trading privileges from Indian rulers and established important centres at Surat in western India, Madras in the south and Calcutta in Bengal. These centres developed into major fortified settlements and bases of British trade.
3. Who was the most remarkable person of the East India Company to pave the way for British imperialism in India? Write four arguments about his strategies.
Answer: Robert Clive played a major role. He defeated Siraj-ud-daulah at Plassey (1757), used diplomacy and alliances, strengthened Company military power, and helped secure the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in 1765. These steps laid the foundation of British political power.
4. Write the causes for the outbreak of the Sepoy Mutiny.
Answer: Major causes included discontent among soldiers, resentment against British policies, the annexation of Indian states, interference with traditional institutions, economic hardship, and the immediate controversy over rifle cartridges believed to be greased with cow and pig fat.
5. Mention four important results of the Sepoy Mutiny.
Answer: The East India Company lost power; India came under the British Crown; the Governor-General became the Viceroy; and a new system of administration was created under the Government of India Act, 1858. British policy towards Indian princes was also changed.
6. Mention four administrative changes brought through the Government of India Act.
Answer: The Crown took direct control of India; the post of Secretary of State for India was created; the Governor-General became the Viceroy; and the Company's army and administrative machinery came under the British Government.
7. Write about two main reforms of Lord Canning.
Answer: Lord Canning introduced changes after the transfer to Crown rule and worked to reorganise administration. He also introduced measures in education and administration and helped implement the new system created by the Government of India Act, 1858.
8. Write two merits and two demerits of the Indian Councils Act.
Answer: Merits: it enlarged the legislative councils and allowed limited Indian participation. Demerits: the councils had limited powers and the Viceroy retained overriding authority; representation was still very restricted.
9. Discuss the changes through local self-government during the British period.
Answer: The British gradually introduced municipal boards and local boards. Lord Ripon gave local self-government a stronger foundation through the Resolution of 1882, encouraging elected Indian representatives and local administration.
10. How were the Indians included in the civil services? Discuss the growth of these services.
Answer: Initially the higher services were dominated by Europeans. The Charter Act of 1833 opened the principle of equal eligibility, and the Civil Service Act of 1853 introduced competitive examination. Later Indians such as Satyendranath Tagore entered the service. Over time more Indians joined administrative services.
11. Mention three differences between the civil services of British occupied India and independent India.
Answer: During British rule the higher services mainly served colonial interests and were dominated by Europeans. Recruitment and examinations were centred largely in Britain. In independent India, civil services serve a democratic Indian government, recruitment is through Indian competitive examinations, and citizens of India have equal opportunity to enter them.

Chapter 2 — Growth of Indian Nationalism

1. What was the main factor responsible for the growth of Indian Nationalism?
Answer: The growth was caused by several factors, especially western education, modern transport and communication, the press, socio-religious reform movements, and the common experience of British rule.
2. Who was the writer of Anandamath?
Answer: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay.
3. Who composed the song 'Sare Jahan Se Achha'?
Answer: Muhammad Iqbal.
4. When and where was the first railway line in India constructed?
Answer: The first railway line was opened in 1853 between Bombay and Thane.
5. When and where was the first telegraph line in India constructed?
Answer: The first major experimental telegraph line was established between Calcutta and Diamond Harbour in 1851.
6. When and where was the first Printing Press established in India?
Answer: The first printing press in India was established by the Portuguese at Goa in 1556.
7. When was the Calcutta Presidency College established? By what name was it known earlier?
Answer: It was established in 1855 as Presidency College. It had earlier been known as the Hindu College.
8. Mention two important social reforms of Lord William Bentinck.
Answer: He abolished Sati and took steps to suppress Thuggee.
9. Name the first newspaper published in India.
Answer: Hicky's Bengal Gazette, published in 1780.

Long Answer Questions

1. Write briefly how the Revolt of 1857 led to the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: The revolt united people against foreign rule and created a stronger feeling that Indians shared common political interests. Its failure also showed the need for organised national action. Later educated Indians and political organisations drew lessons from the revolt and worked towards a broader national movement.
2. Write the main objectives of Wood's Despatch.
Answer: Wood's Despatch of 1854 aimed to develop education, establish a graded system from primary to higher education, promote teacher training, encourage female education, and expand English and vernacular education.
3. Write about six important reasons for the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Western education; modern transport and communication; the growth of newspapers and the printing press; socio-religious reform movements; economic and administrative policies of British rule; and the development of political organisations such as the Indian National Congress.
4. Write at least four effects of the introduction of western education in India.
Answer: It spread modern ideas, encouraged rational thinking, created an educated middle class, introduced ideas of liberty and equality, and helped Indians understand political rights and national unity.
5. Discuss the impact of western education towards the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Western education exposed Indians to ideas of liberty, democracy, nationalism and equality. Educated Indians studied their country's problems, criticised colonial policies and organised political movements. It therefore became an important force behind Indian nationalism.
6. How did the development of transport and communication lead to the growth of nationalism in India?
Answer: Railways, roads, post and telegraph connected distant regions. People could travel, exchange ideas and organise meetings more easily. Newspapers and political messages could also spread quickly, helping Indians develop a common national outlook.
7. Discuss the contribution of Raja Ram Mohun Roy in the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Raja Ram Mohun Roy opposed social evils, promoted modern education and supported freedom of thought and the press. He worked for social reform and helped create a modern public consciousness among Indians.
8. Discuss the contribution of Dayanand Saraswati in the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Dayanand Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj and encouraged social reform, education, self-respect and a return to Indian cultural values. His work helped strengthen social and national consciousness.
9. Discuss the contribution of Keshab Chandra Sen in the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Keshab Chandra Sen worked for social and religious reform through the Brahmo Samaj movement. He promoted modern education, social equality and reform, helping create a progressive public outlook.
10. Discuss the contribution of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa in the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Ramakrishna Paramahamsa stressed spiritual unity and respect for different religions. His teachings inspired self-confidence and cultural pride, which later influenced nationalist thought.
11. Discuss the contribution of Anne Besant in the growth of Indian nationalism.
Answer: Anne Besant supported Indian self-government, founded the Home Rule movement in India and used education and political organisation to spread national consciousness.
12. What is Aligarh Movement? To what extent did it infuse modern outlook and thinking among Indian Muslims?
Answer: The Aligarh Movement was led by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan. It promoted modern scientific education among Muslims through the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College. It encouraged modern thinking, education and social reform.
13. How did the printing press in India lead to the growth of Indian nationalism?
Answer: The printing press made books, newspapers, journals and political writings widely available. It spread information, criticised unjust policies, connected people and created public opinion in favour of national unity.
14. Discuss briefly the factors that led to the birth of the Indian National Congress.
Answer: The spread of western education, growth of the press, improved transport and communication, social reform, political awareness and the need for an organisation representing Indian interests prepared the ground for the Congress. It was founded in 1885 with the efforts of A.O. Hume and Indian leaders.
15. Give a brief account of the working of the Indian National Congress during the early years of its formation.
Answer: In its early years the Congress used constitutional methods. It held annual sessions, submitted petitions and resolutions, demanded administrative reforms and greater Indian representation, and gradually created a common political platform for educated Indians.

Chapter 3 — The Moamoriya Rebellion

1. What is Mayamara?
Answer: Mayamara refers to the Vaishnavite religious tradition and the Mayamara Sattra followed by the Moamoriya community.
2. What is Sattra?
Answer: A Sattra is a Vaishnavite religious and cultural institution or monastery.
3. What is Songhoti?
Answer: Songhoti means a group or association of people formed around a common religious or social purpose.
4. During the reign of which Ahom King did the Moamoriya Rebellion begin?
Answer: It began during the reign of Swargadeo Lakshmi Singha.
5. During the reign of which Ahom King did the Moamoriya Rebellion conclude?
Answer: It finally ended during the reign of Swargadeo Kamaleswar Singha.
6. Which Ahom King is said to have adopted the title of Swargadeo/Swarganarayan?
Answer: Sudangpha is traditionally associated with the adoption of the title Swarganarayan.
7. During the reign of Surampha Bhagaraja which Sattradhikar was murdered?
Answer: Gokulchandra, the Sattradhikar associated with the Mayamara tradition, was murdered during the period of repression.
8. Which Sattradhikar was humiliated in the royal court of Rudra Singha?
Answer: Krishnaram Bhattacharya was humiliated in the royal court.
9. Who was the chief deity of the Ahoms?
Answer: The chief deity of the Ahoms was Lengdon.
10. Which Sattradhikar did Jaydhwaj Singha accept as his religious preceptor?
Answer: Parbatiya Gosain.
11. Which Sattradhikar did Rudra Singha accept as his religious preceptor?
Answer: Krishnaram Bhattacharya.
12. Who was Krishnaram Bhattacharya?
Answer: He was a Shakta religious leader who became influential in the Ahom royal court.
13. During which Ahom King's reign did Shaktism become very strong?
Answer: Shaktism became particularly strong during the reign of Rudra Singha.
14. Who was Gagini?
Answer: Gagini was a woman associated with the Mayamara religious tradition and its followers during the period of the rebellion.
15. Whom did the Moamoriyas first establish as King?
Answer: They first established Ramakanta as king.
16. How long did Captain Welsh stay in Assam?
Answer: Captain Welsh stayed in Assam for about two years, from 1792 to 1794.
17. Where was salt produced in Assam?
Answer: Salt was produced in the Namrup area and other local salt-producing areas.
18. Why were the common people of Assam unable to use salt?
Answer: Salt was heavily controlled and taxed, making it too costly for many common people.

Short / Long Questions

1. Who were the Moamoriyas? Can this rebellion be called a mass uprising?
Answer: The Moamoriyas were mainly followers of the Mayamara Vaishnavite Sattra, especially people from communities such as Morans and others who suffered discrimination and exploitation. The rebellion became a mass uprising because large sections of common people joined it against Ahom oppression.
2. Analyse the political causes of the Moamoriya Rebellion.
Answer: The Ahom monarchy became weak; nobles fought among themselves; royal interference in religious institutions increased; cruel officials exploited people; and repeated political conflicts weakened the administration. These conditions created widespread discontent.
3. Do you think the change in the religious policy of the Ahoms was a cause of the Moamoriya Rebellion? Discuss.
Answer: Yes. The Ahom rulers increasingly favoured Shakta practices and interfered with Vaishnavite Sattras. The Mayamara followers faced humiliation and restrictions. This religious discrimination added to economic and political grievances.
4. How far was the paik system responsible for the Moamoriya Rebellion?
Answer: The paik system contributed greatly because forced labour, unequal treatment and heavy demands placed pressure on ordinary people. Corruption and exploitation by officials made the system more oppressive and helped create conditions for rebellion.
5. Discuss the results of the Moamoriya Rebellion.
Answer: The Ahom monarchy became weak; the socio-economic condition of Assam deteriorated; Rangpur and other areas suffered repeated destruction; a separate Matak Kingdom emerged; and British influence gradually increased.
6. Do you think the Moamoriya Rebellion was successful or unsuccessful? Give logic for your answer.
Answer: It was partly successful but ultimately unsuccessful. It challenged the Ahom rulers and created an independent Matak Kingdom, but it could not permanently replace Ahom rule. The rebellion also weakened Assam and made British expansion easier.
7. Under what circumstances did Captain Welsh come to Assam? Why did he leave his expedition incomplete and go back?
Answer: The Ahom king Gaurinath Singha requested British military help against the Moamoriyas. Captain Welsh arrived with troops and suppressed the rebels. He left because the British Government ordered him to withdraw after concluding a political and trade arrangement with Gaurinath Singha.
8. Describe the role played by Captain Welsh in suppressing the Moamoriya Rebellion. How far was he successful?
Answer: Welsh defeated rebel forces, restored Gaurinath Singha at Rangpur and helped restore the Ahom administration in areas he occupied. He was militarily successful, but the rebellion and political instability were not permanently removed.
9. Discuss Captain Welsh's Account of Assam.
Answer: Welsh described Assam's political condition, administration, trade, agriculture, population and resources. He noted the weakness of the Ahom administration and the importance of Assam's trade and natural resources. His account is useful for understanding Assam in the late eighteenth century.

Chapter 4 — Burmese Invasion of Assam

1. Which country was won by the Burmese in 1784 during the regime of Burmese King Bodawpaya?
Answer: The Burmese conquered Arakan in 1784.
2. From when did the enmity between the Burmese and the British begin?
Answer: The rivalry grew during the expansion of Burmese power into territories adjoining British India, especially from the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries.
3. Name the King of Cachar who fled to Srihatta, unable to tolerate Burmese oppression.
Answer: Govinda Chandra.
4. Purnananda Buragohain died after hearing the news of the defeat of the Ahoms in which battle?
Answer: He died after hearing of the defeat at the Battle of Ghiladhari.
5. What is Baishali Hukong?
Answer: It refers to the Burmese political and territorial expansion in the region of Baishali, associated with Burmese control in the north-eastern frontier.
6. What is Baishali Mung-dun-Sun Khan?
Answer: It was a Burmese territorial division or region connected with the expansion of Burmese power towards Assam and neighbouring areas.
7. Who was the Prime Minister of Assam when the Burmese army came with Badanchandra?
Answer: Purnananda Buragohain.
8. What was the strength of the Burmese army which came to Assam with Badanchandra?
Answer: The Burmese force that accompanied Badanchandra was about 8,000 soldiers.
9. How many additional soldiers did Badanchandra collect on his arrival in Assam?
Answer: He collected about 2,000 additional soldiers.
10. What title was given by Chandrakanta Singha to Badanchandra on his appointment as Prime Minister?
Answer: He was given the title Barphukan.
11. Mention the years in which the Burmese invasions took place.
Answer: The Burmese invasions occurred in 1817, 1819 and 1821.
12. When was Badanchandra killed?
Answer: Badanchandra Barphukan was assassinated in 1818.

Long Answer Questions

1. Write in brief about the expansion of the Burmese empire in Manipur and Arakan.
Answer: The Burmese conquered Arakan in 1784 and later expanded into Manipur. Their expansion brought them close to Assam and British territories. This expansion created political pressure and repeated conflicts in the north-east.
2. Discuss how the despotism of Purnananda Buragohain gave birth to political conspiracies.
Answer: Purnananda concentrated power and controlled administration strongly. His strict measures created dissatisfaction among nobles and rival groups. Several conspiracies were formed against him, especially by people who wanted to weaken his authority.
3. What was the ultimate result of the political conspiracies against Purnananda Buragohain?
Answer: The conspiracies weakened the Ahom administration, led to the murder of Badanchandra and finally opened the way for Burmese intervention in Assam.
4. Why did the Burmese invade Assam under the leadership of Badanchandra Phukan?
Answer: Badanchandra sought Burmese help against his political enemies. The Burmese wanted to expand their power, and their intervention provided an opportunity to enter Assam. Thus Badanchandra's political conflict and Burmese expansion combined to cause the invasion.
5. Discuss briefly the Burmese interference in Assam under the leadership of Badanchandra Barphukan.
Answer: Badanchandra obtained Burmese military support and entered Assam. The Burmese army fought the Ahom forces and interfered in Assam's internal politics. Their intervention weakened the Ahom kingdom and paved the way for later Burmese invasions.
6. Discuss the causes of the Burmese invasion of Assam.
Answer: Political instability in Assam, rivalry among Ahom nobles, Badanchandra's appeal for Burmese help, Burmese expansion in Manipur and Arakan, and the weakness of the Ahom administration were major causes.
7. What were the results of the Burmese invasion of Assam?
Answer: The Ahom kingdom was badly weakened; people suffered killings and displacement; the economy and administration collapsed; many people fled Assam; and the British eventually defeated the Burmese and established their influence after the Treaty of Yandaboo.
8. Which rebellion is known as the 'Panimua rebellion'?
Answer: The uprising of the Assamese people against Burmese oppression and forced demands is known as the Panimua rebellion.
9. Mention two results of the military intervention of the Burmese in Assam in 1817.
Answer: The Burmese gained strong influence over Assam and the Ahom administration was weakened. The intervention also increased violence, political instability and suffering among the people.
10. When did the first Anglo-Burmese War take place? Where was it fought?
Answer: The First Anglo-Burmese War took place from 1824 to 1826. It was fought in Assam, Arakan, Manipur and other eastern frontier areas as well as Burmese territory.
11. Mention the main provisions of the Treaty of Yandaboo.
Answer: Burma paid an indemnity to the British; it ceded Assam, Manipur, Arakan and Tenasserim; a British resident was allowed at Ava; and the Burmese agreed not to interfere in Assam and neighbouring territories.
12. Write about the importance of the Treaty of Yandaboo.
Answer: The treaty ended the First Anglo-Burmese War and brought Assam under British control. It marked the beginning of a new phase of British rule in Assam and greatly expanded British influence in north-east India.

Short Answer Questions

1. To which kingdom did Govinda Chandra belong?
Answer: He was the king of the Cachar Kingdom.
2. Who was David Scott?
Answer: David Scott was an important British administrator who served as Commissioner and helped establish British administration in Assam and neighbouring areas.
3. Who was the next commissioner to David Scott?
Answer: Robertson succeeded David Scott as Commissioner.
4. Of which kingdom was Tirot Singh the Siyem?
Answer: He was the Siyem of Nongkhlaw in the Khasi Hills.
5. What was the title of the Muttock King?
Answer: The Muttock ruler was recognised as the Bar Senapati / local chief of the Matak-Muttock territory.
6. According to the instruction of Jenkins who lost the kingdom?
Answer: The rulers who failed to meet the conditions imposed by the British administration lost their territories; the policy particularly affected local chiefs and rulers during British consolidation.
7. What were the districts into which Jenkins divided Lower Assam?
Answer: Jenkins reorganised Lower Assam into administrative districts including Kamrup, Darrang, Goalpara and Nowgong, with further changes made as British administration expanded.
8. To whom did the British give Rupees 50 as pension and occupy Cachar?
Answer: The British occupied Cachar after the death and political settlement involving Govinda Chandra; the former ruling authority was provided a pension arrangement.

Long Answer Questions

1. How was the revenue policy of David Scott? Discuss.
Answer: David Scott introduced a more systematic revenue administration. Land was surveyed and assessed, revenue was collected through officials, and the British aimed to obtain regular cash revenue. His administration also reorganised districts and revenue collection.
2. What steps were adopted in the matters of judiciary during the time of David Scott?
Answer: David Scott organised courts and administrative divisions, appointed officials to deal with civil and criminal cases, and introduced procedures intended to replace older systems. His judicial reforms brought Assam under a more formal British legal administration.
3. How was the revenue administration of Robertson?
Answer: Robertson continued and modified the revenue system. He introduced land assessment based on land quality and regularised collection. His administration aimed at increasing revenue while making collection more systematic.
4. What steps were adopted by Major Jenkins regarding the welfare of Assam?
Answer: Major Jenkins improved administration, developed trade and communication, reorganised districts, encouraged economic activity and introduced administrative reforms. He also promoted measures connected with revenue, justice and public order.
5. How did Jenkins divide Assam into different districts?
Answer: Jenkins reorganised the Brahmaputra Valley into separate administrative districts. Areas such as Kamrup, Darrang, Nowgong and Goalpara were placed under separate administrative arrangements with appointed British officers.
6. Who was Tirot Singh? Why did he rise against the British?
Answer: Tirot Singh was the Siyem of Nongkhlaw in the Khasi Hills. He opposed British attempts to build a road through his territory and feared loss of Khasi independence. When negotiations failed, he led resistance against the British.
