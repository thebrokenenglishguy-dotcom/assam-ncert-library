Chapter 1 — Political Parties in India

Exercise

Give Very Short Answer

1. Give the name of a country having one party rule.
Answer: China.
2. What is the party system that is found in the United States of America?
Answer: The Two-Party System is found in the United States of America.
3. Mention the two characteristics of political party.
Answer:
  1. A political party has a group of people with common ideas and objectives.
  2. It aims to capture political power through elections.
4. Mention a chief function of political party.
Answer: A chief function of a political party is to contest elections and form the government.
5. Mention the name of a regional party of Assam.
Answer: Asom Gana Parishad (AGP).
6. What is the name of the chief regional party of Jharkhand?
Answer: Jharkhand Mukti Morcha (JMM).
7. Give the full term of UPA.
Answer: United Progressive Alliance.
8. Which is the first political party of India?
Answer: The Indian National Congress (INC) is generally regarded in the textbook context as the first major all-India political party.
9. Under whose leadership the Indian Association was formed?
Answer: The Indian Association was formed under the leadership of Surendranath Banerjee and Ananda Mohan Bose.
10. Who was the first President of the Indian National Congress?
Answer: Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee) was the first President of the Indian National Congress in 1885.

Give Short Answer

1. What is the chief characteristic of the Indian political system?
Answer: The chief characteristic of the Indian political system is the multi-party system. Many political parties participate in elections and compete for political power.
2. Which party system is particularly useful for a democratic state?
Answer: The multi-party system is particularly useful for a democratic state because it gives people many choices and represents different interests and opinions.
3. Mention four chief characteristics of political party.
Answer:
  1. A political party has a group of people with common ideas.
  2. It has certain policies and programmes.
  3. It contests elections to gain political power.
  4. It tries to form the government or influence the government.
4. Give the name of five regional political parties of India.
Answer:
  1. Asom Gana Parishad (AGP)
  2. Jharkhand Mukti Morcha (JMM)
  3. Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK)
  4. Biju Janata Dal (BJD)
  5. Shiv Sena
5. What is meant by the term Alliance government?
Answer: An Alliance government is a government formed by two or more political parties when no single party has enough seats to form the government alone.

Give Long Answer

1. What is meant by Alliance government?
Answer:

An Alliance government is a government formed by two or more political parties.

It is formed when no single political party gets enough seats to form the government on its own.

The parties come together and agree to work together.

They support a common government and share responsibilities.

In simple words:
Alliance government = Government formed by two or more parties working together.

2. Discuss two merits and two demerits of the Coalition or the Alliance government.
Answer:

Merits:

  1. Representation of different groups: Different parties represent different regions, communities and interests.
  2. Power is shared: Political power is not concentrated in only one party.

Demerits:

  1. Instability: Disagreements between alliance partners may make the government unstable.
  2. Slow decision-making: Different parties may have different opinions, making it difficult to take quick decisions.
3. Discuss the role of the opposition in democracy.
Answer:

The opposition has an important role in a democracy.

  1. It checks the work of the ruling party.
  2. It points out the mistakes of the government.
  3. It questions government policies and decisions.
  4. It raises important public issues.
  5. It gives alternative ideas and policies.
  6. It helps to prevent the misuse of political power.

Thus, a strong opposition helps to make democracy more responsible and effective.

4. Discuss the main reasons for the birth of the regional parties.
Answer:

Regional parties developed mainly because of the following reasons:

  1. Regional interests: People of a particular region wanted their local interests to be protected.
  2. Regional identity: Language, culture and traditions encouraged the growth of regional parties.
  3. Economic problems: Some regions felt that their economic problems were not properly addressed.
  4. Political differences: People sometimes became dissatisfied with national political parties.
  5. Demand for greater autonomy: Some regions wanted greater attention and powers for their states.
  6. Local leadership: Strong regional leaders helped in the formation of regional parties.
5. Write about the necessity of political parties in a democratic state.
Answer:

Political parties are necessary for the proper functioning of democracy.

  1. They contest elections.
  2. They select candidates for elections.
  3. They present different policies and programmes before the people.
  4. The party that gets majority support can form the government.
  5. Political parties form the opposition when they do not form the government.
  6. They make people aware of political and public issues.
  7. They connect the people with the government.
  8. They help in making and implementing government policies.

Therefore, political parties are an important part of a democratic system.

6. Mention the characteristics and the functions of the political party.
Answer:

Characteristics of Political Parties

  1. A political party consists of a group of people.
  2. Members have some common ideas and objectives.
  3. A party has its own policies and programmes.
  4. It contests elections.
  5. Its main aim is to gain political power.
  6. It tries to form the government or influence government policies.

Functions of Political Parties

  1. They contest elections.
  2. They select candidates.
  3. They form the government when they get enough support.
  4. They act as the opposition when they are not in power.
  5. They make policies and programmes.
  6. They create political awareness among people.
  7. They raise public issues.
  8. They connect people with the government.
7. Discuss the reasons for getting special significance by the system of the Alliance government at present.
Answer:

The Alliance government has gained importance because:

  1. Many political parties have become active in India.
  2. Regional parties have become important in different states.
  3. Sometimes no single party gets a clear majority in elections.
  4. Different parties therefore come together to form the government.
  5. Alliance governments give representation to different regions and groups.
  6. They require different parties to cooperate with one another.
  7. The system allows regional parties to participate in national-level government.

Therefore, the Alliance system has become an important feature of India's multi-party political system.

Important Factual Note

The Indian National Congress held its first session in Bombay in 1885, with Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee as its first president. The Indian Association was founded in 1876 under the leadership of Surendranath Banerjee, with Ananda Mohan Bose as an important co-founder.

Chapter 2 — Types or Classification of Government

Exercise

Give Very Short Answer

1. Who was the first political philosopher to classify the government?
Answer: Plato was the first political philosopher to classify government.
2. Which is the most popular government in the world at present?
Answer: Democracy is the most popular government in the world at present.
3. Give the name of a state having Parliamentary form of government.
Answer: India has a Parliamentary form of government.
4. Who is the Head of the government and the Head of the state in the Parliamentary form of government.
Answer: The Prime Minister is the Head of the Government, while the President is the Head of the State.
5. In what type of government Principle of Separation of Power is found?
Answer: The Principle of Separation of Power is mainly found in the Presidential form of government.
6. Give the name of a state having Presidential form of government.
Answer: The United States of America (USA) has a Presidential form of government.
7. Give the name of a state having Unitary form of government.
Answer: Great Britain (England) has a Unitary form of government.
8. What are the two governments found in the Federal form of government?
Answer:
  1. Central Government
  2. Provincial/State Government
9. Which type of government is found in Switzerland?
Answer: A Federal form of government is found in Switzerland.
10. Is there the system of Dual citizenship in India?
Answer: No, there is no system of Dual Citizenship in India.

Give Short Answer

1. What are the different types of government prevalent in the world at present?
Answer:
  1. Parliamentary Government
  2. Federal Government
  3. Unitary Government
  4. Presidential Government
2. Give the two characteristics of the Parliamentary form of government.
Answer:
  1. There are two heads—the nominal head and the real executive head.
  2. The executive is closely related to the legislature, and ministers are generally members of the legislature.
3. Write briefly about the two merits and two demerits of the Parliamentary form of government.
Answer:

Merits:

  1. There is cooperation and coordination between the executive and the legislature.
  2. The government gives importance to public opinion.

Demerits:

  1. If no party gets a clear majority, an Alliance Government may be formed, which may lead to political instability.
  2. Ministers may be inexperienced in departmental work and may depend on bureaucrats.
4. What is meant by the Principle of Separation of Power?
Answer: The Principle of Separation of Power means that the powers of the government are divided among different organs, mainly the Legislature, Executive and Judiciary. Each organ works independently in its own field.
5. Give the two characteristics of the Presidential form of government.
Answer:
  1. There is no separate Head of the State and Head of the Government. The President is both.
  2. There is Separation of Powers between the Executive and the Legislature.
6. Write briefly about two merits and two demerits of the Unitary form of government.
Answer:

Merits:

  1. Administration is uniform, strong and effective.
  2. Government expenditure is comparatively less.

Demerits:

  1. It is not suitable for a very large country.
  2. The central government may become overburdened and regional problems may not receive enough attention.

Give Long Answer

1. Write the merits and demerits of the Presidential form of government.
Answer:

Merits:

  1. Stability: The President has a fixed term of office.
  2. Separation of Powers: The Executive and Legislature work separately.
  3. Strong Executive: The President can take decisions effectively.
  4. Continuity: The government does not easily fall because of loss of legislative majority.
  5. The three organs of government can work independently.

Demerits:

  1. The President may become too powerful.
  2. There may be a lack of cooperation between the Executive and Legislature.
  3. The system can become rigid.
  4. If the President and Legislature belong to different political parties, conflicts may arise.
  5. There may be a risk of misuse of executive power.
2. Write the characteristics of the Parliamentary form of government.
Answer:

The Parliamentary form of government is also known as the Cabinet form of government. Its main characteristics are:

  1. There are two heads—the nominal head and the real executive head.
  2. The Prime Minister is the real head of the government.
  3. The executive is closely related to the legislature.
  4. Ministers are generally members of the legislature.
  5. The Council of Ministers is responsible to the legislature.
  6. If the government loses the confidence of the legislature, it may have to resign.
  7. The Council of Ministers works as a team.
  8. India is an example of a Parliamentary form of government.
3. Discuss with examples why the Federal form of government has become popular at present.
Answer:
  1. Division of powers: Powers are divided between the Central and State Governments.
  2. National unity: Different groups, languages and regions can live together under one system.
  3. Regional interests: State governments can solve local and regional problems.
  4. Decentralisation: Power is not concentrated only in the Central Government.
  5. Democratic participation: People can participate in government at both Central and State levels.
  6. It reduces the pressure on the Central Government.
  7. It helps maintain a balance between national and regional interests.

Examples: The USA, India and Switzerland have federal systems.

4. Write the characteristics of the Unitary form of government.
Answer:
  1. The Central Government holds the main powers.
  2. There is no constitutional division of powers between the Centre and the regional units as in a federation.
  3. The local or regional governments work under the control of the Central Government.
  4. Administration is generally uniform throughout the country.
  5. The Central Government is strong and powerful.
  6. There is generally single citizenship.
  7. The Constitution may be written or unwritten.
  8. Examples include Great Britain and France.
5. Is the Unitary form of government really democratic? Give a critical analysis.
Answer:

The Unitary form of government can be democratic, but it has some limitations.

  1. In a Unitary system, most powers are concentrated in the Central Government.
  2. Local governments have less independent power.
  3. Regional problems may not always receive enough attention.
  4. A very large country may find it difficult to manage all regions from the Centre.
  5. Too much concentration of power may reduce local participation.
  6. However, if the government is elected by the people and protects democratic rights and freedoms, a Unitary system can also function democratically.

Therefore, a Unitary form of government is not automatically undemocratic; its democratic nature depends on how the government is organised and how people's rights are protected.
