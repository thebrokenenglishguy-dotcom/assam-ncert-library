Short Answer Questions

1. Why did the British government send the Cabinet Mission to India?
Answer:
  • Britain wanted to find a solution to the political problems of India.
  • It wanted to discuss the transfer of power to Indians.
  • It also wanted to help create a Constituent Assembly to frame India's Constitution.
  • The Mission tried to find an agreement between the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League.
  • It came to India in 1946. Constitution of India
2. According to which Act, India's administration was governed between 15 August, 1947 to 26 January, 1950 and who enacted this Act?
Answer:
  • India was governed mainly according to the Indian Independence Act, 1947 during this period.
  • The Act was passed by the British Parliament.
  • It came into effect on 15 August 1947.
  • It created the two independent Dominions of India and Pakistan.
  • India continued under this arrangement until its Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.
3. Why are the words "We the people of India" incorporated in the Preamble?
Answer:
  • These words show that the people are the ultimate source of power.
  • The Constitution gets its authority from the people of India.
  • It shows the democratic nature of India.
  • It means that the Constitution was made for and by the people of India.

The Preamble itself begins with "We, the People of India". Legislative Government of India

4. What is meant by the term Sovereign?
Answer:

Sovereign means that:

  • India is free to take its own decisions.
  • No other country can control India.
  • India can make its own laws and policies.
  • India is independent in both internal and external matters.
5. According to which constitutional amendment were the two words "Socialist" and "Secular" incorporated in the Preamble and in which year?
Answer:
  • The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment.
  • The amendment was passed in 1976.
  • The changes came into effect in 1977. Legislative Government of India
6. What is meant by "Quasi-federal state"?
Answer:

A quasi-federal state has both federal and unitary features.

  • Power is divided between the Central Government and State Governments.
  • Both levels have their own areas of authority.
  • But the Central Government is stronger in some important matters.
  • Therefore, India is often described as a quasi-federal state.
7. Which Indian Act had introduced limited Parliamentary democracy in pre-independence India?
Answer:
  • The Government of India Act, 1919 introduced limited parliamentary features in British India.
  • It introduced dyarchy in the provinces.
  • It also increased the participation of Indians in the legislative process.

Long Answer Questions

1. Discuss how the ideals of the Indian Constitution have been manifested in its Preamble.

Answer:

The Preamble expresses the main ideals and objectives of the Indian Constitution.

1. Sovereign
  • India is an independent country.
  • It can take its own decisions without outside control.
2. Socialist
  • The Constitution aims at social and economic equality.
  • It seeks to reduce economic inequality.
3. Secular
  • India has no official religion.
  • All religions are treated equally.
  • Every citizen has freedom of religion.
4. Democratic
  • The people choose their representatives through elections.
  • The government works according to the wishes of the people.
5. Republic
  • The head of the country is an elected President.
  • The position is not hereditary.
6. Justice

The Preamble promises:

  • Social justice
  • Economic justice
  • Political justice
7. Liberty
  • Citizens have freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship.
8. Equality
  • All citizens are equal.
  • Everyone should get equal status and opportunity.
9. Fraternity
  • It promotes a feeling of brotherhood among citizens.
  • It protects the dignity of every person.
10. Unity and Integrity
  • It aims to maintain the unity and integrity of India.

These ideals are clearly expressed in the Preamble of the Constitution. Legislative Government of India

2. Write in detail about what is meant by sovereign, socialist, secular, Democratic and Republic.

Answer:
1. Sovereign
  • India is an independent nation.
  • It is free to make its own decisions.
  • No foreign country can control India.
2. Socialist
  • The government aims to reduce economic and social inequalities.
  • It promotes equal opportunities for all.
  • It tries to ensure a fair distribution of resources.
3. Secular
  • India does not have an official state religion.
  • All religions are treated equally.
  • Citizens are free to follow and practise their religion.
4. Democratic
  • The people are the source of political power.
  • People elect their representatives.
  • Elections are held regularly.
  • The government is responsible to the people.
5. Republic
  • India has an elected President as its constitutional head.
  • The position of President is not hereditary.
  • Any eligible citizen can become President according to the constitutional process.

The Preamble describes India as a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic. Legislative Government of India

3. Discuss the objective resolutions put forward by Pandit Nehru in relation to the Indian constitution.

Answer:
  • Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the Objectives Resolution in the Constituent Assembly on 13 December 1946. Constitution of India
  • It explained the basic ideals on which the Constitution would be based.

The main objectives were:

1. India would be an independent and sovereign nation.

2. India would have a democratic system of government.

3. Justice would be provided to all citizens.

4. All citizens would get equality of status and opportunity.

5. Citizens would enjoy freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship.

6. Adequate safeguards would be provided for minorities and backward groups.

7. The Constitution would protect the unity and integrity of India.

8. India would work for the welfare of its people.

9. The country would promote peace and human welfare.

The Objectives Resolution became an important basis for the Preamble of the Indian Constitution. Constitution of India

4. Discuss the characteristics of the Indian federal system.

Answer:

The Indian federal system has the following important characteristics:

1. Two levels of government
  • There is a Central Government.
  • There are State Governments.
2. Division of powers
  • Powers are divided between the Centre and the States.
  • The Constitution clearly defines their powers.
3. Written Constitution
  • India has a written Constitution.
  • It clearly explains the powers of different governments.
4. Supremacy of the Constitution
  • The Constitution is the highest law of the country.
  • Both the Centre and States must follow it.
5. Independent Judiciary
  • India has an independent judiciary.
  • The Supreme Court settles disputes between the Centre and States.
6. Strong Central Government
  • The Central Government has more powers in certain important matters.
  • During emergencies, the Centre becomes even stronger.
7. Single citizenship
  • India provides single citizenship.
  • A person is a citizen of India, not separately a citizen of a particular state.
8. Union of States
  • India is described in the Constitution as a Union of States.
  • The federal structure therefore has some strong unitary features.

Thus, India has a federal system with a strong Centre, which is why it is often described as quasi-federal.

5. Explain the important characteristics of the Parliamentary form of government in India.

Answer:

India follows the Parliamentary form of government. Its main characteristics are:

1. Two executives
  • There is a nominal executive – the President.
  • There is a real executive – the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers.
2. Prime Minister is the real head
  • The Prime Minister is the head of the government.
  • The Prime Minister leads the Council of Ministers.
3. Council of Ministers
  • The Council of Ministers helps the Prime Minister in running the government.
  • It is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
4. Collective responsibility
  • The Council of Ministers works as a team.
  • If it loses the confidence of the Lok Sabha, it must resign.
5. Close relation between legislature and executive
  • Ministers are generally members of Parliament.
  • The executive is closely connected with the legislature.
6. Majority party forms the government
  • Normally, the party or coalition having majority support in the Lok Sabha forms the government.
7. Opposition
  • The parties that do not form the government act as the Opposition.
  • They question and examine the government's actions.
8. Fixed constitutional head
  • The President is the constitutional head of India.
  • The President normally acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

In short: India's parliamentary system is based on the relationship between the President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers and Parliament.
