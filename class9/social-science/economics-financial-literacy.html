Chapter 2 – An Introduction to Financial Literacy

EXERCISE – 1

VERY SHORT ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

1. What is money?

Answer: Money is anything that is generally accepted as a means of exchange for goods and services.

2. Give an example of non-legal tender money.

Answer: Cheque is an example of non-legal tender money.

3. Whose definition of money is the most acceptable?

Answer: Crowther's definition of money is considered the most acceptable.

4. What is the fifth stage in the evolution of money?

Answer: Digital money is the fifth stage in the evolution of money.

5. Give two examples of UPI payment app.

Answer: 1. Google Pay
2. PhonePe

6. What is the standard money in India?

Answer: The Indian Rupee (₹) is the standard money in India.

7. The refusal of which type of money will be violation of law?

Answer: Refusal of legal tender money is a violation of law.

SHORT ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

1. What are the characteristics of money?

Answer:
  1. It should be generally accepted.
  2. It should be easy to carry.
  3. It should be durable.
  4. It should be divisible.
  5. It should have stable value.
  6. It should be easily recognisable.

2. How does the lack of double coincidence of wants create problems in the barter system?

Answer: In the barter system, two persons must have each other's goods that they want to exchange. For example, A has rice and wants shoes. B has shoes but does not want rice. In this case, exchange cannot take place. Therefore, the lack of double coincidence of wants makes barter difficult.

3. What is money-of-account and money proper?

Answer: Money-of-account: It is the unit in which the value of goods and services is measured. In India, the rupee is the money-of-account.
Money proper: It is the money that is actually used for making payments.

4. What is meant by store of value?

Answer: Store of value means the ability of money to keep its value and be saved for future use. For example, we can save money today and use it later to buy goods and services.

5. Why is money the common unit of measurement of the value of goods and services?

Answer: Money provides a common unit for expressing the value of different goods and services. For example, the price of a book may be ₹200 and a bag may be ₹500. Thus, money helps us compare their values easily.

6. What is the difference between limited and unlimited legal tender?

Answer:
Limited Legal TenderUnlimited Legal Tender

7. What is plastic money and digital money?

Answer: Plastic money: Payment instruments made of plastic, such as debit cards and credit cards, are called plastic money.
Digital money: Money used or transferred through digital or electronic methods is called digital money. Examples include UPI payments, mobile banking and digital wallets.

LONG ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

1. Explain the types of money.

Answer:
  1. Commodity Money: Goods that were used as money in the early stages. Example: cattle, salt and grains.
  2. Metallic Money: Coins made of metals such as gold, silver and copper.
  3. Paper Money: Currency notes issued by the government or authorised monetary authority.
  4. Credit Money: Money created through banking and credit instruments. Example: cheques and other credit instruments.
  5. Digital Money: Money used through electronic and digital systems. Example: UPI payments and digital wallets.

2. Is cheque money? Give reasons for your answer.

Answer: A cheque is not legal tender money, but it is an important means of making payments.
  1. A cheque is an instruction to a bank to make payment.
  2. It is not compulsory for a person to accept a cheque.
  3. It is accepted only when the receiver agrees to accept it.
  4. It helps in making payments without using cash.
  5. It is therefore considered non-legal tender money.
Thus, a cheque can be used as a means of payment but it is not legal tender money.

3. What are the stages in the evolution of money?

Answer:
  1. Barter system – Goods were exchanged directly for other goods.
  2. Commodity money – Useful goods such as cattle, salt and grains were used as money.
  3. Metallic money – Metals such as gold, silver and copper were used.
  4. Paper money – Paper currency was introduced for making payments.
  5. Digital money – Money is now transferred electronically through banks, UPI and other digital systems.

4. “Financial transactions have undergone a major shift from Cash Payment to Digital Payment.” Explain.

Answer:
  1. Easy and convenient: Payments can be made using a mobile phone.
  2. Fast: Digital payments are completed quickly.
  3. UPI: UPI allows direct transfer of money between bank accounts.
  4. Less need for cash: People do not always need to carry cash.
  5. Easy records: Digital transactions provide a transaction record.
  6. Online shopping: Digital payment is useful for online purchases.
  7. Banking facilities: Mobile and internet banking have made digital transactions easier.
Examples include UPI, debit cards, credit cards, mobile banking and digital wallets.

5. What are the functions of money?

Answer:
  1. Medium of exchange: Money is used to buy and sell goods and services.
  2. Measure of value: Money measures the value of goods and services.
  3. Store of value: Money can be saved for future use.
  4. Standard of deferred payment: Future payments can be expressed in terms of money.
  5. Transfer of value: Money makes it easy to transfer purchasing power from one person to another.

6. State the role of money in an economy.

Answer:
  1. It makes buying and selling easy.
  2. It removes the difficulties of the barter system.
  3. It helps to determine the value and price of goods.
  4. It encourages trade and commerce.
  5. It helps people save wealth.
  6. It makes investment easier.
  7. It helps banks and financial institutions provide credit.
  8. It supports the overall economic activities of a country.

EXERCISE – 2

VERY SHORT ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

1. Give the meaning of the following:

Answer:
  • Financial literacy: The knowledge and skills needed to manage money properly.
  • Income: The money earned by a person from different sources.
  • Expense: The money spent on goods, services and other needs.
  • Savings: The part of income that is not spent.
  • Investment: Putting money into an asset or financial instrument to earn a return in the future.
  • Financial Goals: Financial targets that a person wants to achieve in the future.

2. What percentage of the population is financially literate in India?

Answer: 27% of the population is financially literate in India.

3. Define financial fraud.

Answer: Financial fraud is the illegal use of deception to obtain money or financial benefits.

4. Give an example of wants of an individual.

Answer: Buying a new mobile phone is an example of an individual's want.

5. Define the acronym SMART.

Answer:
  • S – Specific
  • M – Measurable
  • A – Achievable
  • R – Relevant
  • T – Time-bound

6. Mention two investment options.

Answer: 1. Fixed Deposit
2. Mutual Funds

7. Give the full form of SEBI.

Answer: SEBI – Securities and Exchange Board of India

SHORT ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

1. What is the importance of financial literacy?

Answer:
  1. Manage income properly.
  2. Control unnecessary expenses.
  3. Develop the habit of saving.
  4. Make proper investment decisions.
  5. Avoid financial fraud.
  6. Achieve financial goals.
  7. Prepare for emergencies.

2. Why is savings important for an individual?

Answer:
  1. Help meet future needs.
  2. Help during emergencies.
  3. Help achieve financial goals.
  4. Provide financial security.
  5. Provide money for future investment.

3. What are the types of expenses?

Answer:
  1. Fixed expenses: Expenses that remain more or less the same, such as rent.
  2. Variable expenses: Expenses that change according to use, such as food, electricity and entertainment.

4. Why should financial goals be achievable? Give an example.

Answer: Financial goals should be achievable so that a person can realistically complete them without creating unnecessary financial pressure. Example: If a person earns ₹20,000 per month, saving ₹5,000 per month for a planned purchase may be an achievable goal.

5. What are the sources from which a person can earn income?

Answer: Salary or wages, business, rent, interest, profit and investment returns.

6. What is financial planning?

Answer: Financial planning is the process of planning income, expenses, savings and investments to achieve financial goals.

7. Where should savings amount be kept and why?

Answer: Savings should be kept in safe and reliable places, such as a bank savings account or other suitable financial instruments. This provides safety, easy access and, in some cases, interest on savings.

LONG ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS

1. What are the goals of financial planning?

Answer:
  1. Managing income properly.
  2. Controlling expenses.
  3. Increasing savings.
  4. Achieving financial goals.
  5. Planning investments.
  6. Preparing for emergencies.
  7. Providing financial security.
  8. Planning for future needs.

2. Explain the importance of investment.

Answer:
  1. It can help increase wealth.
  2. It can provide returns in the future.
  3. It helps achieve long-term financial goals.
  4. It can help prepare for retirement.
  5. It can help meet future education or housing expenses.
  6. It helps put savings to productive use.
  7. Some investments may help protect money from the effects of inflation.

3. What precautions should a person take against financial fraud?

Answer:
  1. Never share OTP, PIN or password with anyone.
  2. Do not share bank or card details unnecessarily.
  3. Do not click on suspicious links.
  4. Verify the identity of the person or organisation before making payments.
  5. Use only official banking and payment applications.
  6. Check bank statements and transaction messages regularly.
  7. Do not trust offers promising very high returns with no risk.
  8. Report suspicious transactions immediately to the concerned bank or authority.

4. Explain the principles that can guide an individual in taking investment decisions.

Answer:
  1. Understand the investment before investing.
  2. Consider the risk involved.
  3. Consider the expected return.
  4. Invest according to your financial goals.
  5. Consider the time period of the investment.
  6. Diversify investments instead of putting all money in one place.
  7. Choose investments according to your ability to bear risk.
  8. Use reliable and regulated financial institutions.

5. What are the steps of financial planning?

Answer:
  1. Identify financial goals.
  2. Calculate income.
  3. Analyse expenses.
  4. Prepare a budget.
  5. Plan savings.
  6. Plan investments.
  7. Prepare for emergencies.
  8. Review the financial plan regularly.

6. How can an individual be prepared for financial emergencies?

Answer:
  1. Creating an emergency fund.
  2. Saving money regularly.
  3. Avoiding unnecessary debt.
  4. Having suitable insurance.
  5. Keeping some money easily accessible.
  6. Preparing a proper monthly budget.
  7. Maintaining more than one source of financial support where possible.

7. Explain how to set SMART goals.

Answer:
  • S – Specific: The goal should be clear and exact.
  • M – Measurable: The progress should be measurable.
  • A – Achievable: The goal should be possible to achieve.
  • R – Relevant: The goal should be important and useful for the person.
  • T – Time-bound: The goal should have a fixed time limit.
Example: “I will save ₹12,000 in 12 months by saving ₹1,000 every month.” This goal is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.
