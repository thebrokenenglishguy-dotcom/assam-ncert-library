Answers are written in simple English and points. Each answer has no more than five main points. Figures that change over time are clearly marked as estimates or official data.

Exercise — Questions and Answers

1. Write about the geographical location of India.

  1. India is located in the northern and eastern hemispheres.
  2. It lies in South Asia.
  3. Its mainland extends from about 8°4′ N to 37°6′ N latitude.
  4. It extends from about 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E longitude.
  5. The Tropic of Cancer (23°30′ N) passes almost through the middle of India.

2. What is the total length of Indian coastlines?

  1. The revised coastline length of India is 11,098.81 km, including island territories.
  2. The older figure used in many textbooks is 7,516.6 km.
  3. The figure changed in 2025 because modern mapping methods measured more coastal details.

Updated government figure: Press Information Bureau — Coastline of the Country.

3. What is the total length of the land boundaries of India?

  1. India has about 15,106.7 km of land borders.
  2. It shares land borders with Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar.

Source: Ministry of Home Affairs — Border Management.

4. Write a short note on Indian landmass.

  1. India has an area of about 3.28 million square kilometres.
  2. It is the seventh-largest country in the world by area.
  3. It has the Himalayas in the north and the Indian Ocean in the south.
  4. The Arabian Sea lies to the west and the Bay of Bengal lies to the east.
  5. India includes a large mainland and island groups such as Lakshadweep and Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

5. Write down four points of differences between the North and the South Indian rivers.

  • North Indian rivers: Mostly rise in the Himalayas; many are perennial because they receive water from rain and melting snow. South Indian rivers: Mostly rise in the Western Ghats or central highlands; many depend mainly on rainfall.
  • North: Usually have long courses and large drainage basins. South: Many have shorter courses and smaller drainage basins.
  • North: Flow through wide alluvial plains and form large floodplains. South: Often flow over hard rocks and plateau areas.
  • North: The Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems are major examples. South: The Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri and Mahanadi are major examples.

6. What are the physiographic divisions of India?

  1. The Himalayan Mountain Region.
  2. The Northern Plains.
  3. The Peninsular Plateau.
  4. The Indian Desert.
  5. The Coastal Plains.
  6. The Islands.

7. What are the characteristics of the Northern Himalayan Mountain Region of India?

  1. It forms a high mountain wall along the northern border of India.
  2. It has three main parallel ranges: Himadri, Himachal and Shiwalik.
  3. It contains high peaks, deep valleys and glaciers.
  4. Many important rivers, such as the Ganga and Indus systems, begin in this region.
  5. It protects the Indian subcontinent from very cold winds from Central Asia and influences the monsoon.

8. Describe the characteristics of the North Indian Plain.

  1. It lies south of the Himalayas.
  2. It is formed by alluvial deposits brought by the Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra and their tributaries.
  3. It is generally flat and fertile.
  4. It has a dense population and supports intensive agriculture.
  5. Its main divisions are the Punjab Plain, Ganga Plain and Brahmaputra Plain.

9. Describe the characteristics of the Deccan Plateau.

  1. It is a large plateau in southern India, mainly south of the Narmada River.
  2. It is roughly triangular in shape.
  3. The Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats form its western and eastern edges.
  4. It is made mainly of old hard rocks and has rich mineral resources.
  5. Many rivers, such as the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri, flow across it.

10. Mention the characteristics of the Coastal Region of India.

  1. India has western and eastern coastal plains.
  2. The western coastal plain lies between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea. It is generally narrow.
  3. The eastern coastal plain lies between the Eastern Ghats and the Bay of Bengal. It is generally wider.
  4. Large rivers form deltas on the eastern coast.
  5. Fishing, ports, trade, farming and tourism are important activities in coastal areas.

11. Write down the characteristics of the climate of India.

  1. India has a monsoon type of climate.
  2. There are clear seasonal changes in temperature and rainfall.
  3. Most parts receive much of their rainfall during the south-west monsoon.
  4. The Himalayas influence temperature and stop many cold winds from the north.
  5. Climate differs from region to region because of relief, altitude and distance from the sea.

12. Write a note on the impact of monsoons in India.

  1. Monsoon rain supplies water for agriculture and drinking.
  2. It fills rivers, reservoirs and groundwater sources.
  3. Good monsoon rainfall supports crops and the rural economy.
  4. Too little rain can cause drought, while very heavy rain can cause floods and landslides.
  5. Monsoon rainfall affects transport, power generation and many parts of daily life.

13. Outline the pattern of rainfall distribution in India.

  1. Very heavy rainfall occurs in parts of the Western Ghats and north-east India.
  2. The Meghalaya region receives some of the highest rainfall in the world.
  3. Rainfall decreases towards the north-western parts of India.
  4. Rajasthan and parts of Ladakh receive very little rainfall.
  5. The Tamil Nadu coast receives much of its rain during the retreating or north-east monsoon.

14. Write about the types of the vegetations of India.

  1. Tropical evergreen forests.
  2. Tropical deciduous forests.
  3. Tropical thorn forests and scrub.
  4. Montane or mountain forests.
  5. Mangrove or tidal forests.

15. What are the characteristics of the Evergreen vegetation of India?

  1. These forests grow in areas with heavy rainfall and high temperature.
  2. Trees remain green throughout the year.
  3. They are thick and have many layers of trees and plants.
  4. Common trees include ebony, mahogany, rosewood and rubber.
  5. They are found in the Western Ghats, north-east India and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

16. Write about the characteristics of the Mountain Vegetation of India.

  1. Mountain vegetation changes with altitude and temperature.
  2. Lower slopes may have broad-leaved forests.
  3. Higher areas have coniferous trees such as pine, deodar and fir.
  4. At very high altitudes, trees become smaller and alpine grasses and shrubs grow.
  5. These forests are found mainly in the Himalayas and other high mountains.

17. What is the total population of India at present?

  1. The United Nations' 2026 estimate is about 1.477 billion, or about 147.7 crore people.
  2. This is an estimate, not a new Census count.
  3. The latest completed official Census is still the 2011 Census, which recorded about 121 crore people.

Population estimate: UN Population Division — 2026 estimate. Census status: Census of India.

18. What are the different types of migration?

  1. Rural to urban: From villages to towns and cities.
  2. Urban to rural: From cities to villages.
  3. Rural to rural: From one village to another.
  4. Urban to urban: From one town or city to another.
  5. Migration may also be internal (within a country) or international (between countries), and temporary or permanent.

19. What are the impacts of migration on India?

  1. It provides workers to industries, farms and cities.
  2. It can improve income and living standards for migrants and their families.
  3. It increases cultural exchange and connects different regions.
  4. It can cause overcrowding, housing shortages and pressure on city services.
  5. Some villages may lose young workers and face changes in family life.

20. Write down the main features of Indian economy.

  1. India is a developing mixed economy, with both public and private sectors.
  2. Agriculture provides work and food for a large number of people.
  3. Industry and manufacturing produce goods and create jobs.
  4. The service sector, including trade, transport, education, health and information technology, is a major part of the economy.
  5. Economic development is uneven, and poverty and unemployment remain important challenges.

21. What are the Union Territories of India?

  1. Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
  2. Chandigarh.
  3. Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu.
  4. Delhi (National Capital Territory), Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Lakshadweep and Puducherry.
  5. India has 8 Union Territories in total.

22. How many states are there in India at present and name those.

  1. India has 28 states.
  2. Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat.
  3. Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra.
  4. Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Odisha, Punjab, Rajasthan.
  5. Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Tripura, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal.

23. Name the latest state formed in India. Write the area and population of it.

  1. Telangana is the most recently formed Indian state.
  2. It was formed on 2 June 2014.
  3. Its area is 112,077 square kilometres.
  4. Its population was 3,50,03,674 (about 3.5 crore) according to the 2011 Census figures for the Telangana region.
  5. Its capital is Hyderabad.

Source: Government of Telangana — State Profile.
