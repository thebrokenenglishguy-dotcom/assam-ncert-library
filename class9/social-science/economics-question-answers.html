GIVE SHORT ANSWER

1. Write the definition of Economics given by Adam Smith.

Answer: According to Adam Smith, Economics is the science of wealth. It studies the production, distribution and consumption of wealth.

2. Write the definition of Economics given by Marshall.

Answer: According to Alfred Marshall, Economics is the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life. It studies how people earn and use their income for their material welfare.

3. Write the definition of Economics given by Robbins.

Answer: According to Lionel Robbins, Economics studies human behaviour as a relationship between unlimited wants and scarce means which have alternative uses.

4. What are the basic concepts of the definition of Economics given by Robbins?

Answer:
  1. Unlimited wants
  2. Scarcity of resources
  3. Alternative uses of resources
  4. Choice

5. What is Micro Economics?

Answer: Micro Economics studies the economic activities of individual units, such as an individual consumer, a household or a firm.
Example: Study of the price of a particular product.

6. What is Macro Economics?

Answer: Macro Economics studies the economy as a whole.
Example: Study of national income, unemployment, inflation and economic growth.

7. Show the differences between free goods and economic goods.

Answer:
Free GoodsEconomic Goods
They are freely available.They are scarce.
They have no price.They generally have a price.
They are available in sufficient quantity.Their supply is limited.

8. What is National Income?

Answer: National Income is the total income earned by the people of a country from the production of goods and services during a particular period, usually one year.

9. What is Per Capita Income?

Answer: Per Capita Income means the average income per person in a country.

Per Capita Income = National Income ÷ Total Population

GIVE LONG ANSWERS

1. Explain with examples, the importance of the study of Economics.

Answer:
  1. Helps in making choices: Human wants are unlimited but resources are limited. Economics helps us make proper choices.
  2. Proper use of resources: It teaches us how to use scarce resources efficiently.
  3. Helps consumers: It helps consumers decide how to spend their income.
  4. Helps producers: Producers can decide what, how and for whom to produce.
  5. Helps the government: The government uses economic knowledge for taxation, employment and development policies.
  6. Helps to understand problems: It helps us understand poverty, unemployment and inflation.
  7. Helps in national development: Economic planning helps a country improve its standard of living.
Example: A family has limited income. Economics helps the family decide how much money should be spent on food, education, clothing and other needs.

2. Which definition of Economics is the most acceptable and why? Explain.

Answer: The scarcity definition given by Lionel Robbins is generally considered more comprehensive because it explains the basic economic problem faced by human beings.

According to Robbins, Economics studies human behaviour in relation to:
  1. Unlimited wants
  2. Scarce resources
  3. Alternative uses of resources
  4. Choice
Resources are limited, but human wants are unlimited. Therefore, people have to make choices.

Example: A person has ₹1,000 but wants to buy many things. Since the money is limited, he has to choose which things are more important.

Thus, the definition of Robbins explains the basic problem of scarcity and choice in Economics.

3. Write about the scope of Economics.

Answer:
  1. Consumption: It studies how people use goods and services to satisfy their wants.
  2. Production: It studies how goods and services are produced.
  3. Exchange: It studies the buying and selling of goods and services.
  4. Distribution: It studies how income is distributed among the factors of production.
  5. Micro Economics: It studies individual consumers, firms and markets.
  6. Macro Economics: It studies the economy as a whole, including national income, employment, inflation and economic growth.
Therefore, Economics covers almost all important activities related to the production, consumption, exchange and distribution of goods and services.

EXERCISE — CHALLENGES FACED BY INDIAN ECONOMY

GIVE VERY SHORT ANSWER

1. Define — (a) Poverty

Answer: Poverty is a condition in which a person is unable to fulfil his basic needs of life.

(b) Unemployment

Answer: Unemployment is a situation in which a person who is willing and able to work does not get work.

(c) Density of population

Answer: Density of population means the number of people living per square kilometre of area.

Population Density = Total Population ÷ Total Area

(d) Sex ratio

Answer: Sex ratio is the number of females per 1,000 males in the population.

2. What is poverty line? What is the poverty line in rural and urban areas?

Answer: The poverty line is the minimum level of income or consumption needed to fulfil the basic necessities of life.

The textbook gives different poverty-line standards for rural and urban areas.

3. What percentage of the total land area of the world is in India?

Answer: India accounts for about 2.4% of the total land area of the world.

4. Which state of India has the highest sex ratio and how much is it?

Answer: Kerala has the highest sex ratio, with 1,084 females per 1,000 males according to the 2011 Census.

5. What is the density of population of Assam?

Answer: The population density of Assam was 398 persons per square kilometre according to the 2011 Census.

6. What is disguised unemployment?

Answer: Disguised unemployment is a situation where more people are employed than actually required, so removing some workers does not reduce total production.

7. What is inflation?

Answer: Inflation is a continuous rise in the general price level of goods and services.

8. What are the causes of inflation?

Answer:
  1. Increase in demand
  2. Increase in cost of production
  3. Increase in money supply
  4. Shortage of goods and services

GIVE LONG ANSWERS

1. Discuss the main causes for the problem of rapid growth of population in India.

Answer:
  1. High birth rate
  2. Decline in death rate
  3. Early marriage
  4. Lack of education
  5. Lack of awareness about family planning
  6. Social and religious beliefs
  7. Preference for larger families
  8. Improvement in medical facilities
  9. Reduction in infant mortality
  10. Poverty and lack of social security
These factors together have contributed to rapid population growth.

2. What is poverty? Explain how the poverty line determines poverty.

Answer: Poverty is a condition in which a person cannot fulfil his basic needs such as food, clothing, housing, education and health care.

The poverty line is used to identify people who are unable to meet the minimum basic requirements of life.

People whose income or consumption is below the poverty line are considered poor.

The poverty line therefore helps the government:
  1. Identify poor people.
  2. Measure the extent of poverty.
  3. Prepare poverty-reduction programmes.
  4. Provide benefits to poor families.

3. What is inflation? Discuss the main effects of inflation.

Answer: Inflation means a continuous rise in the general price level of goods and services.

Main effects of inflation:
  1. Purchasing power falls: The value of money decreases.
  2. Cost of living increases: People need more money to buy the same goods.
  3. Fixed-income earners suffer: People with fixed incomes are adversely affected.
  4. Poor people suffer: They find it difficult to buy essential goods.
  5. Savings may decrease: People may have less money left for saving.
  6. Cost of production increases: Producers may have to pay more for raw materials and wages.
  7. Economic inequality may increase: Different groups may be affected differently.

4. What is unemployment? What are its different types? Mention the main causes of this problem.

Answer: Unemployment is a situation in which a person is willing and able to work but cannot find a suitable job.

Types of unemployment:
  1. Seasonal unemployment: People get work only during certain seasons.
  2. Disguised unemployment: More people are employed than actually required.
  3. Educated unemployment: Educated people are unable to find suitable jobs.
  4. Structural unemployment: Jobs are not available because of changes in the structure of the economy.
  5. Frictional unemployment: Temporary unemployment while changing from one job to another.
Main causes:
  1. Rapid population growth
  2. Slow growth of employment opportunities
  3. Lack of proper education and skills
  4. Dependence on agriculture
  5. Slow industrial development
  6. Lack of vocational training
  7. Technological changes

5. Write short notes: (a) Seasonal Unemployment

Answer: Seasonal unemployment occurs when people get employment only during certain seasons of the year. It is common in the agricultural sector. For example, agricultural workers may get work during sowing and harvesting seasons but may remain unemployed during the remaining period.

(b) Demand-pull and Cost-push Inflation

Demand-pull Inflation: Demand-pull inflation occurs when demand for goods and services becomes greater than their supply.

In simple words: More demand + limited supply → Rise in prices.

Cost-push Inflation: Cost-push inflation occurs when the cost of production increases, causing producers to increase the prices of goods.

For example, an increase in the price of raw materials, fuel or wages can increase the cost of production.

In simple words: Higher production cost → Higher prices.
