1.1 Number Types and Their Relationships

Natural numbers (N)

Counting numbers are called natural numbers.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …}

Whole numbers (W)

Whole numbers include zero and all natural numbers.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …}

Integers (Z)

Integers include negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers.

Z = {…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …}

Rational numbers (Q)

A number is rational if it can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Examples: 1/2, −3/4, 5 (= 5/1), and 0.25 (= 1/4).

Irrational numbers

A number is irrational if it cannot be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-recurring (non-repeating).

Examples: √2, √3 and π.

Real numbers (R)

All rational and irrational numbers together form the real numbers.

Real numbers = Rational numbers ∪ Irrational numbers

Remember: Every natural number is a whole number; every whole number is an integer; every integer is rational; and every rational number is real. Irrational numbers are also real, but they are not rational.

1.2 Irrational Numbers and Their Representation

Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers. Their decimal digits continue forever without forming a repeating block.

Examples

  • √2 = 1.414213…
  • √3 = 1.732050…
  • √5 = 2.236067…

Representing √2 on the number line

  1. Draw a number line and mark O = 0 and A = 1.
  2. At A, draw a perpendicular line segment AB of length 1 unit.
  3. Join O to B. By Pythagoras' theorem, OB² = OA² + AB² = 1² + 1² = 2.
  4. Therefore OB = √2.
  5. With O as centre and OB as radius, draw an arc to cut the number line. The point of intersection represents √2.
If a right triangle has legs 1 and 1, its hypotenuse = √(1² + 1²) = √2.

The same construction idea can be extended to other square roots by using a right triangle with suitable side lengths.

1.3 Decimal Expansions of Real Numbers

A real number can have a terminating decimal expansion or a non-terminating decimal expansion.

Terminating decimal

A decimal that ends after a finite number of digits is terminating.

Example: 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4. The decimal ends, so it is terminating and rational.

Non-terminating recurring decimal

A decimal that continues forever but repeats a digit or a block of digits is recurring. It represents a rational number.

Example: 0.3333… = 1/3 and 0.272727… = 27/99 = 3/11.

Non-terminating non-recurring decimal

A decimal that continues forever without repeating a fixed block is non-recurring. It represents an irrational number.

Example: √2 = 1.414213… is non-terminating and non-recurring.
Rational number ⇔ terminating or non-terminating recurring decimal expansion
Irrational number ⇔ non-terminating, non-recurring decimal expansion

Converting a recurring decimal into p/q form

Example: Convert 0.777… into a fraction.

Let x = 0.777…

Then 10x = 7.777…

Subtract: 10x − x = 7

9x = 7, so x = 7/9.

Therefore 0.777… = 7/9.

1.4 Operations on Real Numbers

Real numbers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication. They are also closed under division when the divisor is not zero.

  • Addition: the sum of two real numbers is real.
  • Subtraction: the difference of two real numbers is real.
  • Multiplication: the product of two real numbers is real.
  • Division: the quotient of two real numbers is real if the denominator is not zero.

Useful properties

Commutative: a + b = b + a; ab = ba
Associative: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c); (ab)c = a(bc)
Distributive: a(b + c) = ab + ac
Additive identity: a + 0 = a
Multiplicative identity: a × 1 = a
Additive inverse: a + (−a) = 0
Multiplicative inverse: a × (1/a) = 1, for a ≠ 0

Operations with surds

Only like surds can be added or subtracted directly. Simplify surds first where possible.

Example: 2√3 + 5√3 = 7√3.
Example: √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.

For multiplication, use √a × √b = √(ab), where a and b are non-negative.

Example: √2 × √8 = √16 = 4.

Quick Revision

  • Natural numbers begin at 1; whole numbers begin at 0.
  • Integers include negative numbers, zero and positive numbers.
  • Rational numbers can be written as p/q, with q ≠ 0.
  • Irrational numbers have non-terminating, non-recurring decimals.
  • Every rational and irrational number is a real number.
  • Terminating and recurring decimals are rational.
  • Non-terminating, non-recurring decimals are irrational.
  • Use Pythagoras' theorem to construct square roots on a number line.
  • Division by zero is undefined.
  • Like surds can be added or subtracted after simplification.
