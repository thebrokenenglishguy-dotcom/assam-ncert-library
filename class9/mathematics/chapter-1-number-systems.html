1.1 Number Types and Their Relationships

Natural numbers (N)

Counting numbers are called natural numbers.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …}

Whole numbers (W)

Whole numbers include zero and all natural numbers.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …}

Integers (Z)

Integers include negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers.

Z = {…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …}

Rational numbers (Q)

A number is rational if it can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Examples: 1/2, −3/4, 5 (= 5/1), and 0.25 (= 1/4).

Irrational numbers

A number is irrational if it cannot be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-recurring (non-repeating).

Examples: √2, √3 and π.

Real numbers (R)

All rational and irrational numbers together form the real numbers.

Real numbers = Rational numbers ∪ Irrational numbers

Remember: Every natural number is a whole number; every whole number is an integer; every integer is rational; and every rational number is real. Irrational numbers are also real, but they are not rational.

1.2 Irrational Numbers and Their Representation

Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers. Their decimal digits continue forever without forming a repeating block.

Examples

  • √2 = 1.414213…
  • √3 = 1.732050…
  • √5 = 2.236067…

Representing √2 on the number line

  1. Draw a number line and mark O = 0 and A = 1.
  2. At A, draw a perpendicular line segment AB of length 1 unit.
  3. Join O to B. By Pythagoras' theorem, OB² = OA² + AB² = 1² + 1² = 2.
  4. Therefore OB = √2.
  5. With O as centre and OB as radius, draw an arc to cut the number line. The point of intersection represents √2.
If a right triangle has legs 1 and 1, its hypotenuse = √(1² + 1²) = √2.

The same construction idea can be extended to other square roots by using a right triangle with suitable side lengths.

1.3 Decimal Expansions of Real Numbers

A real number can have a terminating decimal expansion or a non-terminating decimal expansion.

Terminating decimal

A decimal that ends after a finite number of digits is terminating.

Example: 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4. The decimal ends, so it is terminating and rational.

Non-terminating recurring decimal

A decimal that continues forever but repeats a digit or a block of digits is recurring. It represents a rational number.

Example: 0.3333… = 1/3 and 0.272727… = 27/99 = 3/11.

Non-terminating non-recurring decimal

A decimal that continues forever without repeating a fixed block is non-recurring. It represents an irrational number.

Example: √2 = 1.414213… is non-terminating and non-recurring.
Rational number ⇔ terminating or non-terminating recurring decimal expansion
Irrational number ⇔ non-terminating, non-recurring decimal expansion

Converting a recurring decimal into p/q form

Example: Convert 0.777… into a fraction.

Let x = 0.777…

Then 10x = 7.777…

Subtract: 10x − x = 7

9x = 7, so x = 7/9.

Therefore 0.777… = 7/9.

1.4 Operations on Real Numbers

Real numbers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication. They are also closed under division when the divisor is not zero.

  • Addition: the sum of two real numbers is real.
  • Subtraction: the difference of two real numbers is real.
  • Multiplication: the product of two real numbers is real.
  • Division: the quotient of two real numbers is real if the denominator is not zero.

Useful properties

Commutative: a + b = b + a; ab = ba
Associative: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c); (ab)c = a(bc)
Distributive: a(b + c) = ab + ac
Additive identity: a + 0 = a
Multiplicative identity: a × 1 = a
Additive inverse: a + (−a) = 0
Multiplicative inverse: a × (1/a) = 1, for a ≠ 0

Operations with surds

Only like surds can be added or subtracted directly. Simplify surds first where possible.

Example: 2√3 + 5√3 = 7√3.
Example: √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.

For multiplication, use √a × √b = √(ab), where a and b are non-negative.

Example: √2 × √8 = √16 = 4.

1.5 Laws of Exponents for Real Numbers

For a positive real number a and rational numbers p and q, the laws of exponents help us simplify powers.

aᵖ × aᑫ = aᵖ⁺ᑫ
aᵖ ÷ aᑫ = aᵖ⁻ᑫ (a ≠ 0)
(aᵖ)ᑫ = aᵖᑫ
aᵖ × bᵖ = (ab)ᵖ
a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0), and a⁻ᵖ = 1/aᵖ
Example 1: Simplify 2^(2/3) × 2^(1/3).

Same base, so add the powers:

2^(2/3 + 1/3) = 2^1 = 2.

Example 2: Simplify (3^(1/5))^4.

Multiply the powers: 3^(1/5 × 4) = 3^(4/5).

Example 3: Simplify 7^(1/5) ÷ 7^(1/3).

Subtract the powers: 7^(1/5 − 1/3) = 7^((3−5)/15) = 7^(−2/15).

Example 4: Simplify 13^(1/5) × 17^(1/5).

Same power: (13 × 17)^(1/5) = 221^(1/5).

Exercise 1.1

Q1. Is zero a rational number? Write it in p/q form.

Yes. A rational number can be written as p/q, where q is not zero.

0 = 0/1, and 0 and 1 are integers.

Answer: Yes, zero is rational.

Q2. Find six rational numbers between 3 and 4.

Write 3 and 4 with denominator 7:

3 = 21/7 and 4 = 28/7.

The six fractions between them are 22/7, 23/7, 24/7, 25/7, 26/7 and 27/7.

Answer: 22/7, 23/7, 24/7, 25/7, 26/7, 27/7.

Q3. Find five rational numbers between 3/5 and 4/5.

Make the denominators large enough: 3/5 = 18/30 and 4/5 = 24/30.

Five fractions between them are 19/30, 20/30, 21/30, 22/30 and 23/30.

Answer: 19/30, 20/30, 21/30, 22/30, 23/30.

Q4. State whether the following statements are true or false. Give reasons for your answers.

(i) Every natural number is a whole number.

True. Natural numbers (1, 2, 3, …) are included in whole numbers (0, 1, 2, 3, …).

(ii) Every integer is a whole number.

False. Integers include negative numbers, such as −1, but whole numbers do not include negative numbers.

(iii) Every rational number is a whole number.

False. Rational numbers include fractions such as 1/2, which are not whole numbers.

Answer: (i) True, (ii) False, (iii) False.

Q5. Every natural number is (i) a whole number, (ii) an integer, (iii) a rational number, (iv) a real number. Which option is correct?

Every natural number is a whole number. Whole numbers are integers. Every natural number n can be written as n/1, so it is rational. All rational numbers are real numbers.

Answer: Option (D) — (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) are all correct.

Q6. Every rational number is — (A) a natural number, (B) a whole number, (C) an integer, or (D) a real number.

Rational numbers are included in the set of real numbers. But not every rational number is natural, whole or an integer. For example, 1/2 is rational but is not a natural number, whole number or integer.

Answer: Option (D) — a real number.

Exercise 1.2

Q1(i). Every irrational number is a real number. True or false?

True. Real numbers contain both rational and irrational numbers.

Answer: True.

Q1(ii). Every point on the number line is of the form √m, where m is a natural number. True or false?

False. The number line contains negative numbers, zero, fractions and many other real numbers. For example, −2 cannot be written as √m for a natural number m.

Answer: False.

Q1(iii). Every real number is irrational. True or false?

False. Real numbers include rational numbers too. For example, 1/2 is real and rational.

Answer: False.

Q2. Are the square roots of all positive integers irrational?

No. If the integer is a perfect square, its square root is an integer and therefore rational. For example, √25 = 5 = 5/1.

Answer: No. Example: √25 = 5.

Q5. In the square-root spiral, which segment represents √5?

Each new right triangle adds a perpendicular side of length 1. The hypotenuse lengths are √2, √3, √4 and then √5. Therefore the segment from O to P₄ represents √5.

Answer: OP₄ (Option A).

Exercise 1.3

Q1(i). Write 36/100 in decimal form and state its type.

36 ÷ 100 = 0.36. It ends after two decimal places.

Answer: 0.36, terminating decimal.

Q1(ii). Write 1/11 in decimal form and state its type.

1 ÷ 11 = 0.090909… The block 09 repeats.

Answer: 0.090909…, non-terminating recurring decimal.

Q1(iii). Write 4 1/8 in decimal form and state its type.

4 1/8 = 4 + 0.125 = 4.125.

Answer: 4.125, terminating decimal.

Q1(iv). Write 3/13 in decimal form and state its type.

3 ÷ 13 = 0.230769230769… The block 230769 repeats.

Answer: 0.230769…, non-terminating recurring decimal.

Q2. If 1/7 = 0.142857… predict 2/7, 3/7, 4/7, 5/7 and 6/7.

Multiply 0.142857… by the numerator:

2/7 = 0.285714…; 3/7 = 0.428571…; 4/7 = 0.571428…; 5/7 = 0.714285…; 6/7 = 0.857142….

All are non-terminating recurring decimals.

Q3(i). Express 0.6̅ in p/q form.

Let x = 0.666… Then 10x = 6.666….

Subtract: 10x − x = 6, so 9x = 6 and x = 6/9 = 2/3.

Answer: 2/3.

Q3(ii). Express 0.4̅7̅ (0.474747…) in p/q form.

Let x = 0.474747… Then 100x = 47.474747….

Subtract: 100x − x = 47, so 99x = 47.

Answer: 47/99.

Exercise 1.4

Q2(i). Simplify (3 + √3)(2 + √2).

Multiply each term: 3×2 + 3√2 + 2√3 + √3×√2.

Answer: 6 + 3√2 + 2√3 + √6.

Q6(i). If x = 1 + √2, show that (x − 1/x)³ = 8.

First, 1/x = 1/(1+√2). Rationalise: 1/x = (1−√2)/(1−2) = √2−1.

Therefore x − 1/x = (1+√2) − (√2−1) = 2.

So (x − 1/x)³ = 2³ = 8.

Hence proved.

Q6(ii). Simplify 1/(1+√2) + 1/(√2+√3) + 1/(√3+2).

Rationalise each term using the conjugate:

1/(1+√2) = √2−1.

1/(√2+√3) = √3−√2.

1/(√3+2) = 2−√3.

Add: (√2−1) + (√3−√2) + (2−√3) = 1.

Answer: 1.

Q6(iii). Add 1.2̅7̅ and 0.1̅3̅.

1.272727… = 1 + 27/99 = 1 + 3/11 = 14/11.

0.131313… = 13/99.

Sum = 14/11 + 13/99 = 126/99 + 13/99 = 139/99.

Answer: 139/99 = 1.404040….

Q8. Find three different irrational numbers between 5/7 and 9/11.

5/7 ≈ 0.7143 and 9/11 ≈ 0.8182. Three examples in this interval are √(3/5), √(5/8) and √(2/3), approximately 0.7746, 0.7906 and 0.8165. These are irrational because their square roots are not rational.

Answer: √(3/5), √(5/8), √(2/3).

Q9. Classify the numbers.

(i) √23 is irrational because 23 is not a perfect square.

(ii) √225 = 15, so it is rational.

(iii) 0.3796 is terminating, so it is rational.

(iv) 7.478478… is recurring (the block 478 repeats), so it is rational.

(v) 1.101001000100001… is non-terminating and non-recurring, so it is irrational.

Exercise 1.5

Q1(i). Find 64^(1/2).

64^(1/2) = √64 = 8.

Q1(ii). Find 32^(1/5).

32 = 2⁵, so 32^(1/5) = 2.

Q1(iii). Find 125^(1/3).

125 = 5³, so 125^(1/3) = 5.

Q2(iv). Find 125^(−1/3).

Negative exponent means reciprocal: 125^(−1/3) = 1/125^(1/3) = 1/5.

Answer: 1/5.

Q3(iii). Simplify 11^(1/2) ÷ 11^(1/4).

Subtract exponents: 11^(1/2−1/4) = 11^(1/4).

Answer: ⁴√11.

Multiple-choice questions visible on the uploaded pages

Number Systems MCQ (i): √2 is —

√2 cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers.

Answer: Irrational number (Option D).

Number Systems MCQ (ii): The decimal representation of a rational number cannot be —

A rational decimal is either terminating or non-terminating recurring. It cannot be non-terminating non-recurring.

Answer: Non-terminating non-recurring (Option D).

Number Systems MCQ (iii): The decimal representation of irrational numbers is —

Answer: Non-terminating non-recurring (Option C).

Number Systems MCQ (iv): The sum of any two irrational numbers is —

It can be rational or irrational. For example, √2 + (−√2) = 0 (rational), while √2 + √3 is irrational.

Answer: Either rational or irrational (Option D).

Match the columns: 32^(1/5), 81^(1/4), 625^(1/4), 5096⁰.

32^(1/5) = 2; 81^(1/4) = 3; 625^(1/4) = 5; and 5096⁰ = 1.

Matching: P→3, Q→4, R→1, S→2 (Option C).

Increasing order: ³√2, ⁶√3, ⁹√4.

Approximate values: ⁶√3 ≈ 1.2009; ⁹√4 ≈ 1.1665; ³√2 ≈ 1.2599. Thus the correct increasing order is ⁹√4, ⁶√3, ³√2.

Correct order: ⁹√4 < ⁶√3 < ³√2.

Quick Revision

  • Natural numbers begin at 1; whole numbers begin at 0.
  • Integers include negative numbers, zero and positive numbers.
  • Rational numbers can be written as p/q, with q ≠ 0.
  • Irrational numbers have non-terminating, non-recurring decimals.
  • Every rational and irrational number is a real number.
  • Terminating and recurring decimals are rational.
  • Non-terminating, non-recurring decimals are irrational.
  • Use Pythagoras' theorem to construct square roots on a number line.
  • Division by zero is undefined.
  • Like surds can be added or subtracted after simplification.
