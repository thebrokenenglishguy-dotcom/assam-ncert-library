Class 9 Mathematics — Revision

First part · Number System, arithmetic, indices, percentages and interest

About this revision: These notes follow the revision pages supplied from the textbook (pages 1–10). Examples are arranged by topic, with calculations written step by step. The repeating-decimal notation means the marked digit or group repeats forever.

1. Number System

Natural numbers (N)

Numbers used for counting are called natural numbers.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …}

Whole numbers (W)

Whole numbers include zero along with all natural numbers.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …}

Integers (Z)

Integers include negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers.

Z = {…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …}

Rational numbers (Q)

A number that can be expressed as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0, is a rational number. Examples: 1/2, 1/3, 2/3, −5/4.

Irrational numbers

Numbers that cannot be expressed in the form p/q (with integers p and q, q ≠ 0) are irrational numbers. Their decimal expansions are non-terminating and non-repeating. Examples: √2 and π.

Real numbers (R)

All rational and irrational numbers together form the real numbers.

R = Q ∪ {irrational numbers}

2. Fundamental Mathematical Operations

The four basic arithmetic operations are addition (+), subtraction (−), multiplication (×) and division (÷).

  • Identity with zero: a + 0 = a; a − 0 = a; a × 1 = a.
  • Division by zero: 0 ÷ a = 0 when a ≠ 0, but a ÷ 0 is undefined.
  • Commutative property: a + b = b + a and ab = ba. This does not generally hold for subtraction or division.
  • Associative property: a + (b + c) = (a + b) + c and a(bc) = (ab)c.
  • Distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac and (a + b)c = ac + bc.

3. Indices (Exponents) and Their Laws

In aⁿ, a is the base and n is the index or exponent. It means that a is multiplied by itself n times.

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (a ≠ 0)
a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0)
a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ (a ≠ 0)
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
(ab)ᵐ = aᵐbᵐ
(a/b)ᵐ = aᵐ/bᵐ (b ≠ 0)
(a/b)⁻ⁿ = (b/a)ⁿ

4. Even, Odd, Prime and Coprime Numbers

  • Even numbers: integers divisible by 2; general form 2m, where m is an integer.
  • Odd numbers: integers not divisible by 2; general form 2m + 1.
  • Prime numbers: natural numbers greater than 1 having exactly two distinct factors, 1 and the number itself. Examples: 2, 5, 11.
  • Important: 1 is not a prime number because it has only one distinct factor. 2 is the only even prime number.
  • Coprime numbers: two integers whose only common positive factor is 1. Example: 8 and 15.

5. Conversion of Decimal Fractions into p/q Form

Terminating decimals

Example 1: Express 3.52 as p/q.

3.52 = 352/100 = 88/25

Answer: 88/25

Example 2: Express 3.523 as p/q.

3.523 = 3523/1000

Answer: 3523/1000

Repeating decimals

Example 3: Express 0.818181… as p/q.

Let x = 0.818181…

100x = 81.818181…

Subtract: 100x − x = 81

99x = 81, so x = 81/99 = 9/11.

Answer: 9/11

Example 4: Express 0.811111… as p/q.

Let x = 0.811111…

10x = 8.111111… (1)

100x = 81.111111… (2)

Subtract (1) from (2): 90x = 81 − 8 = 73.

x = 73/90

Answer: 73/90

Example 5: Express 3.811111… as p/q.

3.811111… = 3 + 0.811111… = 3 + 73/90 = 270/90 + 73/90

= 343/90

Answer: 343/90

6. Percentage

Percentage means “per hundred”. Its symbol is %.

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100%
Example 6: ₹2,450 is saved from ₹5,000. Find the saving percentage.

(2450 ÷ 5000) × 100 = 49%

Answer: 49%

Example 7: Express 49% as a fraction.

49% = 49/100

Answer: 49/100

Example 8: Express 4/5 as a percentage.

(4/5) × 100% = 80%

Answer: 80%

Example 9: Express 0.08 as a percentage.

0.08 × 100% = 8%

Answer: 8%

Example 10: A number reduced by 5% becomes 133. Find the number.

Let the number be x. After a 5% reduction, 95% remains.

(95/100)x = 133

x = (133 × 100)/95 = 140

Answer: 140

Example 11: 80% of the voters cast their votes. The winner received 65% of the votes cast, equal to 572,000 votes. Find the total number of voters.

Let the total number of voters be x.

Votes cast = 80% of x = 4x/5.

Winner’s votes = 65% of 4x/5 = 52x/100.

52x/100 = 572000 ⇒ x = (572000 × 100)/52 = 11,00,000

Answer: 11,00,000 voters

7. Profit and Loss

Profit and loss are calculated on the cost price (CP).

Profit = Selling Price (SP) − Cost Price (CP)
Loss = Cost Price (CP) − Selling Price (SP)
Profit % = (Profit ÷ CP) × 100
Loss % = (Loss ÷ CP) × 100
Example 12: An object is sold for ₹1,800 and the profit is ₹300. Find the profit percentage.

CP = SP − Profit = 1800 − 300 = ₹1,500.

Profit % = (300/1500) × 100 = 20%

Answer: 20%

Example 13: Two fans are sold for ₹630 each. There is a 5% profit on the first and a 10% loss on the second. Find the overall profit or loss.

First fan: CP = (100 × 630)/105 = ₹600.

Second fan: CP = (100 × 630)/90 = ₹700.

Total CP = 600 + 700 = ₹1,300.

Total SP = 630 + 630 = ₹1,260.

Loss = 1,300 − 1,260 = ₹40.

Answer: Overall loss = ₹40.

8. Percentage in Bank Transactions — Simple Interest

Principal (P) is the original money deposited or borrowed. Interest (I) is the extra money paid or earned. Amount (A) is the total money after adding interest. Rate (r) is the annual interest rate and time (t) is measured in years.

I = (P × r × t)/100
A = P + I = P(1 + rt/100)
Example 14: A loan of ₹9,000 is taken at 4% per annum. Interest is paid monthly. Find the monthly interest.

Yearly interest = (9000 × 4 × 1)/100 = ₹360.

Monthly interest = 360/12 = ₹30.

Answer: ₹30 per month.

Example 15: ₹3,600 becomes ₹5,220 in 5 years. Find the rate of interest, then find the principal needed to get ₹47,270 in 7 years at the same rate.

Interest in 5 years = 5220 − 3600 = ₹1,620.

Rate = (1620 × 100)/(3600 × 5) = 9% per annum.

At 9% for 7 years, interest on ₹100 = ₹9 × 7 = ₹63, so amount on ₹100 = ₹163.

Required principal = (100 × 47270)/163 = ₹29,000.

Answer: Rate = 9% p.a.; required principal = ₹29,000.
