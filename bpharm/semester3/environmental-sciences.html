Unit I — Environmental Pollution

Q1. Define environmental pollution.

Answer: Environmental pollution is an undesirable change in air, water, or land that harms living organisms, ecosystems, or human health.

Q2. What is the scope of environmental studies?

Answer: It studies natural resources, ecosystems, biodiversity, pollution, environmental laws, and ways to protect the environment.

Q3. Why are environmental studies important for pharmacists?

Answer: Pharmacists handle medicines, chemicals, packaging, and laboratory waste. Environmental knowledge helps them reduce pollution and dispose of waste safely.

Q4. Name the major types of environmental pollution.

Answer: Air, water, soil or land, noise, and radioactive or nuclear pollution.

Q5. Give four causes of air pollution.

Answer: Vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, burning of waste or fuels, and dust from construction.

Q6. State four effects of air pollution.

Answer: It can cause asthma and other breathing problems, heart disease, smog, acid rain, and damage to plants.

Q7. How can air pollution be controlled?

Answer: Use cleaner fuels, control industrial emissions, maintain vehicles, avoid open burning, and increase green cover.

Q8. What is water pollution?

Answer: Water pollution occurs when harmful substances or microorganisms enter water and make it unsafe for people or ecosystems.

Q9. Mention causes of water pollution.

Answer: Untreated sewage, industrial effluents, agricultural chemicals, oil spills, and pharmaceutical residues.

Q10. How can water pollution be reduced?

Answer: Treat sewage and industrial effluent, prevent dumping, control chemical use, and monitor water quality.

Q11. What is soil pollution?

Answer: Soil pollution is the build-up of harmful chemicals, metals, waste, or other contaminants in soil.

Q12. What are the effects of soil pollution?

Answer: It can reduce soil fertility, harm soil organisms, contaminate crops and groundwater, and enter the food chain.

Q13. What is noise pollution?

Answer: Noise pollution is unwanted or excessive sound that disturbs people or animals.

Q14. What is nuclear or radioactive pollution?

Answer: It is contamination of the environment by radioactive substances that emit ionising radiation.

Q15. How can solid waste be managed, and what is the pharmacist's role?

Answer: Follow the 5Rs—refuse, reduce, reuse, repair, and recycle where appropriate; segregate waste; use authorised handlers; conserve water and energy; and educate patients about safe medicine disposal.

Unit II — Pharmaceutical Waste and Effluent Management

Q16. What is pharmaceutical waste?

Answer: It is waste generated during the manufacture, testing, storage, distribution, or use of medicines and pharmaceutical products.

Q17. Name four types of pharmaceutical waste.

Answer: Chemical waste, expired or unused medicines, packaging waste, and biomedical or infectious waste.

Q18. Why should expired medicines not be thrown into ordinary bins or drains?

Answer: They may poison people or wildlife, contaminate water, and contribute to antimicrobial resistance.

Q19. What is biomedical waste?

Answer: Waste generated during diagnosis, treatment, immunisation, or related research that may pose infection or injury risks.

Q20. What is the purpose of the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016?

Answer: They establish requirements for segregation, collection, storage, transport, treatment, and disposal of biomedical waste to protect health and the environment.

Q21. What is waste segregation?

Answer: Segregation means separating waste by type at the point where it is generated so each type receives the correct treatment.

Q22. Why is colour-coded segregation important?

Answer: It prevents mixing of incompatible wastes, reduces infection and injury risks, and supports the correct treatment route under applicable rules.

Q23. What is an Effluent Treatment Plant (ETP)?

Answer: An ETP is a system that treats wastewater from industrial or pharmaceutical processes before reuse or discharge.

Q24. List the main stages of an ETP.

Answer: Typical stages include screening and equalisation, pH adjustment, primary treatment, biological treatment, tertiary treatment, and sludge handling; the exact design depends on the effluent.

Q25. What happens during primary treatment?

Answer: Physical methods such as screening, settling, and oil separation remove larger solids and suspended matter.

Q26. What is secondary or biological treatment?

Answer: Microorganisms break down biodegradable organic matter in wastewater under controlled conditions.

Q27. What is tertiary treatment?

Answer: Advanced treatment removes remaining pollutants using methods such as filtration, adsorption, membranes, or disinfection.

Q28. What is reverse osmosis (RO)?

Answer: RO uses pressure to force water through a semi-permeable membrane, rejecting many dissolved salts and contaminants.

Q29. Differentiate UV treatment and distillation.

Answer: UV treatment inactivates many microorganisms using ultraviolet light but does not remove dissolved salts; distillation evaporates and condenses water, leaving many non-volatile impurities behind.

Q30. What is filtration, and why is monitoring necessary?

Answer: Filtration separates particles using a filter medium. Regular testing confirms that treated effluent meets the required discharge or reuse standards.

Unit III — Environmental Impact of the Pharmaceutical Industry

Q31. Name major sources of pollution in pharmaceutical manufacturing.

Answer: Chemical synthesis, washing and cleaning, fermentation, solvent use, utilities, packaging, laboratories, and rejected products.

Q32. What are the three physical forms of pharmaceutical waste?

Answer: Solid waste, liquid waste, and gaseous emissions.

Q33. Give examples of solid pharmaceutical waste.

Answer: Contaminated filters, rejected tablets, used containers, packaging, laboratory residues, and treatment sludge.

Q34. Give examples of liquid pharmaceutical waste.

Answer: Process wastewater, solvent-containing liquids, cleaning water, laboratory liquids, and liquid residues containing active ingredients.

Q35. Give examples of gaseous emissions from pharmaceutical facilities.

Answer: Solvent vapours, boiler emissions, dust, and gases from certain chemical processes.

Q36. What are active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs)?

Answer: APIs are the substances in medicines responsible for the intended pharmacological action.

Q37. How can APIs enter the environment?

Answer: Through manufacturing effluent, improper disposal of medicines, excretion after use, and wastewater that is not adequately treated.

Q38. Why can pharmaceutical residues harm aquatic ecosystems?

Answer: Some residues can affect growth, reproduction, behaviour, or survival of aquatic organisms even at low concentrations.

Q39. How can antibiotic residues contribute to antimicrobial resistance?

Answer: They may create selection pressure that favours resistant microorganisms, especially when exposure occurs with other risk factors.

Q40. What are the possible human-health risks of pharmaceutical pollution?

Answer: Risks may include exposure to toxic chemicals, altered microbial resistance patterns, and contamination of drinking-water sources; the risk depends on the substance and exposure level.

Q41. What is chemical oxygen demand (COD)?

Answer: COD estimates the oxygen equivalent needed to chemically oxidise substances in water; a high value often indicates substantial pollution.

Q42. What is biochemical oxygen demand (BOD)?

Answer: BOD measures the oxygen used by microorganisms to break down biodegradable organic matter in a water sample over a specified period.

Q43. Why must organic solvents be handled carefully?

Answer: Many solvents are volatile, flammable, toxic, or harmful to aquatic life; they should be contained, recovered where possible, and managed as required.

Q44. How can pharmaceutical industries reduce pollution at source?

Answer: Optimise reactions, use less hazardous materials, recover solvents, prevent leaks, reduce water use, and segregate waste streams.

Q45. Why should pharmaceutical effluent not be discharged untreated?

Answer: It may contain APIs, solvents, salts, high organic loads, or toxic chemicals that can harm ecosystems and violate discharge requirements.

Unit IV — Sustainability in the Pharmaceutical Sector

Q46. Define sustainable development.

Answer: Sustainable development meets present needs without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Q47. What is sustainability in the pharmaceutical sector?

Answer: It means producing and supplying medicines while reducing environmental harm, conserving resources, protecting workers and communities, and maintaining medicine quality and access.

Q48. What is green pharmacy?

Answer: Green pharmacy applies environmental principles to medicine design, manufacture, packaging, use, and disposal to reduce harm across the medicine life cycle.

Q49. State four principles of green pharmacy.

Answer: Prevent waste, use safer chemicals and solvents, improve energy and water efficiency, and design processes that reduce hazards and pollution.

Q50. What is green chemistry?

Answer: Green chemistry designs chemical products and processes to reduce or eliminate the use and generation of hazardous substances.

Q51. How can pharmaceutical companies reduce solvent waste?

Answer: Choose safer solvents where feasible, optimise quantities, recover and reuse solvents, and use validated closed systems.

Q52. What is life-cycle thinking?

Answer: It considers environmental impacts from raw-material extraction and manufacturing through packaging, transport, use, and end-of-life disposal.

Q53. How can packaging become more sustainable?

Answer: Use right-sized packaging, reduce unnecessary material, choose recyclable materials where suitable, and protect product stability and patient safety.

Q54. Why is energy conservation important in pharmaceutical plants?

Answer: It lowers operating costs and greenhouse-gas emissions while supporting efficient production.

Q55. Give examples of energy-saving measures.

Answer: Efficient motors and lighting, heat recovery, insulation, preventive maintenance, optimised HVAC systems, and renewable energy where feasible.

Q56. How can water use be reduced?

Answer: Repair leaks, optimise cleaning, reuse suitable treated water, use efficient equipment, and monitor water consumption.

Q57. What is resource optimisation?

Answer: It means using materials, water, energy, and equipment efficiently while maintaining product quality and safety.

Q58. What are the 3Rs of waste management?

Answer: Reduce, reuse, and recycle, where safe and legally permitted. Hazardous or medicine-contaminated waste must follow its required disposal route.

Q59. How can pharmacists promote responsible medicine disposal?

Answer: Give patients clear disposal advice, support authorised take-back schemes where available, and discourage disposal into sinks or toilets unless official guidance directs it.

Q60. Why must sustainability not compromise medicine quality?

Answer: Medicines must remain safe, effective, stable, and compliant with quality standards; environmental improvements must be validated and controlled.

Unit V — Government Policies, Compliance, and Practical Exposure

Q61. What is the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986?

Answer: It is an Indian law that provides for protection and improvement of the environment and empowers the Central Government to take measures against environmental pollution.

Q62. What is the purpose of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974?

Answer: It aims to prevent and control water pollution and maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water.

Q63. What is the role of the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)?

Answer: CPCB supports national pollution-control programmes, sets or recommends standards and guidelines, coordinates with state boards, and monitors environmental quality under applicable laws.

Q64. What is the role of a State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)?

Answer: An SPCB implements relevant pollution-control laws in its state, monitors facilities, grants applicable consents or authorisations, and takes enforcement action.

Q65. What is the National Green Tribunal (NGT)?

Answer: The NGT is a specialised Indian tribunal that handles environmental protection and conservation cases within its legal jurisdiction.

Q66. What is environmental compliance in a pharmaceutical facility?

Answer: It means meeting applicable laws, permits, emission and discharge limits, waste-management rules, monitoring duties, and record-keeping requirements.

Q67. What is the Swachh Bharat Mission?

Answer: It is a Government of India initiative focused on cleanliness, sanitation, and improved waste management.

Q68. What is the Namami Gange Programme?

Answer: It is a river-conservation programme focused on reducing pollution and rejuvenating the River Ganga through measures including sewage treatment and related interventions.

Q69. What is the Jal Jeevan Mission?

Answer: It is a Government of India programme aimed at providing safe and adequate drinking water through functional household tap connections in rural areas.

Q70. What is ISO 14000?

Answer: ISO 14000 is a family of international standards related to environmental management; it includes ISO 14001 requirements for an environmental management system.

Q71. What is an Environmental Management System (EMS)?

Answer: An EMS is an organised approach for identifying environmental impacts, setting objectives, meeting obligations, monitoring performance, and improving environmental results.

Q72. What is an environmental audit?

Answer: It is a systematic review of environmental practices, records, risks, and compliance to identify gaps and opportunities for improvement.

Q73. What records may a pharmaceutical facility maintain for waste and effluent?

Answer: Depending on applicable rules, records may include waste quantities, segregation and handover details, effluent test results, treatment logs, authorisations, and incident reports.

Q74. What should be checked during a visit to an ETP?

Answer: Observe flow stages, safety measures, pH monitoring, treatment equipment, sludge handling, sampling points, and records—without touching chemicals or entering restricted areas.

Q75. What practical precautions should a pharmacy student follow during an environmental visit?

Answer: Follow staff instructions, wear required PPE, avoid direct contact with waste or effluent, never collect samples without permission, and report unsafe conditions.
