Unit I — Unit Operations Associated with Solids

Q1. What is pharmaceutical engineering?

Answer: It applies engineering principles to equipment and processes used to manufacture medicines.

Q2. What is a unit operation?

Answer: A basic physical step in manufacturing, such as milling, mixing, filtration, drying or distillation.

Q3. What is size reduction?

Answer: The mechanical reduction of large solid particles into smaller particles.

Q4. Why is size reduction important in pharmacy?

Answer: It can improve dissolution, extraction, mixing, content uniformity and powder texture.

Q5. Name the main size-reduction mechanisms.

Answer: Cutting, compression, impact, attrition and combinations of these forces.

Q6. State Kick's law.

Answer: Energy is proportional to the logarithm of the ratio of initial to final particle size; it is most useful for coarse crushing.

Q7. State Rittinger's law.

Answer: Energy required is proportional to the new surface area produced, so it is often applied to fine grinding.

Q8. What is Bond's law?

Answer: It is an intermediate energy-size relationship between Kick's and Rittinger's laws, used to estimate grinding energy.

Q9. What factors affect size reduction?

Answer: Hardness, toughness, moisture, stickiness, feed size, target size, mill speed and heat sensitivity.

Q10. Describe a hammer mill.

Answer: Rotating hammers break material by impact; particles small enough pass through a screen.

Q11. Give one advantage and one limitation of a hammer mill.

Answer: It is simple and rapid, but may generate heat, dust and fines.

Q12. Describe a ball mill.

Answer: A rotating cylinder contains balls that grind material through impact and attrition.

Q13. What is critical speed in a ball mill?

Answer: The speed at which balls remain against the wall by centrifugal force instead of falling to grind material.

Q14. What is size separation?

Answer: Classification of powder into particle-size fractions, commonly using sieves or air classifiers.

Q15. What is mixing, and name common solid mixers.

Answer: Mixing distributes components uniformly. Examples include double-cone, V-blender, ribbon and planetary mixers.

Unit II — Unit Operations Associated with Liquids

Q16. What is filtration?

Answer: Separation of suspended solids from a fluid by passing the mixture through a porous medium.

Q17. State the principle of filtration.

Answer: A pressure difference drives fluid through a medium that retains solids on or within it.

Q18. What is a filter medium?

Answer: A porous material such as filter paper, cloth, membrane or sintered material that allows fluid to pass while retaining particles.

Q19. What is a filter aid?

Answer: An inert porous material, such as suitable diatomaceous earth, used to improve flow or form a permeable precoat.

Q20. What is filter cake?

Answer: The layer of retained solids that builds up on the filter medium.

Q21. List factors affecting filtration rate.

Answer: Pressure difference, filter area, viscosity, cake thickness, particle properties and filter resistance.

Q22. Describe a plate-and-frame filter press.

Answer: Alternating plates and frames lined with filter cloth receive slurry; solids form cakes while filtrate flows through channels.

Q23. What is membrane filtration used for?

Answer: It separates particles or microorganisms by membrane retention; validated membrane processes can be used for sterile filtration.

Q24. What is crystallization?

Answer: Formation of solid crystals from a solution, melt or vapour, usually by creating supersaturation.

Q25. What is supersaturation?

Answer: A condition where a solution contains more dissolved solute than its equilibrium solubility at the same conditions.

Q26. Name the main stages of crystallization.

Answer: Supersaturation generation, nucleation, crystal growth, then crystal separation and drying.

Q27. What factors affect crystal quality?

Answer: Supersaturation, temperature, cooling rate, agitation, solvent, impurities and seed crystals.

Q28. What is centrifugation?

Answer: Separation using centrifugal acceleration, often based on differences in density.

Q29. Describe a basket centrifuge.

Answer: A rotating perforated basket lined with filter cloth lets liquid pass outward while retaining solid cake.

Q30. Differentiate filtration and centrifugation.

Answer: Filtration uses a porous medium and pressure difference; centrifugation uses rotational acceleration to separate components.

Unit III — Unit Operations Associated with Heat Transfer

Q31. Name the three modes of heat transfer.

Answer: Conduction, convection and radiation.

Q32. What is conduction?

Answer: Transfer of heat through a material or contacting materials without bulk movement.

Q33. What is convection?

Answer: Transfer of heat between a surface and a moving fluid; it may be natural or forced.

Q34. What is radiation?

Answer: Transfer of energy by electromagnetic waves, without requiring direct contact or a material medium.

Q35. What is a heat exchanger?

Answer: Equipment that transfers heat between fluids, usually separated by a wall.

Q36. Write a common heat-transfer equation.

Answer: Q = U A ΔTₘ, where Q is heat-transfer rate, U is overall coefficient, A is area and ΔTₘ is mean temperature difference.

Q37. What is evaporation?

Answer: Removal of part of a solvent as vapour to concentrate a solution.

Q38. Differentiate evaporation and drying.

Answer: Evaporation mainly concentrates a liquid solution; drying removes moisture or volatile liquid from a solid or other material.

Q39. Describe a steam-jacketed kettle.

Answer: Steam flows through a jacket around a vessel and transfers heat through the wall to the product, often with agitation.

Q40. What is a multiple-effect evaporator?

Answer: Vapour from one effect heats another effect at lower pressure, improving steam economy in suitable conditions.

Q41. What is drying?

Answer: Removal of moisture or another volatile liquid by vaporisation and transfer of vapour away from the material.

Q42. What is a drying-rate curve?

Answer: A plot showing how the rate of moisture removal changes with time or moisture content.

Q43. Describe a tray dryer.

Answer: Wet material is spread on trays in a chamber and heated air circulates to remove moisture.

Q44. What is a fluidised-bed dryer?

Answer: Upward-flowing air suspends and mixes particles, giving good contact between hot air and the material.

Q45. Differentiate simple and fractional distillation.

Answer: Simple distillation suits a volatile liquid separated from nonvolatile material or components with sufficiently different volatility; fractional distillation uses a column for closer-boiling components.

Unit IV — Materials and Material Handling

Q46. What is material handling?

Answer: Movement, storage, control and protection of materials within a facility.

Q47. Why is material handling important in pharmaceutical plants?

Answer: It reduces manual effort, damage, spills, mix-ups, contamination and delays.

Q48. What factors guide equipment selection?

Answer: Material form, route, capacity, flow properties, cleaning, containment, safety, cost and contamination risk.

Q49. What is a conveyor?

Answer: Equipment that moves materials along a route; types include belt, roller, screw, chain and pneumatic conveyors.

Q50. What is a screw conveyor?

Answer: A rotating helical screw inside a trough or tube moves suitable bulk solids along the route.

Q51. What is pneumatic conveying?

Answer: Transport of powders or granules through pipes using moving air or gas; dust, static and degradation must be controlled.

Q52. What is a hoist?

Answer: A lifting device used to raise and lower loads; it must be rated, inspected and operated safely.

Q53. What is an automated guided vehicle (AGV)?

Answer: A driverless vehicle that follows a guidance system to move materials around a facility.

Q54. What is the difference between AGVs and autonomous mobile robots?

Answer: AGVs commonly follow predefined guidance, while autonomous mobile robots use sensing and navigation to adapt routes; capabilities vary by model.

Q55. What are bins and silos?

Answer: Storage structures for bulk solids; their design must consider capacity, flow, cleaning, dust and structural loads.

Q56. Differentiate FIFO and FEFO.

Answer: FIFO means first in, first out. FEFO means first expiry, first out, prioritising material with the earliest expiry date.

Q57. List safety measures for material handling.

Answer: Use trained operators, safe lifting practices, guards, load ratings, PPE, clear routes, spill procedures and regular inspections.

Q58. What is corrosion?

Answer: Deterioration of a material, usually a metal, due to chemical or electrochemical interaction with its environment.

Q59. Name common types of corrosion and one prevention method.

Answer: Types include uniform, galvanic, pitting, crevice and stress-corrosion cracking. Prevention includes compatible materials, suitable finishes, environmental control and inspection.

Q60. How do waste management, automation and PAT support pharmaceutical production?

Answer: Waste management segregates and disposes of waste safely. Automation improves repeatability; process analytical technology (PAT) uses timely measurements to support process understanding and quality decisions.

Unit V — Flow of Fluids

Q61. What is fluid flow?

Answer: Movement of a liquid or gas due to forces such as pressure difference, gravity or pumping.

Q62. Differentiate laminar and turbulent flow.

Answer: Laminar flow is orderly with limited cross-stream mixing; turbulent flow has irregular fluctuations and stronger mixing.

Q63. What is viscosity?

Answer: A measure of a fluid's resistance to flow or deformation.

Q64. What is a manometer?

Answer: An instrument that measures pressure or pressure difference using the height of a liquid column.

Q65. Name common manometers.

Answer: Piezometer, U-tube manometer, differential manometer and inclined manometer.

Q66. Define Reynolds number.

Answer: A dimensionless number comparing inertial forces with viscous forces in fluid flow.

Q67. Write Reynolds number for pipe flow.

Answer: Re = ρvD/μ, where ρ is density, v is mean velocity, D is pipe diameter and μ is dynamic viscosity.

Q68. What is the significance of Reynolds number?

Answer: For ordinary pipe flow, Re below about 2,000 is usually laminar, above about 4,000 usually turbulent, and between these values transitional; actual conditions matter.

Q69. State Bernoulli's theorem.

Answer: For ideal steady incompressible flow along a streamline, pressure, kinetic and potential energy per unit volume sum to a constant when losses and shaft work are neglected.

Q70. Write Bernoulli's equation in head form.

Answer: p/(ρg) + v²/(2g) + z = constant, where p is pressure, ρ density, v velocity, g gravitational acceleration and z elevation.

Q71. What is an orifice meter?

Answer: A flow meter that relates flow rate to the pressure difference created by a plate with an opening in a pipe.

Q72. What is a Venturimeter?

Answer: A flow meter with a converging section, throat and diverging section; pressure difference is related to fluid velocity and flow rate.

Q73. Differentiate an orifice meter and Venturimeter.

Answer: An orifice meter is compact and inexpensive but usually causes greater permanent pressure loss; a Venturimeter is larger and costlier but generally loses less pressure.

Q74. What is a Pitot tube?

Answer: An instrument that estimates local fluid velocity by comparing stagnation pressure with static pressure.

Q75. What is a rotameter?

Answer: A variable-area flow meter with a float in a tapered vertical tube; float position indicates flow rate after calibration.
